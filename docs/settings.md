# Settings

What each setting of LibreELEC Hades changes on the screen or in the sound, when you would touch it, its default and its level. The first half of this page is for choosing: it starts with the few settings that people do change. The second half is the reference, with every setting in a table. The parts behind the settings are explained in [how it works](how-it-works.md), and the [extras](features.md) say what each one is for.

**Most people change nothing.** Dolby Vision is on by default, and Hades falls back to HDR10 or SDR by itself when something does not qualify. Try a few films first. If the picture is right, leave the settings alone.

## If something goes wrong

| What you see | What to try |
| --- | --- |
| A video that should be Dolby Vision plays as HDR10 | Press O (or the red key of a remote over HDMI-CEC). The first page says why in one sentence. The usual reasons are a TV input in game mode, a converter or receiver that does not carry Dolby Vision, or a video that is not Dolby Vision. |
| A black or broken picture after turning on *Dolby Vision for the menus* | Wait 15 seconds without pressing anything: the setting goes back to Off by itself. |
| Wrong colours after turning on *Dolby Vision for older TVs (HDMI 1.4b)* | Turn it off. Hades cannot tell whether the TV accepts that signalling. |
| A black or broken picture on a 3D film | Turn off *Switch the display to 3D (HDMI)*, in the Display settings. |
| On Intel or AMD graphics, an HDR video at 3840x2160 and 50 or 60 Hz shows a broken picture, or the TV changing mode twice as it starts bothers you | Turn off *HDR in 4:2:2 at 12 bits over HDMI 2.0*, in the Display settings. |
| HDR video shows wrong colours or a black screen on NVIDIA graphics | Turn off *10-bit video at full depth (NVIDIA)*. |
| Dolby Vision video stutters or shows a broken picture | Set *Present frames a frame ahead* to Never. |
| Sound of TrueHD or DTS-HD cuts out every few seconds on a laptop with NVIDIA graphics that is on its charger | Turn on *Keep the graphics card at full speed while HD audio plays through* (Audio settings). |
| A Dolby Vision video on a projector has washed-out blacks or wrong colours | Turn on *Dolby Vision: ignore the studio's fine-tuning (trims)*, then see the section for projectors below. |
| The TV shows no picture and you cannot see the menus | Read [Diagnostics and logs](diagnostics.md#when-a-display-shows-nothing). |
| You are not sure what the TV gets, or a sound format plays silence | Run *Check this setup* (*Settings > LibreELEC > System*): it says what went out, and switches on only the sound formats that you heard. |
| Picture subtitles look grey, or glaringly bright, in an HDR film | Set *Bitmap subtitles made for* (*Settings > Player > Subtitles*) to SDR or HDR for that film's subtitles. |
| Something does not work and you want to report it | *Settings > LibreELEC > System > Submit Log > Create a diagnostic report* makes one file to attach ([diagnostics.md](diagnostics.md#a-report-at-once)). |

## For projectors and displays that map HDR poorly

A projector is far dimmer than a TV, and many map HDR badly. Hades can do the mapping in the PC instead, to the peak brightness and the colours that you give it. All of these are at Expert level, and they are off by default. Do them in this order, and stop when the picture is right.

1. **Find the peak brightness of the projector,** in nits (cd/m²): measured, or from its specification (about 50 to 300 for a projector).
2. **If it takes Dolby Vision:** it maps Dolby Vision with its own processing. If the blacks are washed out or the colours are wrong, turn on *Dolby Vision: ignore the studio's fine-tuning (trims)*. If it offers Dolby Vision only as Low-Latency, turn on *Dolby Vision for TVs in game mode (Low-Latency)* and *Low-Latency: peak and colours set here*, then give the peak, the colours and the black.
3. **If it takes HDR10 only:** turn on *Adapt Dolby Vision to my display (projectors)* and give *Peak brightness of the display* and *Colour gamut of the display*. Turn off the dynamic tone mapping of the projector for HDR10. Set *HDR10 and HDR10+ adapted to the display* to Automatic, so that only the videos brighter than the projector are mapped.
4. **If the projector adapts the picture to the numbers in the video,** *HDR10: limit the luminance told to the display* tells it a lower peak, so that it has less to squeeze.
5. **If its blacks are lifted,** *Black of the display* (with *Adapt Dolby Vision to my display (projectors)* on) takes the black you measured, so that the darkest parts of the film do not sink into it.
6. **If it shows no HDR at all, or you prefer its SDR picture,** *HDR to SDR: the display it is made for* chooses the SDR for a projector (white at 100 nits), or Custom with the white and gamma you measured; *Play HDR videos as SDR* sends HDR videos that way to a display that takes HDR too.

## How the settings are laid out

Kodi hides the settings that few people need. The level is set at the bottom of the left-hand list of a settings screen: Standard, Advanced and Expert. LibreELEC starts at Advanced. At Advanced you find only what is meant for everyone: the Dolby Vision output, Dolby Vision for the menus and the TVs that ask for the older signalling or the game mode, HDR10+ and HDR10 as Dolby Vision, HLG as Dolby Vision, Dolby Vision as HDR10 on a display without it, the SDR target, the brightness of the menus and the brightness and colour of the picture subtitles, the notice of the playback mode, HDR in 4:2:2 at 12 bits over HDMI 2.0, the picture in step with its sound, films rebuilt from interlaced video, the choice of editions of a file, the levelling and the centre level of Dolby Digital sound, and holding an NVIDIA card at full speed for HD audio. Everything else is at Expert. A setting keeps its value, which is its default unless you changed it, whichever level is shown.

In *System > Display* the settings are in four groups: HDR (the brightness of the menus and the notice of the playback mode), Dolby Vision, HDR on displays without Dolby Vision and projectors, and HDR colour depth and format. A group with nothing at your level does not show its heading. The settings about what is sent to a display that takes Dolby Vision are shown only where the display declares Dolby Vision. Some are shown only where they can apply, as their row says. Settings in *System > Display* apply from the next video, unless their row says otherwise.

## Kodi's own settings that matter here

- **Adjust display refresh rate** (*Player > Videos*). Dolby Vision follows it, and never changes the refresh rate it gives. With it off, the video stays at the refresh rate of the menus, and native Dolby Vision is used only where that rate can carry it ([how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#when-native-dolby-vision-is-used)).
- **Monitor** (*System > Display*). LibreELEC hides this setting, whose change took effect there only when Kodi started again; this build shows it again. "Default" chooses the display as Kodi does when it starts: an HDMI display that takes Dolby Vision first, then any HDMI display, then any other, and the computer's own screen last. Each display is shown by the name its EDID gives it and its connector, and by its graphics where the displays are on more than one ("LG TV SSCR2 (HDMI-A-2)"); a display without a name is shown by its connector, and a choice saved before keeps working. The setting is greyed where there is one display, unless it names one that is not connected.
  - A display on the graphics in use is used at once, if the driver takes it in a test commit (if not, nothing changes), and Kodi then asks whether to keep it: without a yes in 10 s, it goes back.
  - A display on other graphics needs Kodi to start again, which it offers to do now ("No" keeps the change for the next start). That start asks "Keep this monitor?" with 15 s to answer. What is not answered yes (no, back, the time running out, or a question that cannot be shown within 90 s) brings the display of before back with one more start, which asks too; where that one is not kept either, the setting goes back to "Default".
  - While a video or music plays, the setting keeps what it had and says to stop playback; while the output still finishes a change of its own (the end of HDR or of Dolby Vision after a video), it says to wait a moment. A change made while Kodi's display sleeps is used from the next start, and one made by other means (a remote control of Kodi, `guisettings.xml`) asks nothing.
- **Resolution** (*System > Display*). When the display's modes change while Kodi runs (Deep Colour turned on at the TV, for example), Kodi takes the new list a few seconds later, while nothing plays, and makes the choices a start would make: "Desktop" goes to the display's preferred mode, a resolution you chose that is still offered stays, and one that is not goes to the nearest. No restart is needed.
- **Use limited colour range (16-235)** (*System > Display*). It now means the same with every graphics driver: the TV gets limited-range RGB, compressed once. On Intel, AMD and NVIDIA graphics the driver compresses it: Kodi sets the connector's "Broadcast RGB" to "Limited 16:235" and draws full range, so the range of everything on the output is compressed once, and the AVI InfoFrame says limited range where the TV can be told. Before 1.0 RC6, Kodi compressed its own picture, and Intel's driver compressed it again at every TV mode (raised blacks), as did AMD's for a TV that cannot select the RGB range, and both in YCbCr (HDR at 4K above 30 Hz). Turning it on or off makes the TV set the picture up again (a moment of black): at once in the menus, or with the next change of mode while a video plays. With it off the driver chooses: Intel sends limited range at every TV mode, AMD to a TV that cannot select the RGB range (no QS), and full range to one that can (the LG C5 can); NVIDIA sends full range at every mode and tells a TV that can select the range so (with such a TV leave it off; turn it on with NVIDIA for a TV that cannot select the range and shows crushed shadows). Native Dolby Vision is not affected: its tunnel goes out in full range whatever the setting says. `kodi.log` says for each mode what Kodi asks for, the property and its value, and who compresses (`RGB range: ...`), and on NVIDIA the kernel's log says each mode set in limited range (`Hades: head N (HDMI-A-1): RGB in limited range (16-235) asked of NVKMS, as Broadcast RGB asks`) and when the head's range compression is on or off (`the range compression of the head is now on`). On NVIDIA a change of the range at the same mode turns the head off and on again (a moment of black), also at Kodi's start with the setting on. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#other-parts).
- **Number of channels** (*System > Audio*). PCM goes out in no more channels than the display declares, whatever this says, while "Limit PCM channels to what the display declares" ([above](#system--audio)) is on. The setting itself is not changed, and `kodi.log` says when the channels are limited and what a multichannel track goes out as.
- **Audio output device** (*System > Audio*). Until an output is chosen, Kodi's "Default" takes the first one it finds, on a PC often the analogue output of the board. Hades uses instead the output whose display is the one Kodi shows its picture on (it knows it by the maker, product and name that the display's EDID and its audio data both give), for PCM and for passthrough, also when that display is turned on later; where that cannot be told for sure (no output with a display, two alike, a display without audio), nothing is changed. An output that you choose is kept and never changed; choosing "Default" again gives the choice back to Hades. `kodi.log` says which output is used and why ([diagnostics.md](diagnostics.md#what-to-look-for-in-kodilog)). Where the output that is set is not there in this start (the HDMI output of graphics that are not in use, for example), the output that Kodi would take in its place is used, with the formats asked for passthrough. The setting is not changed: when that output is there again, it is used again. `kodi.log` says what is used in its place.
- **Sync playback to display** (*Player > Videos*). As in Kodi, with it on the sound is always decoded to PCM, never passed through: the speed of the sound is then adjusted to the display's clock, which cannot be done with sound that is passed through, so Dolby Atmos and DTS:X do not reach the receiver or sound bar. The audio output row of the player information's overview says "PCM, decoded (Sync playback to display: Enabled)", and `kodi.log` says it for each audio stream (`this audio is decoded to PCM, not passed through`). Leave it off for passthrough; Hades changes the display's refresh rate to that of the video ("Adjust display refresh rate"), which is what keeps the picture smooth.
- **Include LFE when downmixing** (*System > Audio*). Kodi offers Off, 50 % and 100 %; this build adds 150 %, 200 % and 300 % (swresample's LFE level: in a stereo downmix the LFE goes into the front left and right, each at that level times 0.71, or into the centre where the output has one). Off is still the default. With "Maintain original volume on downmix" on, the whole mix is brought down so that no channel overflows, so more LFE also makes the rest quieter.
- **Passthrough and its formats, the DTS-HD core, Stereo upmix and Include LFE when downmixing** (*System > Audio*). A change while a video plays applies at once: in Kodi the passthrough ones waited for the next video, and the other two did not apply while the output stayed the same. A switch between passthrough and PCM stops the sound for a moment while the output opens again; `kodi.log` says `an audio setting changed during playback`. The passthrough device, and "Allow passthrough on live TV and streams", still apply from the next video for how the stream is played.
- **Video scaling method** (in the player's video settings). Not used for Dolby Vision, native or shown as HDR10: the Dolby Vision engine resamples the enhancement layer as the specification says and scales the picture with libplacebo's filters. It applies to HDR10, HLG, SDR, and Dolby Vision shown by its base layer. The Dolby Vision page of the player information says so. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#other-parts).
- **Allow hardware acceleration - VAAPI** (*Player > Videos*), and its setting for each codec. Native Dolby Vision output and Dolby Vision rebuilt as HDR10 need the video decoded with VAAPI: with it off, Dolby Vision videos play as Kodi plays them, and the overview page of the player information says why ([how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#from-the-file-to-the-hdmi-cable)).
- **Allow using DRM PRIME decoder** (*Player > Videos*). No video decoder of this build decodes to DRM PRIME in hardware: with it on, videos are decoded by the processor, except those of more than 8 bits (HDR), which are decoded as with it off. Dolby Vision videos that are sent natively or rebuilt as HDR10 are decoded with VAAPI either way.
