# Settings

The settings that LibreELEC Hades adds to Kodi and to the LibreELEC settings add-on: where each one is, what it does, its default, and when to change it. How the parts behind them work is in [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md).

In Kodi's settings screens, these settings appear at the settings level Advanced or Expert (the level is changed at the bottom of the list on the left of a settings screen); those marked Expert need that level. Some are shown only where they can apply, as their row says. The name in brackets is the key under which Kodi keeps the setting in `guisettings.xml`. Of these settings, those in *System > Display* apply from the next video, unless their row says otherwise.

## System > Display > HDR

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GUI and subtitle brightness in HDR (`videoscreen.hadesguiwhite`) | 100 % | Brightness of the menus, the OSD and text subtitles while the TV is in HDR10, HLG or Dolby Vision mode (and of the menus in Dolby Vision), from 25 to 300 % of the reference white of ITU-R BT.2408 (100 % is 203 cd/m², as in Kodi). Change it if they look too dim or too bright beside the video. Image subtitles made in HDR (UHD Blu-ray) keep their own levels, and the video is never changed. Applies at once. Shown only with an HDR display. |
| 10-bit video at full depth (NVIDIA) (`videoscreen.hadesfloatoutput`) | On | NVIDIA graphics offer no 10-bit output, only an 8-bit and a 16-bit floating-point one. With this on, HDR10, HLG and other 10-bit video go out through the 16-bit one, without the banding of 8 bits. Turn it off if such video shows wrong colours or a black screen. Dolby Vision is not affected. Expert; shown only with NVIDIA graphics. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#other-parts). |
| HDR where RGB would be 8-bit (`videoscreen.hadeshdr420`) | YCbCr, 10 bits or more | At 3840x2160 above 30 Hz, an HDMI 2.0 link (or an HDMI 2.1 link driven without FRL) carries RGB at 8 bits only, and HDR10 and HLG would lose two bits. With the default they go out in YCbCr at 10 bits or more instead: in 4:2:2 at 12 bits on AMD graphics where both the graphics and the TV take it, otherwise in 4:2:0 where the TV takes that mode in 4:2:0 with deep colour. The video is 4:2:0 already: only the menus and subtitles over it have less colour resolution. "8-bit RGB" sends RGB at 8 bits, as in LibreELEC. Dolby Vision is not affected. Expert; shown only with Intel and AMD graphics. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#other-parts). |
| HDR output depth (NVIDIA) (`videoscreen.hadeshdrdepth`) | Automatic | How many bits per colour the graphics card sends for HDR10 and HLG. "Automatic": as many as the TV and the mode take, 10 or 12. "8 bits": never more than 8, for a TV with an 8-bit panel that shows less banding with 8 bits at its input than with 10 or 12; the picture is drawn as before, and the graphics card brings it down to 8 bits as it does where the link takes no more. SDR and native Dolby Vision are not affected; Dolby Vision shown as HDR10 is, since it is HDR10. Applies from the next video. Expert; shown only where the driver says the depth it sends (nvidia-drm). See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#other-parts). |
| Native Dolby Vision output (`videoscreen.hadesdvnative`) | On | Sends Dolby Vision as such to a display that takes it (Dolby Vision Standard over HDMI). Off: Dolby Vision videos play as on a display without it, with their HDR10 picture, or rebuilt as HDR10 where "Dolby Vision as HDR10 when needed" says so (profile 5 always), and the Dolby Vision page of the player information says that it is turned off. The next four settings are greyed out while it is off. For a single video, the player's video settings have the same choice ([below](#in-the-player-video-settings)). See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#when-native-dolby-vision-is-used). |
| Dolby Vision Low-Latency (player-led) (`videoscreen.hadesdvlowlatency`) | Off | For a TV input that offers Dolby Vision only as Low-Latency (its game mode, a device in between that offers only that, or a TV with no other form): sends Dolby Vision that way, with the picture mapped to the display by Hades, not by Dolby's display management. A display that takes Dolby Vision Standard keeps it, whatever this says. Off: such an input gets HDR10, and a short notice says what to do. Turn it on where the input cannot offer Dolby Vision Standard. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#dolby-vision-low-latency). |
| Dolby Vision with the older signalling (HDMI 1.4b) (`videoscreen.hadesdvhdmi14b`) | Off | Shown only for a display that asks for it: Dolby Vision TVs of 2016 (the LG OLEDs of that year among them) and some later ones, whose Dolby Vision block asks for the older HDMI 1.4b signal. On: native Dolby Vision goes to such a display with that signal, in place of HDR10. Whether the TV takes it cannot be checked from the player: if the picture comes out with wrong colours, turn it off. Off: such a display gets HDR10, as before. Applies from the next video. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#dolby-vision-with-the-hdmi-14b-signalling). |
| Dolby Vision for the menus (`videoscreen.hadesdvmenus`) | Off | Keeps the TV in Dolby Vision while the menus are shown: a Dolby Vision video then starts without the TV changing its picture mode, and the menus come back the same way. Any other video makes the TV change its mode once more at its start and once more at its end. The menus go out with fixed metadata at the white of "GUI and subtitle brightness in HDR", and the TV shows them in its Dolby Vision picture mode, which may look different from its SDR one; while they change, the graphics chip converts each of their frames. Only with Dolby Vision Standard (not Low-Latency, not the HDMI 1.4b signalling). If the TV shows a black or broken picture, turn it off. Applies at once. Shown only for a display that takes Dolby Vision Standard, with the menus at 3840x2160. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#dolby-vision-for-the-menus). |
| HDR10+ as Dolby Vision (`videoscreen.hadesdvfromhdr10plus`) | Off | For a display that takes Dolby Vision but not HDR10+: an HDR10+ video is sent as native Dolby Vision, with metadata made scene by scene from its own HDR10+ metadata (the peak and the average brightness of each scene). The picture is the same HDR10 picture; the studio's tone curve, target display, saturation weight and windows do not carry over. Dolby Vision videos, HDR10 videos without HDR10+, and displays that take HDR10+, are not affected. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#other-formats-sent-as-dolby-vision). |
| HDR10 as Dolby Vision, measured (`videoscreen.hadesdvfromhdr10`) | Off | For a display that takes Dolby Vision: an HDR10 video, which has no dynamic metadata of its own, is sent as native Dolby Vision with metadata that Hades measures from the picture as it plays (the peak, the average and the darkest level of each scene, and the black bars). These are not the studio's metadata, and no trims are added. Scene cuts are found from the picture: a few are missed, a few are found where there is none, and some are found a picture late. Dolby Vision and HDR10+ videos are not affected. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#other-formats-sent-as-dolby-vision). |
| Dolby Vision: leave out the trims (`videoscreen.hadesdvnotrims`) | Off | The trims are how the colourist mapped each scene to displays of a few given peaks (metadata levels 2 and 8, with the targets of level 8 in level 10). With this on they are not sent, and the display maps each scene from its brightness range alone. Turn it on only for a display that shows Dolby Vision with washed-out blacks or wrong colours because of them, as some projectors do. The picture that is sent does not change. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#other-formats-sent-as-dolby-vision). |
| Dolby Vision as HDR10 when needed (`videoscreen.hadesdvashdr10`) | On | When Dolby Vision cannot be sent natively (a display without it, or a TV input in its game mode), a Dolby Vision video whose base layer is no picture by itself (profile 5, as streaming services use it) is always rebuilt and sent as HDR10, whatever this says: played as it is, its colours would be wrong. This setting is for the other Dolby Vision videos (such as profiles 7 and 8), which play their own base picture: while it is on, the two settings below can have them rebuilt too and have what is rebuilt sent as HDR10+, and the mapping to the display (further down) rebuilds every Dolby Vision video; while it is off, they play their base picture, and a profile 5 video goes out as plain HDR10 (mapped, where the mapping is on). Where rebuilding fails, the video plays as Kodi plays it. Applies from the next video. Shown only with an HDR display; on a display without HDR, profile 5 is rebuilt and mapped to SDR whatever this says. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#dolby-vision-shown-as-hdr10). |
| Also with an enhancement layer (`videoscreen.hadesdvashdr10fel`) | Off | Under the one above: also rebuilds profile 7 videos with an enhancement layer (UHD Blu-ray) and sends them as HDR10, instead of their HDR10 picture. This sends the Dolby Vision master, which the display adapts as plain HDR10: it is not always better than the disc's HDR10 version, and it asks more of the graphics. Greyed out while "Dolby Vision as HDR10: map to the display" is on, which covers every video. |
| Send it as HDR10+ where the display takes it (`videoscreen.hadesdvashdr10plus`) | Off | Under "Dolby Vision as HDR10 when needed", and not in effect while that is off: for a display that takes HDR10+ and not Dolby Vision, as many Samsung TVs. A Dolby Vision video rebuilt as HDR10 is sent as HDR10+, with dynamic metadata that Hades measures from the rebuilt picture, scene by scene (the scenes of its Dolby Vision metadata): the average and the distribution of its brightness, and no tone curve, so the display maps each scene as it maps an HDR10+ film. Profile 8.1 is then rebuilt too; profile 7 as the setting above says. These are not the studio's metadata. The first picture of a scene waits for its own measurement where that comes before the next refresh, and otherwise goes out with the metadata of the scene before (a picture is measured after it is drawn). The display may show a scene otherwise than as plain HDR10, which is why it is off. Not available while the mapping to the display (next) is on. Applies from the next video. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#dolby-vision-sent-as-hdr10). |
| Dolby Vision as HDR10: map to the display (`videoscreen.hadesdvhdr10map`) | Off (as mastered) | For projectors and HDR10 displays that adapt HDR10 poorly. "To this display": the picture rebuilt from its Dolby Vision data is mapped here, shot by shot, to the peak and the colour gamut set below, with libplacebo's tone and gamut mapping, and the display is told that the picture now fits it. While "Dolby Vision as HDR10 when needed" is on, every Dolby Vision video that is not sent natively then goes out this way, whatever its profile, and while it is off, a profile 5 one, which is always rebuilt; a video for which "Dolby Vision as HDR10" is chosen in the player is mapped too. Turn off the display's own dynamic tone mapping for HDR10. The creative trims of Dolby Vision (levels 2 and 8) are not applied. Shown only with an HDR display. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#dolby-vision-shown-as-hdr10). |
| Peak brightness of the display (`videoscreen.hadesdvhdr10peak`) | 1000 cd/m² | Under the one above, while it is on: the brightest white the display shows in its HDR mode, as measured or as its specification gives it (about 50 to 300 for a projector, 400 to 4000 for a TV), from 40 to 4000. Nothing brighter is sent. |
| Colour gamut of the display (`videoscreen.hadesdvhdr10gamut`) | DCI-P3 (D65) | Under the same: the colours the display can show, BT.2020, DCI-P3 (D65) or BT.709. Colours of the picture outside them are brought inside gently (perceptual gamut mapping) instead of being clipped. DCI-P3 (D65) suits most HDR projectors and TVs. |
| Tone mapping curve (`videoscreen.hadesdvhdr10curve`) | BT.2390 (ITU-R) | Under the same; Expert. "BT.2390 (ITU-R)" keeps what is well below the display's peak nearly as it was mastered and compresses only the brightest part. "Spline (libplacebo)", libplacebo's own default, also darkens the middle tones and the shadows of a shot to keep its contrast, the more the brighter the shot is than the display. |

HDR10+ has no setting: its metadata go out with each frame wherever the display declares HDR10+ and the graphics driver can send them (Dolby Vision sent as HDR10+, above, is a setting: those metadata are made by Hades). Where each driver can is in [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#hdr10).

## System > Display > Dolby Vision engine

How native Dolby Vision frames are drawn and sent. Each default is the automatic choice, and the other values are there to measure or to compare: leave them at their defaults, except where a row says when to change one. While the drawing path, the composition or the transport is not at its default, or a step-down level is set, what the automatic choices measure is not kept, or not all of it.

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drawing path (`videoscreen.hadesdvpathmode`) | Automatic | Expert. A frame with nothing over it is drawn directly into the output, or composed into a full-precision picture that is then packed. "Automatic" measures each picture format on these graphics and draws it the faster way, and keeps what it finds for these graphics and this build. "Always direct" and "Always composed" are there to measure each way. Frames with subtitles, the player bar or other GUI elements, and pictures that do not fill the screen, are always composed. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#two-ways-of-drawing-a-frame). |
| Present frames a frame ahead (`videoscreen.hadesdvaheadmode`) | When needed | Where the graphics need most of the time of a frame to prepare a picture (integrated graphics with 4K Blu-ray video), the next frame is prepared while the last one waits to be shown, so that a frame that takes a little longer is still shown in time. The menus over such a video react one frame later. "When needed": only where the frames take half of a refresh or more. "Always": wherever it can be done, to try it on any graphics. "Never": choose it if native Dolby Vision video stutters or shows a broken picture with it. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#a-frame-ahead-when-needed). |
| Fidelity step-down (`videoscreen.hadesdvstepmode`) | Automatic | Where the graphics cannot prepare a picture in the time of a frame, even drawn the faster way and a frame ahead, the picture can be rebuilt a little less precisely: some of its planes are resampled with simpler filters. "Automatic": one level at a time and only as far as the video needs to play smoothly; graphics that keep up always show the exact picture, and what is found is kept for these graphics and this build. At "Automatic", where the frames of a profile 5 picture with menus over it miss refreshes, the conversion of the pixels that the menus cover also steps up, one level at a time: first to a way that gives the same result, then to a simpler rounding (at most one code apart). "Never": always the exact picture, even if the video stutters. "Level 1", "Level 2", "Level 3": that level for every frame, to measure what it saves; nothing that is measured is kept. Frames with menus or the OSD over the picture are measured apart: where they do not keep up and the others do, they step down while the menus are on screen, and only they. A level set in `advancedsettings.xml` ([below](#advancedsettingsxml)) comes first. Which frames have levels, and what each level changes: [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#a-step-down-in-fidelity-where-the-graphics-chip-cannot-keep-up). |
| Composition (`videoscreen.hadesdvcomposer`) | As the specification | Expert. How the picture is rebuilt from its layers and metadata: "As the specification" (the composer of ETSI GS CCM 001) or "libplacebo's reshaping", to compare the two on a display. A picture outside what the specification covers is rebuilt by libplacebo's reshaping either way, except one whose base layer is SDR (profile 8.2), which only the composer of the specification rebuilds (clause 5.5): with libplacebo's reshaping it plays as Kodi plays it. A profile 8.4 picture (HLG base layer) is rebuilt by either where its RPU declares a PQ picture, and by neither where it does not (it plays as Kodi plays HLG). See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#from-the-file-to-the-hdmi-cable). |
| Transport (`videoscreen.hadesdvtransport`) | As licensed players send (IPT) | Expert. "As licensed players send (IPT)": the composed picture in IPT-PQ-c2, as licensed players are reported to send every profile: profile 5 as it is, profiles 7 and 8 converted from the composer's YCbCr, with metadata that describe it; where the graphics cannot keep up, the automatic fidelity step-down sends profiles 7 and 8 as composed at its last level ([how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#what-licensed-players-send)). "As composed": the composed picture as it is, in the colour representation that the video describes (YCbCr, or IPT for profile 5), as clause 6 of ETSI GS CCM 001 allows. "Converted to BT.2020": the picture is rebuilt in RGB and converted to BT.2020 YCbCr, as earlier builds sent it, to compare the two on a display. A video that is scaled up to the screen (a 1080p one), zoomed, shifted or stretched goes out the same way, scaled in the composer's representation; one drawn smaller than its own size, or rebuilt by libplacebo's reshaping, goes out converted either way. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#from-the-file-to-the-hdmi-cable). |
| Keep repeated pictures (`videoscreen.hadesdvkeeprepeats`) | On | Expert. While a picture stays on the screen for more than one refresh (film at 60 Hz, a paused video), it is kept there instead of being drawn and sent again: the display gets the same bytes, and the graphics do less work. Off: every refresh is drawn again. On nvidia-drm with its default module parameters, which give no vertical blank to wait for, a picture that repeats after a frame presented the way that waits is kept by committing it again ([how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#two-ways-of-drawing-a-frame)). |
| Finer gamut mapping (`videoscreen.hadesdvgamutfine`) | Off | Expert. Where a picture is mapped to the display or to SDR (Dolby Vision or HDR10 on an SDR output, Dolby Vision as HDR10 mapped to the display, Low-Latency), the colours that the display cannot show are mapped through a table of 96x64x512 entries (25 MB of the graphics chip's memory). On: one of 256x192x512 (201 MB), closer to the mapping computed exactly (measured on a software renderer: the error of 99.9 % of the pixels about a third, the largest about half), made once for a video (a third of a second on a fast processor, a few seconds on a slow one). Where the graphics chip cannot make it, the standard table is used; `kodi.log` says which one is in use ([diagnostics.md](diagnostics.md)). Applies from the next video. |

## System > Display > Stereoscopic 3D

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Switch the display to 3D (HDMI) (`videoscreen.hades3doutput`) | Automatic | For a 3D TV or projector. While a split 3D picture is drawn (over/under or side by side), the display is told so over HDMI and switches to 3D by itself, with frame packing where the display and the graphics driver have it. Off: the two halves go out as an ordinary picture and the display is switched to 3D by hand. Turn it off if a 3D video shows a black or broken picture. Without a 3D display nothing changes. ## System > Audio

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Limit PCM channels to what the display declares (`audiooutput.hadespcmlimit`) | On | Sound that Kodi decodes goes out in no more channels of PCM than the display declares in its EDID, whatever "Number of channels" says: a TV that declares PCM in 2 channels gets stereo, one that declares 6 gets 5.1. While a change of display mode takes the display's audio data away for a moment, the last ones are used. Turn it off only for a display that takes more channels of PCM than it declares: one that does not take them gives no sound with the multichannel tracks that Kodi decodes. Passthrough is not affected. Expert; under "Number of channels". |

## System > Audio > Audio Passthrough

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surround as Dolby when the display takes only stereo PCM (`audiooutput.hadessurroundtodolby`) | On | When the display declares PCM in 2 channels only, and Dolby Digital Plus or Dolby Digital in more (as an LG C5 does when it declares its own audio), a multichannel track that Kodi decodes (DTS where the display does not take it, or a track whose passthrough is off) is encoded to Dolby Digital Plus 5.1 at 768 kb/s and passed through, so the sound stays surround; or to Dolby Digital 5.1 at 640 kb/s where Dolby Digital Plus cannot go (not declared, its passthrough off, or an output that does not take 192 kHz). The encoding is lossy, and 7.1 is mixed to 5.1. Off, or where neither can be sent: the track is mixed down to stereo PCM. Needs passthrough and Dolby Digital passthrough on. Shown where "Number of channels" is above 2.0 and "Limit PCM channels to what the display declares" is on. At 2.0 the setting is Kodi's own "Enable Dolby Digital transcoding", as in Kodi; above 2.0 that one does not count, even if it was left on. |
| Allow passthrough on live TV and streams (`audiooutput.hadeslivepassthrough`) | On | The sound of live TV and of live streams is passed through as that of any other video, Dolby Atmos included. Kodi follows the clock of a live source by slowing its own clock a little, which it cannot do with sound that is passed through: where the clock of the source runs slower than that of this device, playback stops now and then for about a second to fill its buffer again. Turn it off if that happens: the sound of live sources is then decoded to PCM, as Kodi does it, without Atmos. Applies from the next video. Shown when passthrough is allowed. |

## In the player: video settings

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dolby Vision output (this video) | As the settings say | Only for a Dolby Vision video, and for that video only: the next one starts from the default again. "As the settings say" (shown as "Native Dolby Vision" where native output can be had now). "Dolby Vision as HDR10": rebuilt and sent as HDR10 whatever its profile, mapped to the display where that is set; offered where the display takes HDR10 and the rebuilding has not failed in this video. "Without Dolby Vision (as Kodi plays it)": not offered for profile 5, whose base picture is no picture by itself. "Dolby Vision measured from the HDR10 picture": offered where the base layer is the HDR10 picture of the film (profiles 7 and 8.1, not 5) and native output can be had; the RPU and the enhancement layer of the file are left aside, and the picture goes out as native Dolby Vision with metadata that Hades measures from it as it plays, as "HDR10 as Dolby Vision, measured" does (for discs whose own Dolby Vision metadata are flat). Without the mastering display's luminance in the HDR10 metadata of the stream, it goes out as HDR10. The video stream is opened again where it is, as when another video stream is chosen. |
| Tone mapping (Kodi's own setting, with a new method) | Reference (BT.2390) | How HDR video is brought to a display without HDR (shown where that is needed). "Reference (BT.2390)", added by Hades, maps HDR10 and HLG as a reference display would, and rebuilds Dolby Vision profiles 7 and 8.1 and maps them with the brightness of each picture. Kodi's "Reinhard", "ACES Filmic" and "Hable" are as they were. "Set as default for all media" keeps the choice. A Hable saved before this method existed, Kodi's default, becomes "Reference (BT.2390)" once. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#a-display-without-hdr). |

## LibreELEC settings

What this build adds to the LibreELEC settings add-on.

| Where | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *System > OS Configuration > Hardware clock* | Local time (as Windows keeps it) | What the clock on the computer's board keeps. "Local time" is how Windows keeps it: this system reads that clock and never writes it. Choose "UTC" on a computer without Windows, or whose Windows keeps the clock in UTC; otherwise the time is off by your time zone until it is taken from the internet, and wake-ups for recordings come at the wrong hour. Before UTC is set it says what that means on a computer that also runs Windows, and asks, with "No" preselected. Takes effect from the next start. Not changed on a system that keeps its storage in memory (live mode). |
| *System > Internal disks > Show the internal disks (read-only)* | Off | Makes the boot entry that shows the internal disks, read-only, the one the boot menu starts by itself, from the next start and on every computer the drive is started on. Before it is turned on it explains what that means and asks: shown, the disks can be read by everything that runs in Kodi, add-ons included; "read-only" is how they are mounted, not a lock; and that entry starts the kernel without its lock-down, unless disks are kept away (below). Choosing "Run" once in the boot menu turns it off again, and so do a reset to defaults and an update made with the installer. |
| *System > Internal disks > Internal disks that are shown* | All of them | Shown while the switch above is on. It lists the NVMe and SATA disks of this start, by their model, size and the end of their serial number, and from the next start the ones that are not ticked are kept away from the kernel: at each start that shows the internal disks the init lets go of them before anything else runs, tells the kernel to attach no other NVMe or ATA disk until the next start, and locks the kernel down (integrity), so that not even a program with full rights can bring them back. What it did is in the kernel log, on lines that start with `hades-disks:`. The first line is for any disk that is not in the list (a new one, another PC). A disk is told by its model and serial number, never by a name such as sda; one without a serial number, or with the same one as another disk of that start, is kept away while any disk is, and every disk is kept away, with no internal partition mounted, where the list cannot be read. The disk the system is on is never kept away, and disks behind RAID or SAS controllers and eMMC cannot be. Where a disk that is to be kept away cannot be (the system cannot let go of it, or cannot tell which disk it starts from), it stays, no internal partition is mounted in that start, and that is said when Kodi starts. While a disk is kept away the kernel loads only the modules of this build (not the drivers that add-ons bring), and a disk that is plugged in while the system runs is seen from the next start. It does not hold against a program that changes the list, the boot menu or what the start runs before it ([below](#outside-the-settings-screens)), and waits for the next start. It is kept in `/storage/.config/hades-internal-disks.conf`, which a reset to defaults clears and an update keeps. |
| *System > Internal disks > Internal partitions that are shown* | All of them | Shown while the switch above is on. It lists the partitions of the internal disks that this start sees, by their label, file system, size and disk, and from the next start the ones that are not ticked are not mounted: Kodi, its add-ons and file sharing do not see them. The first line is for any partition that is not in the list (a new one, another disk, another PC). A partition is told by the WWN and the serial number of its disk and its identifier in the partition table, never by a name such as sda1; one that cannot be told apart goes with the first line. A partition that is not mounted is still on its disk, where a program that runs with full rights could read it: to keep a disk out of reach, keep it away in the list above, or turn the switch off. Where the list cannot be read, no internal partition is mounted. It is kept in `/storage/.config/hades-internal-partitions.conf`, which a reset to defaults clears. |
| *System > Submit Log* | Kept on the system | Each of its two buttons asks first where the log goes: kept on this system, or uploaded to LibreELEC's paste service; filtered either way. See [diagnostics.md](diagnostics.md#submit-log). |
| *System > Submit Log > Copy the diagnostics to a USB drive* | (a button) | Copies what the diagnostics saved to a USB drive that you choose, for a system on a disk inside the PC, whose boot partition Windows does not show. See [diagnostics.md](diagnostics.md#from-a-system-on-an-internal-disk). |
| *Services > Phone remote control > Set up a phone app (Kore, Yatse)* | (a button) | Turns on what remote control apps for phones need: Kodi's web server, with a user and a password (a password is made if there is none), and control from applications on other devices. Kodi asks you to confirm each. It then shows the address, port, user and password to enter in the app, and warns when the firewall lets nothing in from the network. Whoever has the password and is on your network can then control the media centre, its add-ons included: keep it to yourself. To turn it off, or to change the password: *Settings > Services > Control*. |
| *Updates > OS Updates* | No channel | Hades does not use LibreELEC's update server. ## Outside the settings screens

Kernel parameters go on the kernel line of the boot entries, on the partition LIBREELEC: the `APPEND` lines of `syslinux.cfg` (BIOS) and the `linux` lines of `EFI/BOOT/grub.cfg` (UEFI). An update with the Windows installer writes these menus anew, so a parameter added there has to be added again after it.

| What | Default | How to change it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly trim of the storage partition (`fstrim.timer`) | On | Once a week, at idle priority, the unused blocks of `/storage` are given back to the drive; a week that the computer was off is made up for at its next start. Only that partition is trimmed. `systemctl mask fstrim.timer` turns it off |
| sysctl hardening (`60-hardening.conf`) | On | No ICMP redirects, RFC 1337, the kernel log and the kernel's addresses for root only, and no line discipline loaded by a program without privileges. A file with the same name in `/storage/.config/sysctl.d` replaces it, and one whose name sorts after it can change any of it back |
| HDR at 12 bits over HDMI on NVIDIA Turing (GTX 16, RTX 20) instead of 8, where the display takes 36-bit deep colour and the mode fits (3840x2160 up to 30 Hz on HDMI 2.0) | Off | `nvidia-modeset.hades_turing_12bpc=1` on the kernel line. Then `/sys/module/nvidia_modeset/parameters/hades_turing_12bpc` shows `Y`, and the kernel log says `Hades: HDR on ... goes out at 12 bpc` where it is used |
| 3D signalling on amdgpu | On | `amdgpu.hdmi_3d=0` on the kernel line turns it off |
| Native Dolby Vision on Intel graphics (the tunnel of `i915` and `xe`) | On | `i915.enable_dv_lab=0` on the kernel line turns it off (`xe.enable_dv_lab=0` for graphics that use the `xe` driver): Dolby Vision videos then play as on a display without native output |
| Copies between the kernel and programs checked against the bounds of their objects | On | `hardened_usercopy=off` on the kernel line turns it off |
| 32-bit programs | Off | `ia32_emulation=1` on the kernel line turns them on |
| The internal disks in the "Run" and "Live" entries (started from USB) | Hidden | The NVMe and AHCI controllers are not started (`initcall_blacklist` on the kernel line). Before anything else runs, every other storage controller that the kernel has a driver for (SATA in IDE mode and older controllers, RAID and SAS, UFS, Intel VMD, the virtual disks of a virtual machine) and an eMMC are let go, and `kernel.internal_disk_attach_disabled` at level 2 keeps the kernel from taking any of them again until the next start. Card readers, SD cards and USB drives are not concerned. What cannot be let go (a controller whose driver does not let go of it, or all of them where the disk the system starts from cannot be told) stays, and none of its partitions is mounted in those entries. The kernel log says what was let go (`hades-disks:`). The entry "Run (internal disks read-only)" shows them, read-only |
| What the start runs before the internal disks are hidden or kept away | As in LibreELEC | The start runs, as root, `post-flash.sh`, `mount-storage.sh` and `post-sysroot.sh` where they are on the partition LIBREELEC, before it hides the internal disks (the "Run" and "Live" entries, an installation on an internal disk) or keeps them away (*Internal disks that are shown*), and it installs an update left in `/storage/.update`, which is the system of the next start. So a program that writes any of them can undo the hiding from the next start, as one that changes the boot menu can: the hiding holds against what runs once the system has started, until the next start |
| The diagnostic switch | Off | The file `DIAG-ON.TXT` on the partition LIBREELEC: see [diagnostics.md](diagnostics.md#the-diagnostic-switch) |

## Kodi's own settings that matter here

- **Adjust display refresh rate** (*Player > Videos*). Dolby Vision follows it, and never changes the refresh rate it gives. With it off, the video stays at the refresh rate of the menus, and native Dolby Vision is used only where that rate can carry it ([how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#when-native-dolby-vision-is-used)).
- **Monitor** (*System > Display*). LibreELEC hides this setting, whose change took effect there only when Kodi started again; this build shows it again. "Default" chooses the display as Kodi does when it starts: an HDMI display that takes Dolby Vision first, then any HDMI display, then any other, and the computer's own screen last. Each display is shown by the name its EDID gives it and its connector, and by its graphics where the displays are on more than one ("LG TV SSCR2 (HDMI-A-2)"); a display without a name is shown by its connector, and a choice saved before keeps working. The setting is greyed where there is one display, unless it names one that is not connected.
  - A display on the graphics in use is used at once, if the driver takes it in a test commit (if not, nothing changes), and Kodi then asks whether to keep it: without a yes in 10 s, it goes back.
  - A display on other graphics needs Kodi to start again, which it offers to do now ("No" keeps the change for the next start). That start asks "Keep this monitor?" with 15 s to answer. What is not answered yes (no, back, the time running out, or a question that cannot be shown within 90 s) brings the display of before back with one more start, which asks too; where that one is not kept either, the setting goes back to "Default".
  - While a video or music plays, the setting keeps what it had and says to stop playback; while the output still finishes a change of its own (the end of HDR or of Dolby Vision after a video), it says to wait a moment. A change made while Kodi's display sleeps is used from the next start, and one made by other means (a remote control of Kodi, `guisettings.xml`) asks nothing.
- **Resolution** (*System > Display*). When the display's modes change while Kodi runs (Deep Colour turned on at the TV, for example), Kodi takes the new list a few seconds later, while nothing plays, and makes the choices a start would make: "Desktop" goes to the display's preferred mode, a resolution you chose that is still offered stays, and one that is not goes to the nearest. No restart is needed.
- **Use limited colour range (16-235)** (*System > Display*). On AMD graphics the driver now sends limited-range RGB itself to a TV that cannot select the RGB range, as the TV expects. If you turned this on for such a TV, turn it off, or the range is compressed twice. A TV that can select the range (the LG C5 can) gets full range, as before. Dolby Vision is not affected. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#other-parts).
- **Number of channels** (*System > Audio*). PCM goes out in no more channels than the display declares, whatever this says, while "Limit PCM channels to what the display declares" ([above](#system--audio)) is on. The setting itself is not changed, and `kodi.log` says when the channels are limited and what a multichannel track goes out as.
- **Audio output device** (*System > Audio*). Until an output is chosen, Kodi's "Default" takes the first one it finds, on a PC often the analogue output of the board. Hades uses instead the output whose display is the one Kodi shows its picture on (it knows it by the maker, product and name that the display's EDID and its audio data both give), for PCM and for passthrough, also when that display is turned on later; where that cannot be told for sure (no output with a display, two alike, a display without audio), nothing is changed. An output that you choose is kept and never changed; choosing "Default" again gives the choice back to Hades. `kodi.log` says which output is used and why ([diagnostics.md](diagnostics.md#what-to-look-for-in-kodilog)). Where the output that is set is not there in this start (the HDMI output of graphics that are not in use, for example), the output that Kodi would take in its place is used, with the formats asked for passthrough. The setting is not changed: when that output is there again, it is used again. `kodi.log` says what is used in its place.
- **Video scaling method** (in the player's video settings). Not used for Dolby Vision, native or shown as HDR10: the Dolby Vision engine resamples the enhancement layer as the specification says and scales the picture with libplacebo's filters. It applies to HDR10, HLG, SDR, and Dolby Vision shown by its base layer. The Dolby Vision page of the player information says so. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#other-parts).
- **Allow hardware acceleration - VAAPI** (*Player > Videos*), and its setting for each codec. Native Dolby Vision output and Dolby Vision rebuilt as HDR10 need the video decoded with VAAPI: with it off, Dolby Vision videos play as Kodi plays them, and the Dolby Vision page of the player information says why ([how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#from-the-file-to-the-hdmi-cable)).
- **Allow using DRM PRIME decoder** (*Player > Videos*). No video decoder of this build decodes to DRM PRIME in hardware: with it on, videos are decoded by the processor, except those of more than 8 bits (HDR), which are decoded as with it off. Dolby Vision videos that are sent natively or rebuilt as HDR10 are decoded with VAAPI either way.
