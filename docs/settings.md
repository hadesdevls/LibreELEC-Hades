# Settings

The settings that LibreELEC Hades adds to Kodi and to the LibreELEC settings add-on. In Kodi's settings screens, Kodi shows them when the settings level is **Advanced** or **Expert** (the level is changed at the bottom of the list on the left of a settings screen); those marked Expert need that level. Some appear only where they can apply, as said below. Each one says on screen what it does; this page puts them in one place, with the key under which Kodi keeps it in `guisettings.xml`. Those of native Dolby Vision output are read when a video starts and hold until it ends.

## System > Display > HDR

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **GUI and subtitle brightness in HDR** (`videoscreen.hadesguiwhite`) | 100 % | Brightness of the menus, the OSD and text subtitles while the TV is in HDR10, HLG or Dolby Vision mode, relative to the BT.2408 reference white (100 % = 203 nits, as in Kodi). From 25 to 300 %. Image subtitles made in HDR (UHD Blu-ray) keep their own levels. The video is never changed. Shown only with an HDR display. |
| **10-bit video at full depth (NVIDIA)** (`videoscreen.hadesfloatoutput`) | On | NVIDIA graphics give no 10-bit output, only an 8-bit and a 16-bit floating-point one; with this on, HDR10, HLG and other 10-bit video go out through the 16-bit one, without the banding of 8 bits. Turn it off if such video shows wrong colours or a black screen. Dolby Vision is not affected. Applies from the next video. Expert; shown only where the graphics have such an output (NVIDIA). |
| **HDR where RGB would be 8-bit** (`videoscreen.hadeshdr420`) | YCbCr, 10 bits or more | At 3840x2160 above 30 Hz, an HDMI 2.0 link (and an HDMI 2.1 one driven without FRL) carries RGB at 8 bits only, and HDR10 and HLG then lose two bits; in YCbCr the same link carries 10 bits or more. On AMD graphics HDR goes out in YCbCr 4:2:2 at 12 bits where both the graphics and the TV take it (display hardware DCN, but not DCN 3.1 and 3.1.5), which keeps the colour of every line; otherwise in YCbCr 4:2:0 where the TV takes that mode in 4:2:0 with deep colour, as on Intel graphics. The video is 4:2:0 already, so none of it is lost; only what is drawn over it (menus, subtitles) has less colour resolution. **8-bit RGB** sends it as before, and turns both off. Dolby Vision is not affected. Applies from the next video. Expert; shown only on Intel and AMD graphics. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#other-parts). |
| **Native Dolby Vision output** (`videoscreen.hadesdvnative`) | On | Sends Dolby Vision as such to a display that takes it (Dolby Vision Standard over HDMI). Off: Dolby Vision videos play as on a display without it: rebuilt and sent as HDR10 where "Dolby Vision as HDR10 when needed" says so, else with their HDR10 picture, as Kodi plays them; the Dolby Vision page of the player information says that it is turned off. For one video, the player's video settings have the same choice (below). Applies from the next video. The four settings below it need it on. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#when-native-dolby-vision-is-used). |
| **Dolby Vision Low-Latency (player-led)** (`videoscreen.hadesdvlowlatency`) | Off | On a TV input that offers Dolby Vision only as Low-Latency (its game mode, or a TV that has no other form), sends Dolby Vision that way: LibreELEC Hades maps the picture to the display itself, as a player does for Low-Latency, with the BT.2390 curve guided by the level 1 metadata of each scene, to the peak and the colours that the display gives for it. The mapping is that of LibreELEC Hades, not that of Dolby. A display that takes Dolby Vision Standard keeps it, whatever this says. Off: such an input gets HDR10, as before, with a notice of one sentence that says what to do (turn this on, or turn off the game settings of the TV input); why, in `kodi.log` and on the Dolby Vision page of the player information. Applies from the next video. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#dolby-vision-low-latency). |
| **HDR10+ as Dolby Vision** (`videoscreen.hadesdvfromhdr10plus`) | Off | For a display that takes Dolby Vision but not HDR10+. An HDR10+ video is sent as native Dolby Vision, with metadata made from its own HDR10+ metadata, scene by scene: the peak and the average brightness of each scene. The picture is the same HDR10 picture. What does not carry over: the tone curve and the target display that the studio chose, its saturation weight and its windows. Dolby Vision videos, HDR10 videos without HDR10+, and displays that take HDR10+, are not affected. Applies from the next video. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#other-formats-sent-as-dolby-vision). |
| **HDR10 as Dolby Vision, measured** (`videoscreen.hadesdvfromhdr10`) | Off | For a display that takes Dolby Vision. An HDR10 video, which has no dynamic metadata of its own, is sent as native Dolby Vision with metadata that Hades measures from the picture as it plays: the peak, the average and the darkest level of each scene, and the black bars. These are not the studio's metadata, and no trims are added. The picture is the same HDR10 picture. Scene cuts are found from the picture: a few are missed, a few are found where there is none, and some are found a picture late. Dolby Vision and HDR10+ videos are not affected. Applies from the next video. |
| **Dolby Vision: leave out the trims** (`videoscreen.hadesdvnotrims`) | Off | The metadata carry the trims of the film: how the colourist mapped each scene to displays of a few given peaks (levels 2 and 8). With this on the display is not sent them (nor level 10, the targets of level 8), and maps each scene from its brightness range alone. Turn it on only for a display that shows Dolby Vision with washed-out blacks or wrong colours because of them, as some projectors do. The picture that is sent does not change. Applies from the next video. |
| **Dolby Vision as HDR10 when needed** (`videoscreen.hadesdvashdr10`) | On | When Dolby Vision cannot be sent as such (a display without it, or a TV input in its game mode), a Dolby Vision video whose base picture is not HDR10 (profile 5, as streaming services use it) is rebuilt and sent as HDR10; without it, such a video plays with wrong colours. Other Dolby Vision videos play their HDR10 picture. Turn it off if such a video shows a black or broken picture. Applies from the next video. Shown only with an HDR display. On a display without HDR, profile 5 is rebuilt and mapped to SDR whatever this says (see the tone mapping, below). See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#dolby-vision-shown-as-hdr10). |
| **Also with an enhancement layer** (`videoscreen.hadesdvashdr10fel`) | Off | Under the one above: also rebuilds Dolby Vision videos with an enhancement layer (profile 7, from UHD Blu-ray) and sends them as HDR10, instead of playing their HDR10 picture. What is sent is the Dolby Vision master, which the display adapts as plain HDR10: it is not always better than the disc's HDR10 version, and it asks more of the graphics. Not available while the mapping to the display (next) is on, which covers every video. |
| **Dolby Vision as HDR10: map to the display** (`videoscreen.hadesdvhdr10map`) | Off (as mastered) | For projectors and HDR10 displays that adapt HDR10 poorly. **To this display**: the picture rebuilt from its Dolby Vision data is adapted here to the peak and the colours of the display below, shot by shot with the brightness data of each shot (level 1), with libplacebo's tone and gamut mapping, and the display is told that the picture now fits it. While "Dolby Vision as HDR10 when needed" is on, every Dolby Vision video that is not sent natively is then sent this way, whatever its profile. Turn off the display's own dynamic tone mapping for HDR10. The creative trims of Dolby Vision (levels 2 and 8) are not applied. Applies from the next video. Shown only with an HDR display. |
| **Peak brightness of the display** (`videoscreen.hadesdvhdr10peak`) | 1000 cd/m² | Under the one above, when it is on: the brightest white the display shows in its HDR mode, as measured or as its specification gives it (about 50 to 300 for a projector, 400 to 4000 for a TV). From 40 to 4000. Nothing brighter is sent. |
| **Colour gamut of the display** (`videoscreen.hadesdvhdr10gamut`) | DCI-P3 (D65) | Under the same: BT.2020, DCI-P3 (D65) or BT.709. Colours of the picture outside them are brought inside gently (perceptual gamut mapping) instead of being clipped. |
| **Tone mapping curve** (`videoscreen.hadesdvhdr10curve`) | BT.2390 | Under the same, Expert. **BT.2390** (ITU-R BT.2390) keeps what is well below the display's peak nearly as it was mastered and compresses only the brightest part; **Spline** (libplacebo's own default) also darkens the middle tones of a shot to keep its contrast, more the brighter the shot is than the display. |

The four settings added in 1.0 RC3 (Low-Latency, HDR10+ as Dolby Vision, HDR10 as Dolby Vision, the trims) are at the Advanced level, and are greyed out while "Native Dolby Vision output" is off. HDR10+ itself has no setting: its metadata go out wherever the display declares HDR10+ and the driver can send them ([how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#hdr10)). Over HDMI on NVIDIA RTX 40 graphics (Ada) that includes a display that declares HDMI variable refresh, where the driver sends its own variable refresh data in the slot HDR10+ takes on older graphics: on Ada HDR10+ then goes in another slot, which NVIDIA does not document for HDMI (not verified on a display); GTX 16, RTX 20 and RTX 30 graphics send no HDR10+ to such a display. On Intel graphics, at 3840x2160 at 24, 25 and 30 Hz, it goes out only to an HDMI 2.0 display that lists the CTA VIC of that mode, which the AVI InfoFrame then carries instead of the HDMI VIC (not verified on a display). Over DisplayPort: the official HDR10+ Gaming FAQ says that on a PC it goes over HDMI only, and whether a monitor shows HDR10+ over DisplayPort from Hades is not verified.

## System > Display > Dolby Vision engine

How native Dolby Vision frames are drawn and sent. Every default is automatic: native output as it works without these settings. While the drawing path, the composition or the transport is not at its default, or a step-down level is set, what the automatic choices measure in a video is not kept for later sessions, or not all of it. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md).

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Drawing path** (`videoscreen.hadesdvpathmode`) | Automatic | Expert. A frame without GUI elements is drawn directly into the output, or composed into a full-precision picture that is then packed. **Automatic**: each picture format is measured on this graphics chip and drawn the faster way, and what is found is kept for this graphics chip and build. **Always direct**, **Always composed**: to measure each way. Frames with subtitles, the player bar or other GUI elements, and pictures that do not fill the screen, are always composed. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#two-ways-of-drawing-a-frame). |
| **Present frames a frame ahead** (`videoscreen.hadesdvaheadmode`) | When needed | Where the graphics chip needs most of the time of a frame to prepare a picture (an integrated one with 4K Blu-ray video), the next frame is prepared while the last one waits to be shown, so that a frame that takes a little longer is still shown in time. The menus over such a video react one frame later. **When needed**: only where the frames take half of a refresh or more. **Always**: wherever it can be done, to try it on any graphics chip. **Never**: if native Dolby Vision video stutters or shows a broken picture with it. Earlier builds had this as an on/off setting; its value is carried over. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#a-frame-ahead-when-needed). |
| **Fidelity step-down** (`videoscreen.hadesdvstepmode`) | Automatic | Where the graphics chip cannot prepare a picture in the time of a frame, even drawn the faster way and a frame ahead, the picture can be rebuilt a little less precisely: some of its planes are resampled with simpler filters. **Automatic**: one level at a time and only as far as the video needs to play smoothly; graphics that keep up always show the exact picture, and what is found is kept for this graphics chip and build. **Never**: always the exact picture, even if the video stutters. **Level 1, 2, 3**: that level for every frame, to measure what it saves; a frame that a level does not change is drawn at the highest level under it that changes it, or exactly, and nothing that is measured is kept. With the default composition and transport, only frames with the residual of a full enhancement layer (FEL) have levels to step down to (1 and 2), and pictures that go out converted to BT.2020, such as 1080p videos, level 3. A level set in `advancedsettings.xml` (below) comes first. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#a-step-down-in-fidelity-where-the-graphics-chip-cannot-keep-up). |
| **Composition** (`videoscreen.hadesdvcomposer`) | As the specification | Expert. How the picture is rebuilt from its layers and metadata. **As the specification**: as the composer of ETSI GS CCM 001 does it. **libplacebo's reshaping**: as libplacebo does it by itself, to compare the two on a display. A picture outside what the specification covers is rebuilt by libplacebo's reshaping either way. |
| **Transport** (`videoscreen.hadesdvtransport`) | As certified players | Expert. **As certified players**: the picture that the composer builds, as it is, in the colour representation that the video describes (YCbCr, or IPT for profile 5), with metadata that describe it; where the menus or subtitles are drawn over the video, those pixels are converted back into that representation. **Converted to BT.2020**: the picture is rebuilt in RGB and converted to BT.2020 YCbCr, as earlier builds sent it, to compare the two on a display. A video that is scaled to the screen (a 1080p one), or rebuilt by libplacebo's reshaping, goes out converted either way. |
| **Keep repeated pictures** (`videoscreen.hadesdvkeeprepeats`) | On | Expert. While a picture stays on the screen for more than one refresh (film at 60 Hz, a paused video), it is kept there instead of being drawn and sent again: the display gets the same bytes, and the graphics chip does less work. Off: every refresh is drawn again. On nvidia-drm, which has no vertical blank to wait for with its default module parameters, every refresh is drawn again either way (see `dvkeepbycommit` below). |

## System > Display > Stereoscopic 3D

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Switch the display to 3D (HDMI)** (`videoscreen.hades3doutput`) | Automatic | For a 3D TV or projector. While a split 3D picture is drawn (over/under or side by side), the display is told so over HDMI and switches to 3D by itself; with frame packing, which keeps both eyes at full resolution, where the display and the graphics driver have it. Where the display has no 3D mode at the refresh rate of a video, as for a 30p one, the 3D mode at exactly twice that rate is used where the display has it, and each picture is shown twice. Off: the two halves go out as an ordinary picture and the display is switched to 3D by hand. Turn it off if a 3D video shows a black or broken picture. |

## System > Audio

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Limit PCM channels to what the display declares** (`audiooutput.hadespcmlimit`) | On | Sound that Kodi decodes goes out in no more channels of PCM than the display says it takes in its audio data (from its EDID), whatever "Number of channels" says: a TV that declares PCM in 2 channels gets stereo, one that declares 6 gets 5.1. While a change of display mode takes the audio data of the display away for a moment, the ones it had last are used. Off: decoded sound follows "Number of channels" alone, for a display that takes more channels of PCM than it declares; one that does not take them gives no sound with the multichannel tracks that Kodi decodes. Passthrough is not affected. Expert; under "Number of channels". |

## System > Audio > Audio Passthrough

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Surround as Dolby when the display takes only stereo PCM** (`audiooutput.hadessurroundtodolby`) | On | When the display declares PCM in 2 channels only, and Dolby Digital Plus or Dolby Digital in more (as an LG C5 does when it declares its own audio), a multichannel track that Kodi decodes (DTS where the display does not take it, or a track whose passthrough is off) is encoded to Dolby Digital Plus 5.1, or to Dolby Digital 5.1 where the display does not declare Dolby Digital Plus, its passthrough is off or the output does not take its rate (192 kHz, as Kodi's own transcoding asks), and passed through: the sound stays surround. The encoding is lossy (768 kb/s; 640 kb/s for Dolby Digital), and 7.1 is mixed to 5.1. Off, or where neither can be sent or no encoder opens: it is mixed down to stereo PCM. Needs passthrough and Dolby Digital passthrough. Shown where "Number of channels" is above 2.0 and "Limit PCM channels to what the display declares" is on; for 2.0, Kodi's "Enable Dolby Digital transcoding" is the setting, which counts only there, as in Kodi, also when it was left on from a 2.0 setting. |
| **Allow passthrough on live TV and streams** (`audiooutput.hadeslivepassthrough`) | On | The sound of live TV and of live streams is passed through as that of any other video, Dolby Atmos included. Kodi follows the clock of a live source by slowing its own clock a little, and decoded sound with it, which it cannot do with sound that is passed through: when the clock of the source runs slower than that of this device, playback stops now and then for about a second to fill its buffer again. Off: the sound of live sources is decoded to PCM, as Kodi does it, without Atmos. Applies from the next video. Shown when passthrough is allowed. |

| Setting | Default | What it does |

## LibreELEC settings

What this build adds to the LibreELEC settings add-on.

| Where | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *System > OS Configuration > Hardware clock* | Local time (as Windows keeps it) | What the clock of the computer (the one on its board) is set to. **Local time** is how Windows keeps it: on a computer that also runs Windows, this system reads that clock and never writes it. **UTC** is how Linux and most other systems keep it: choose it on a computer without Windows, or whose Windows keeps the clock in UTC; otherwise the time is off by your time zone until it is taken from the internet, and wake-ups for recordings come at the wrong hour. Before UTC is set it says what that means on a computer that also runs Windows, and asks, with "no" preselected. It takes effect from the next start: UTC writes `/storage/.cache/adjtime`, which the start of the system reads. Not changed on a system that keeps its storage in memory (live mode). |
| *System > Internal disks > Show the internal disks (read-only)* | Off | Makes the boot entry that shows the internal disks, read-only, the one the boot menu starts by itself. It explains what it means and asks before it is turned on. Shown, the disks can be read by everything that runs in Kodi, add-ons included; "read-only" is how they are mounted, not a lock; and that entry starts the kernel without its lock-down. Choosing "Run" once in the boot menu turns it off again, and so do a reset to defaults and an update made with the installer. |
| *System > Submit Log* | Saved on the system | Each button asks first where the log goes. By default it is saved on the system in `/storage/logfiles`, through the filter of the diagnostics; uploading to LibreELEC's paste service is offered, with what that means explained first, and the uploaded log goes through the same filter. See [diagnostics.md](diagnostics.md#submit-log). |
| *System > Submit Log > Copy the diagnostics to a USB drive* | (a button) | Copies what the diagnostics saved on this system to a USB drive that you choose, into its folder `hades-diag`, in a folder named after the date and time, and reads it back from the drive, file by file, before it says that it is there. Nothing on the drive is written over. It is for a system on a disk inside the PC, whose boot partition Windows does not show. See [diagnostics.md](diagnostics.md). |
| *Services > Phone remote control > Set up a phone app (Kore, Yatse)* | (a button) | Turns on what remote control apps for phones need: Kodi's web server, with a user and a password (one is made if it has none), and control from applications on other devices. Whoever has the password and is on your network can then control the media centre, its add-ons included: keep it to yourself. To turn it off, or change the password: *Settings > Services > Control*. |
| *Updates > OS Updates* | No channel | LibreELEC Hades does not use LibreELEC's update server. **Available Versions** says where the versions of Hades are published and how to install one; **Automatic Updates** looks for nothing and downloads nothing, whatever is chosen. A signed update channel of Hades is built in (a manifest signed with Ed25519, never an older version, files over HTTPS checked against their SHA-256) and stays off until an image carries a channel, which none does yet. |

## Kodi's own settings that matter here

- **Player > Videos > Adjust display refresh rate.** Dolby Vision follows it, and never changes the refresh rate it gives. With it on, a video gets its own refresh rate. With it off, the refresh rate stays the one of the menus: native Dolby Vision is then used if that rate can carry it (up to 60 Hz), and with the menus at 1920x1080 the video's mode is 3840x2160 at that rate, also for a file opened while another plays. With the menus at 100 Hz or more and the adjustment off, a Dolby Vision video plays without native output: its HDR10 picture, or rebuilt as HDR10 where "Dolby Vision as HDR10 when needed" says so.
- **System > Display > Monitor.** LibreELEC hides this setting on GBM, where a change of it took effect only when Kodi started again, past the question that keeps or undoes a change; this build shows it again. It is greyed where there is one display. Each display is shown by the name its EDID gives it, with its connector, and with its graphics where the displays offered are on more than one ("LG TV SSCR2 (HDMI-A-2)"); a display without a name is shown by its connector, and a choice saved before keeps working. A change is used at once where the display is on the graphics in use: Kodi moves there, after asking the driver with a commit that only tests it (where the driver would not take it, nothing changes), and Kodi's own question to keep the change follows; without a yes in 10 s, Kodi goes back. A display of other graphics asks whether to start Kodi again now ("no" keeps it for the next start); the start that follows asks "Keep this monitor?" with 15 s to answer, and what is not answered yes (no, back, the time running out, or a question that cannot be shown within 90 s) puts the display of before back with one more start, which asks too; where that one is not kept either, the setting goes back to Default. A change made by other means (a remote control of Kodi, `guisettings.xml`) asks nothing. While a video or music plays the setting keeps what it had and says to stop playback, and while the output still finishes a change of its own (the end of HDR or of Dolby Vision after a video, a change of mode that waits) it says to wait a moment. A change made while the display of Kodi sleeps is used from the next start. Not yet tried on graphics hardware.
- **System > Display > Use limited colour range (16-235).** On AMD graphics the driver now sends limited-range RGB itself to a TV that cannot select the RGB range, as the TV expects. If you turned this on for such a TV, turn it off, or the range is compressed twice. A TV that can select the range (the LG C5 does) gets full range, as before. Dolby Vision is not affected.
- **System > Audio > Number of channels.** PCM goes out in no more channels than the display says, in its EDID, that it takes ("Limit PCM channels to what the display declares", above): a TV whose EDID declares 2 channels of PCM gets stereo when Kodi decodes a multichannel track (DTS on a TV without DTS, TrueHD without passthrough), or Dolby Digital Plus 5.1, passed through, where it declares that ("Surround as Dolby when the display takes only stereo PCM"; with the setting at 2.0, Kodi's "Enable Dolby Digital transcoding"). Before, it got as many channels as the setting asked, which such a TV may not play: seen with an LG C5 and its sound bar on eARC, silent with a DTS-HD Master Audio track on a day when its EDID declared 2 channels of PCM. The setting is not changed, and `kodi.log` says when the channels are limited and what a multichannel track goes out as.
- **System > Audio > Audio output device.** Where the output that is set is not there in this start (the HDMI output of graphics that is not in use, for example), the output that Kodi would take in its place is used, and its formats are the ones asked for passthrough. The setting is not changed: when that output is there again, it is used again, and `kodi.log` says what is used in its place.
- **Player > Videos > Video scaling method.** Not used for Dolby Vision, native or shown as HDR10: the Dolby Vision engine resamples the enhancement layer as the specification says and scales the picture with libplacebo's filters. It applies to HDR10, HLG, SDR, and Dolby Vision shown by its base layer. The Dolby Vision page of the player information says so.
- **Player > Videos > Allow hardware acceleration - VAAPI** (and its setting for each codec). Native Dolby Vision output and Dolby Vision rebuilt as HDR10 need the video decoded with VAAPI: with it off, Dolby Vision videos play as Kodi plays them, and the Dolby Vision page of the player information says why.
- **Player > Videos > Allow using DRM PRIME decoder.** No video decoder of this build decodes to DRM PRIME in hardware: with it on, videos are decoded by the processor, except those of more than 8 bits (HDR), which are decoded as with it off. Dolby Vision videos that are sent natively or rebuilt as HDR10 are decoded with VAAPI either way.
