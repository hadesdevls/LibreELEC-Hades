# Settings

The settings that LibreELEC Hades adds to Kodi. In the settings screens Kodi shows them when the settings level is **Advanced** or **Expert** (the level is changed at the bottom of the list on the left of a settings screen); those marked Expert need that level. Some appear only where they can apply, as said below. Each one says on screen what it does; this page puts them in one place. Those of native Dolby Vision output are read when a video starts and hold until it ends.

## System > Display > HDR

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **GUI and subtitle brightness in HDR** | 100 % | Brightness of the menus, the OSD and text subtitles while the TV is in HDR10, HLG or Dolby Vision mode, relative to the BT.2408 reference white (100 % = 203 nits, as in Kodi). From 25 to 300 %. Image subtitles made in HDR (UHD Blu-ray) keep their own levels. The video is never changed. Shown only with an HDR display. |
| **10-bit video at full depth (NVIDIA)** | On | NVIDIA graphics give no 10-bit output, only an 8-bit and a 16-bit floating-point one; with this on, HDR10, HLG and other 10-bit video go out through the 16-bit one, without the banding of 8 bits. Turn it off if such video shows wrong colours or a black screen. Dolby Vision is not affected. Applies from the next video. Expert; shown only where the graphics have such an output (NVIDIA). |
| **HDR where RGB would be 8-bit** | 10-bit YCbCr 4:2:0 | At 3840x2160 above 30 Hz, an HDMI 2.0 link (and an HDMI 2.1 one driven without FRL) carries RGB at 8 bits only, and HDR10 and HLG then lose two bits; in YCbCr 4:2:0 the same link carries 10 bits or more. The video is 4:2:0 already, so none of it is lost; only what is drawn over it (menus, subtitles) has half the colour resolution. Used only where the TV takes that mode in 4:2:0 with deep colour. **8-bit RGB** sends it as before. Dolby Vision is not affected. Applies from the next video. Expert; shown only on Intel and AMD graphics. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#other-parts). |
| **Native Dolby Vision output** | On | Sends Dolby Vision as such to a display that takes it (Dolby Vision Standard over HDMI). Off: Dolby Vision videos play as on a display without it: rebuilt and sent as HDR10 where "Dolby Vision as HDR10 when needed" says so, else with their HDR10 picture, as Kodi plays them; the Dolby Vision page of the player information says that it is turned off. For one video, the player's video settings have the same choice (below). Applies from the next video. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#when-native-dolby-vision-is-used). |
| **Dolby Vision as HDR10 when needed** | On | When Dolby Vision cannot be sent as such (a display without it, or a TV input in its game mode), a Dolby Vision video whose base picture is not HDR10 (profile 5, as streaming services use it) is rebuilt and sent as HDR10; without it, such a video plays with wrong colours. Other Dolby Vision videos play their HDR10 picture. Turn it off if such a video shows a black or broken picture. Applies from the next video. Shown only with an HDR display. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#dolby-vision-shown-as-hdr10). |
| **Also with an enhancement layer** | Off | Under the one above: also rebuilds Dolby Vision videos with an enhancement layer (profile 7, from UHD Blu-ray) and sends them as HDR10, instead of playing their HDR10 picture. What is sent is the Dolby Vision master, which the display adapts as plain HDR10: it is not always better than the disc's HDR10 version, and it asks more of the graphics. Not available while the mapping to the display (next) is on, which covers every video. |
| **Dolby Vision as HDR10: map to the display** | Off (as mastered) | For projectors and HDR10 displays that adapt HDR10 poorly. **To this display**: the picture rebuilt from its Dolby Vision data is adapted here to the peak and the colours of the display below, shot by shot with the brightness data of each shot (level 1), with libplacebo's tone and gamut mapping, and the display is told that the picture now fits it. While "Dolby Vision as HDR10 when needed" is on, every Dolby Vision video that is not sent natively is then sent this way, whatever its profile. Turn off the display's own dynamic tone mapping for HDR10. The creative trims of Dolby Vision (levels 2 and 8) are not applied. Applies from the next video. Shown only with an HDR display. |
| **Peak brightness of the display** | 1000 cd/m² | Under the one above, when it is on: the brightest white the display shows in its HDR mode, as measured or as its specification gives it (about 50 to 300 for a projector, 400 to 4000 for a TV). From 40 to 4000. Nothing brighter is sent. |
| **Colour gamut of the display** | DCI-P3 (D65) | Under the same: BT.2020, DCI-P3 (D65) or BT.709. Colours of the picture outside them are brought inside gently (perceptual gamut mapping) instead of being clipped. |
| **Tone mapping curve** | BT.2390 | Under the same, Expert. **BT.2390** (ITU-R BT.2390) keeps what is well below the display's peak nearly as it was mastered and compresses only the brightest part; **Spline** (libplacebo's own default) also darkens the middle tones of a shot to keep its contrast, more the brighter the shot is than the display. |

## System > Display > Dolby Vision engine

How native Dolby Vision frames are drawn and sent. Every default is automatic: native output as it works without these settings. While the drawing path, the composition or the transport is not at its default, or a step-down level is set, what the automatic choices measure in a video is not kept for later sessions, or not all of it. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md).

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Drawing path** | Automatic | Expert. A frame without GUI elements is drawn directly into the output, or composed into a full-precision picture that is then packed. **Automatic**: each picture format is measured on this graphics chip and drawn the faster way, and what is found is kept for this graphics chip and build. **Always direct**, **Always composed**: to measure each way. Frames with subtitles, the player bar or other GUI elements, and pictures that do not fill the screen, are always composed. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#two-ways-of-drawing-a-frame). |
| **Present frames a frame ahead** | When needed | Where the graphics chip needs most of the time of a frame to prepare a picture (an integrated one with 4K Blu-ray video), the next frame is prepared while the last one waits to be shown, so that a frame that takes a little longer is still shown in time. The menus over such a video react one frame later. **When needed**: only where the frames take half of a refresh or more. **Always**: wherever it can be done, to try it on any graphics chip. **Never**: if native Dolby Vision video stutters or shows a broken picture with it. Earlier builds had this as an on/off setting; its value is carried over. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#a-frame-ahead-when-needed). |
| **Fidelity step-down** | Automatic | Where the graphics chip cannot prepare a picture in the time of a frame, even drawn the faster way and a frame ahead, the picture can be rebuilt a little less precisely: some of its planes are resampled with simpler filters. **Automatic**: one level at a time and only as far as the video needs to play smoothly; graphics that keep up always show the exact picture, and what is found is kept for this graphics chip and build. **Never**: always the exact picture, even if the video stutters. **Level 1, 2, 3**: that level for every frame, to measure what it saves; a frame that a level does not change is drawn at the highest level under it that changes it, or exactly, and nothing that is measured is kept. With the default composition and transport, only frames with the residual of a full enhancement layer (FEL) have levels to step down to (1 and 2), and pictures that go out converted to BT.2020, such as 1080p videos, level 3. A level set in `advancedsettings.xml` (below) comes first. See [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#a-step-down-in-fidelity-where-the-graphics-chip-cannot-keep-up). |
| **Composition** | As the specification | Expert. How the picture is rebuilt from its layers and metadata. **As the specification**: as the composer of ETSI GS CCM 001 does it. **libplacebo's reshaping**: as libplacebo does it by itself, to compare the two on a display. A picture outside what the specification covers is rebuilt by libplacebo's reshaping either way. |
| **Transport** | As certified players | Expert. **As certified players**: the picture that the composer builds, as it is, in the colour representation that the video describes (YCbCr, or IPT for profile 5), with metadata that describe it; where the menus or subtitles are drawn over the video, those pixels are converted back into that representation. **Converted to BT.2020**: the picture is rebuilt in RGB and converted to BT.2020 YCbCr, as earlier builds sent it, to compare the two on a display. A video that is scaled to the screen (a 1080p one), or rebuilt by libplacebo's reshaping, goes out converted either way. |
| **Keep repeated pictures** | On | Expert. While a picture stays on the screen for more than one refresh (film at 60 Hz, a paused video), it is kept there instead of being drawn and sent again: the display gets the same bytes, and the graphics chip does less work. Off: every refresh is drawn again. |

## System > Display > Stereoscopic 3D

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Switch the display to 3D (HDMI)** | Automatic | For a 3D TV or projector. While a split 3D picture is drawn (over/under or side by side), the display is told so over HDMI and switches to 3D by itself; with frame packing, which keeps both eyes at full resolution, where the display and the graphics driver have it. Off: the two halves go out as an ordinary picture and the display is switched to 3D by hand. Turn it off if a 3D video shows a black or broken picture. |

## System > Audio > Audio Passthrough

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Allow passthrough on live TV and streams** | On | The sound of live TV and of live streams is passed through as that of any other video, Dolby Atmos included. Kodi follows the clock of a live source by slowing its own clock a little, and decoded sound with it, which it cannot do with sound that is passed through: when the clock of the source runs slower than that of this device, playback stops now and then for about a second to fill its buffer again. Off: the sound of live sources is decoded to PCM, as Kodi does it, without Atmos. Applies from the next video. Shown when passthrough is allowed. |

## Player > Videos > Stereoscopic 3D

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Decode both views of 3D Blu-ray video (MVC)** | Automatic | Automatic: both views are decoded, by the processor, where the display says that it takes 3D (it has to be on when Kodi starts); elsewhere the video plays in 2D as before, with hardware decoding. Always: on any display, for anaglyph glasses or a 3D display that does not say what it is. Off: never. Applies from the next video. |

## In the player: video settings

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dolby Vision output (this video)** | As the settings say | Only for a Dolby Vision video, for that video only (the next one starts from the default again). **As the settings say** (shown as **Native Dolby Vision** where native output can be had now); **Dolby Vision as HDR10**: rebuilt and sent as HDR10 whatever its profile, mapped to the display where that is set; offered where the display takes HDR10 and the rebuilding has not failed in this video; **Without Dolby Vision (as Kodi plays it)**: not for a video whose base picture is no picture by itself (profile 5). The video stream is opened again where it is, as when another video stream is chosen. |

## Kodi's own settings that matter here

- **Player > Videos > Adjust display refresh rate.** Dolby Vision follows it, and never changes the refresh rate it gives. With it on, a video gets its own refresh rate. With it off, the refresh rate stays the one of the menus: native Dolby Vision is then used if that rate can carry it (up to 60 Hz), and with the menus at 1920x1080 the video's mode is 3840x2160 at that rate. With the menus at 100 Hz or more and the adjustment off, a Dolby Vision video plays without native output: its HDR10 picture, or rebuilt as HDR10 where "Dolby Vision as HDR10 when needed" says so.
- **System > Audio > Number of channels.** PCM goes out in no more channels than the display says, in its EDID, that it takes: a TV whose EDID declares 2 channels of PCM gets stereo when Kodi decodes a multichannel track (DTS on a TV without DTS, TrueHD without passthrough), or, with passthrough and "Enable Dolby Digital transcoding" on, Dolby Digital. Before, it got as many channels as the setting asked, which such a TV may not play: seen with an LG C5 and its sound bar on eARC, silent with a DTS-HD Master Audio track on a day when its EDID declared 2 channels of PCM. The setting is not changed, and `kodi.log` says when the channels are limited.
- **Player > Videos > Allow hardware acceleration - VAAPI** (and its setting for each codec). Native Dolby Vision output and Dolby Vision rebuilt as HDR10 need the video decoded with VAAPI: with it off, Dolby Vision videos play as Kodi plays them, and the Dolby Vision page of the player information says why.
- **Player > Videos > Allow using DRM PRIME decoder.** No video decoder of this build decodes to DRM PRIME in hardware: with it on, videos are decoded by the processor, except those of more than 8 bits (HDR), which are decoded as with it off. Dolby Vision videos that are sent natively or rebuilt as HDR10 are decoded with VAAPI either way.

## Hidden settings and `advancedsettings.xml`

| Where | What | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `videoscreen.hadesdvpath` in `guisettings.xml` (not shown in the settings screens) | The way of drawing found for each picture format, graphics chip, driver and build ([how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#two-ways-of-drawing-a-frame)). Empty it to have it measured again by the next Dolby Vision video | Empty |
| `videoscreen.hadesdvstep` in `guisettings.xml` (not shown) | The fidelity step-down levels found for each picture format, graphics chip, driver and build ([how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#a-step-down-in-fidelity-where-the-graphics-chip-cannot-keep-up)). Empty it to have them found again | Empty |
| `videoscreen.hadesdvahead` and `videoscreen.hadesdvstepdown` in `guisettings.xml` (not shown) | The on/off settings of earlier builds that "Present frames a frame ahead" and "Fidelity step-down" took over, kept so that a choice survives an update and the way back to an earlier build | On |
| `<video><dvaheadalways>true</dvaheadalways></video>` in `advancedsettings.xml` | Turns "When needed" of "Present frames a frame ahead" into "Always" (not "Never") | `false` |
| `<video><dvearlymask>false</dvearlymask></video>` | Stops writing the black bars before the picture is rebuilt. It changes no pixel; it is there to measure what it saves | `true` |
| `<video><dvstepdown>N</dvstepdown></video>` | Forces a fidelity step-down level for every native Dolby Vision frame, N from 0 to 3, whatever the setting says. 0 is the faithful picture; 1 to 3 change it. It is there to measure on a graphics chip how much GPU time each level saves: the level is in the GPU-time lines of `kodi.log` and on the Dolby Vision page | Not set |
| `<video><dvccmcomposer>false</dvccmcomposer></video>` | Rebuilds the picture with libplacebo's reshaping, as the "Composition" setting does | `true` |
| `<video><dvkeeprepeats>false</dvkeeprepeats></video>` | Draws every refresh again, as "Keep repeated pictures" off does | `true` |
| `<video><dvverify>N</dvverify></video>` | For a test on a graphics chip: up to N native frames of each native output are checked against the same picture drawn once more with every optimisation that must not change bytes turned off | `0`: none |
| `<video><dvwarmup>false</dvwarmup></video>` | Makes each GPU program of native output when a frame first needs it, instead of before the first frame | `true` |
| `<audio><pcmchannelsfromeld>false</pcmchannelsfromeld></audio>` | Sends PCM in as many channels as "Number of channels" asks, whatever the display says it takes, for a display that takes more than it says | `true` |
