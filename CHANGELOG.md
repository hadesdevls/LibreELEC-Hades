# Changelog

The versions of LibreELEC Hades. Before 1.0 RC1 there were only private test builds.

*Las versiones de LibreELEC Hades, cada una también en español. Antes de 1.0 RC1 solo hubo builds de prueba privadas.*

## 1.0 RC3

### What's new for you

- **Menus over Dolby Vision cost far less.** A frame of native Dolby Vision with a menu, the OSD, the seek bar or subtitles over it is now made only where they are drawn, with the same bytes sent. Measured on the integrated Radeon graphics of a Ryzen 9 7950X3D: 17.8 ms of GPU time instead of 30.5 with the seek bar over a profile 7 film, which has 41.7 ms for each frame. A measurement that cost about 40 ms a frame on a Radeon RX 7600, and made films with a menu over them play at about 16 frames a second (issue #3), is now made block by block. Profile 5, whose pixels under a menu cost the most, now has a pass of its own for them: under the player information, about 60 ms instead of about 83 on that integrated graphics, measured through Direct3D 12 under Windows on a machine busy with other work and not yet with the Linux driver, with the same bytes sent.
- **Dolby Vision Low-Latency ("player-led"), as an option.** For a TV input that offers Dolby Vision only in its low-latency form (the game mode of some TVs, or a TV that has no other), Hades can send it that way, mapping the picture to the display itself. Off by default. The mapping is LibreELEC Hades', not Dolby's.
- **HDR10+ to displays that take it.** The dynamic metadata of each frame of an HDR10+ video now goes out with that frame to a display that declares HDR10+, on NVIDIA, AMD and Intel graphics, within the limits of each driver. Over DisplayPort on NVIDIA Ada graphics (RTX 40) it is now written without the checksum that the hardware adds to HDMI InfoFrames: before this change a monitor that declares HDR10+ showed HDR10 over DisplayPort; whether it shows HDR10+ now is not verified, and the official HDR10+ Gaming FAQ says that on a PC it goes over HDMI only. Over HDMI it now also goes out from RTX 40 graphics to a display that declares HDMI variable refresh, and from Intel graphics at 3840x2160 at 24, 25 and 30 Hz to an HDMI 2.0 display that lists that mode; neither has been seen on a display yet.
- **Other formats sent as Dolby Vision, as options (off by default):** an HDR10+ video to a TV that takes Dolby Vision but not HDR10+, with metadata made from its own HDR10+ metadata; and an HDR10 video, with metadata that Hades measures from the picture as it plays and that say so: they are not the studio's.
- **For projectors:** Dolby Vision can be sent without its trims, for a display that shows washed-out blacks or wrong colours with them (off by default).
- **HDR and Dolby Vision on a display without HDR.** HDR10, HLG and Dolby Vision profiles 5, 7 and 8.1 are mapped to SDR with the mapping of a reference display (ITU-R BT.2390), now the default tone mapping method: measured, much closer to the master than Kodi's own methods. Profile 5 on such a display no longer comes out green and purple.
- **More Dolby Vision files play as Dolby Vision:** profile 7 in two video tracks of MKV and MP4 files (issues #1 and #5), and HEVC streams that carry Dolby Vision without a record of it in their container.
- **More TVs and graphics:** Dolby Vision TVs that read display management 2 or 3 (many of 2017 and 2018), Radeon RX 9000 graphics (RDNA 4), Intel UHD 6xx graphics, and the HDMI port of laptops whose NVIDIA graphics drive it through a dual-mode DisplayPort connector.
- **Better HDR10 on AMD and NVIDIA:** on AMD, HDR10 in YCbCr 4:2:2 at 12 bits where RGB would be 8 bits; on NVIDIA GeForce GTX 16 and RTX 20 graphics, HDR over HDMI at 12 bits instead of 8 where the mode fits, off by default and turned on with `nvidia-modeset.hades_turing_12bpc=1` on the kernel line.
- **Fewer black frames and display changes:** Dolby Vision shown as HDR10 keeps the last whole picture where one fails; the next video of a list at another frame rate changes the display once; the menus keep moving while a change of display waits; an NVIDIA output whose display stops answering is turned off and on until it comes back; and after a wake from suspend the mode is set again where NVIDIA's driver left the output off (a laptop had no picture after a wake).
- **Sound:** PCM in no more channels than the TV says it takes (a TV that declared 2 channels of PCM could be sent more, the likely cause of a DTS-HD track that played no sound), also through a change of display mode, with an expert setting to turn it off; where the TV takes only stereo PCM but Dolby in more channels, a multichannel track that Kodi decodes goes out as Dolby Digital Plus or Dolby Digital 5.1 instead of stereo (a setting, on); and an HDMI audio output that is set but not there no longer keeps passthrough off. The Dolby encoding has not been heard through a TV yet.
- **The mouse and the menus:** the pointer keeps the fractions of a pixel of each motion (a slow movement moved it less than it should, or not at all), and motions that pile up are handled as one; when a film starts from an add-on, the menus keep drawing, with the busy dialog, while the server of the stream is asked what the stream is (they froze for 3.2 to 6.7 s in tests); and `kodi.log` says once a minute when the interface was slow. Not yet tried on hardware.
- **Changing the monitor:** *System > Display > Monitor*, which LibreELEC hides, is shown again, by the name of each display, and greyed with one display. A change to another display of the same graphics moves Kodi there at once, with Kodi's question to keep it; a display of other graphics starts Kodi again, and the start asks whether to keep it, going back to the display before where it is not kept. Not while something plays. Not yet tried on hardware.
- **Diagnostics that can be shared:** addresses, serial numbers, network names and the names of films become markers or go, in every file; the diagnostics can be copied to a USB drive, also with nothing on the screen, and an update with the installer keeps them.
- **Easier to live with:** a phone remote control in one step, a setting for a PC whose clock keeps UTC, a weekly trim of the storage partition, the player information of Hades in any skin with the GPU's use and temperature, and hardening that was measured to cost nothing in playback.
- **Up to date and safer:** LibreELEC and Kodi at their latest commits, libplacebo, LLVM, systemd and kernel firmware, and fixes for known security issues in FFmpeg, OpenSSH, Python, curl, BlueZ, libarchive and libtiff, with four fixes of Hades for libbluray, libdisplay-info and libdvdnav.

### Before you update

- **AMD graphics with a TV that cannot select the RGB range.** AMD graphics now send such a TV limited-range RGB, as the TV expects (the change is AMD's). If you turned on Kodi's *System > Display > Use limited colour range (16-235)* because blacks were crushed on such a TV, turn it off, or the range is compressed twice. A TV that selects the range, as the LG C5 does, gets what it got before.
- **Tone mapping.** A tone mapping method saved as Hable, which was Kodi's default, becomes "Reference (BT.2390)" once. Hable chosen again afterwards stays.

### Details

**Dolby Vision picture and metadata**

- The PQ curve of the Dolby Vision decoding is computed without cancellation (`libplacebo-9942`): a profile 5 picture rebuilt as HDR10 had bands of up to 315 codes of 4095 at the edge of BT.2020; now 39 at most, measured on a chart.
- The mapping to a display (SDR, projectors, Low-Latency) maps to the display's peak, not to a virtual peak above it that darkened the whole picture by 2 to 11 % (`libplacebo-9943`): measured on 17 pictures, the 99th percentile of the error lower in all 45 mappings, by up to 7.60 to 1.35 Delta E ITP, with the highlights within 1. Its gamut table is 96x64x512 instead of 48x32x256: the largest error on SDR 1.10 instead of 2.22.
- Menus or subtitles over the bars no longer mark a new scene in the metadata (ETSI GS CCM 001, 6.2.2): a change of the active area takes a new metadata id instead, and a bar stays in the area for 2 s of video after what was drawn over it is gone (a minute of subtitles in a bar: 2 changes instead of 32).
- A display of display management 2 or 3 is sent the content mapping v2.9 part of the metadata, which it reads; the content mapping v4.0 blocks, which it does not, are left out.
- A picture that goes out without its enhancement picture (after a seek, or late) carries the RPU of its own access unit, not that of a neighbour.
- The enhancement layer starts by picture order at a random-access picture of its own where the two layers do not share one; with the base layer reordered, it never started before (measured with a stream made for it). A profile 7 title whose layer never started on integrated AMD graphics (253 enhancement units passed over in 10 s) may have been such a stream: not verified. A new VAAPI device context of the same display no longer starts the layer again, and a MEL stream loses a residual once at most.
- Dolby Vision shown as HDR10 draws into a second texture and shows a picture only when it is whole (132 MB more at 3840x2160); where nothing rebuilt can be shown, the base layer is shown when it is a picture by itself. Its 8-bit limited-range copy has white at 235, not 234.
- The video scaling method of Kodi is not used by the Dolby Vision engine, which resamples as the specification says; the Dolby Vision page of the player information now says so.

**Performance**

- A frame with GUI elements over native Dolby Vision is made only in the tiles of 16x16 pixels that the GUI reaches, with the same bytes. Measured through Mesa's OpenGL on Direct3D 12 under Windows, not yet with the Linux drivers: on the integrated Radeon graphics of a Ryzen 9 7950X3D, the frame that waits for its refresh (a frame ahead) with two lines of subtitles 17.7 ms (16.5) instead of 29.8 (24.2), with an OSD 28.5 (21.6) instead of 42.6 (25.9), and 13.3 ms without GUI; on an RTX 4080 SUPER, 1.4 to 2.7 ms instead of 1.5 to 9.3. `<video><dvdrawntiles>false</dvdrawntiles></video>` turns it off, to measure it.
- The edges drawn over the bars are measured in blocks, each pixel read once: on that integrated graphics 5.5 ms instead of 19.6 under a dimmed screen.
- Profile 5 frames with the GUI over them: the picture under a pixel drawn over is decoded once instead of twice, and a pass of its own converts each pair of those pixels once before the transport takes them, three conversions where there were four. On the integrated Radeon graphics above, through Direct3D 12 under Windows and on a machine busy with other work, so the figures are approximate, a profile 5 frame under the player information takes about 60 ms instead of about 83, a window over a dimmed screen about 74 instead of about 101, the OSD about 30 instead of about 38; with radeonsi, the Linux driver, not measured yet. Profiles 7 and 8.1 do not take the pass. The same bytes in 162 frames with the GUI over them, on a software renderer and on both GPUs of that PC. Where a GPU cannot make the pass, the transport converts those pixels itself as before (the same bytes, more GPU time) and `kodi.log` says so.
- The presentation a frame ahead is not switched on or off by a seek, or by menus that have just appeared.
- The fidelity step-down keeps a level for each set of levels a format can have, so what it found is remembered also with the default transport, which it was not: in a model of a slow GPU, 26 refreshes missed at the start of a video instead of 102.

**Display and HDMI**

- Dolby Vision Low-Latency (`videoscreen.hadesdvlowlatency`, off): only on a display that offers Low-Latency and not Standard. The picture is mapped by the BT.2390 curve guided by each scene's level 1 metadata, to the peak and primaries of the display's Dolby Vision block, and sent as YCbCr 4:2:2 at 12 bits, BT.2020, limited range, through the tunnel, with the low-latency vendor InfoFrame (`linux-9975` to `9977` for amdgpu, i915 and xe; `nvidia-open-kernel-modules-9030`). `<video><dvllpattern>true</dvllpattern></video>` shows a test pattern in place of the picture, for a first look at a display.
- HDR10+: a property of DRM for the vendor InfoFrame of each frame (`linux-9980`) and its backends (`linux-9981` amdgpu, `linux-9982` i915 and xe, `nvidia-open-kernel-modules-9020`), sent with the commit of the frame and never with a change of mode; fewer than 12 pictures without metadata keep the last InfoFrame, 12 go back to HDR10. The flag of graphics over the video is set while Kodi's GUI is over it.
- HDR10+ over DisplayPort on NVIDIA Ada: the InfoFrame SDP is written into its slot without the checksum of the hardware (`nvidia-open-kernel-modules-9020`). That checksum is the one of an HDMI InfoFrame, and in an SDP it would take the place of the version byte; the bytes of the SDP do not change. Before this change, a monitor that declares HDR10+ showed HDR10 over DisplayPort from an RTX 4080 SUPER, with the SDP written in each of five films; whether the checksum was the cause, and whether it shows HDR10+ now, is not verified. A host test runs the packets through NVIDIA's own packet writers (24 checks). The HDMI route does not change. The official HDR10+ Gaming FAQ says that on a PC HDR10+ goes over HDMI only.
- HDR10+ over HDMI on NVIDIA to a display that declares HDMI variable refresh: the driver sends its variable refresh data in the generic InfoFrame slot every frame to such a display (at 50 Hz and 720 lines and up), whether the flips use variable refresh or not, and HDR10+ was refused there (seen from an RTX 4080 SUPER to a monitor that declares HDR10+ and HDMI VRR). On Ada it now goes in shared generic slot 2, every frame, with its own checksum; GTX 16, RTX 20 and RTX 30 graphics, which have no other slot, still send none. That this slot reaches an HDMI display is not documented by NVIDIA and not verified. A host test runs the packets through NVIDIA's own packet writers (49 checks). A refusal says why in the kernel log (`Hades: HDR10+ refused on head N: ...`), and `kodi.log` says that the driver refused the metadata in the test of its commits.
- HDR10+ over HDMI on Intel at 3840x2160 at 24, 25 and 30 Hz and 4096x2160 at 24 Hz (and their 1000/1001 rates), which DRM signals with the HDMI VIC in the vendor InfoFrame that HDR10+ also needs: to an HDMI 2.0 display that lists the CTA VIC of the mode and declares HDR10+, the AVI InfoFrame carries that VIC, as NVIDIA's driver signals these modes, and the vendor InfoFrame carries HDR10+ (`linux-9982`); the change of mode of the video carries the HDR10+ data of its first frame. A film at 23.976 Hz from an Ice Lake to an HDR10+ TV (issue #2) went out as HDR10. Everything else goes out as before, and a refusal says why in the kernel log. Host test with the kernel's own InfoFrame packer and the EDIDs of three displays (33 checks); not yet seen on a display.
- HDR10+ as Dolby Vision (`videoscreen.hadesdvfromhdr10plus`, off): profile 8.1 metadata scene by scene from the HDR10+ metadata, with the rules and defaults of dovi_tool and hdr10plus_tool; measured on 166 pictures against the level 1 computed from the pictures, the average 11 codes off on average (dovi_tool's rule: 112 too high).
- HDR10 as Dolby Vision, measured (`videoscreen.hadesdvfromhdr10`, off): level 1 and the active area measured on the GPU from the pictures that Kodi has decoded ahead, scene cuts found from the picture and marked as such, no trims and no level 3. Measured on two open films against the scene cuts of their Dolby Vision metadata: 96 of 99 found on their picture on the one the rule was tuned on, 42 of 51 on the other, with cuts also found where there is none.
- Trims left out (`videoscreen.hadesdvnotrims`, off): no level 2, 8 or 10 blocks are sent; the picture does not change.
- Dolby Vision TVs whose 12-byte version 1 block says display management 2 or 3: 119 of 584 public EDIDs with a Dolby block (LG of 2017 and 2018, TCL, Toshiba, Vestel, Vizio, Hisense, Roku, AV receivers) got HDR10 before (`linux-9978`, and Kodi's rule).
- AMD: the tunnel on DCN 4.0.1 (Radeon RX 9000), refused under anything the display core would apply to the stream on its own (`linux-9973`, `linux-9974`); HDR in YCbCr 4:2:2 at 12 bits where RGB would be 8 bits, on DCN but not DCN 3.1 and 3.1.5, which keep 4:2:0 (`linux-9972`); RGB range on HDMI as CTA-861 says, AMD's change by Adrian Betschart that is in no Linux release yet (`linux-9970`), kept full on a TV that selects the range (`linux-9971`), and the 4K modes whose VIC goes in the HDMI vendor InfoFrame (3840x2160 at 24, 25 and 30 Hz, 4096x2160 at 24 Hz) counted as CTA modes too, which AMD's change leaves out (`linux-9983`).
- Intel display version 9 (UHD 6xx): native Dolby Vision no longer needs a connector property that i915 gives only from version 10; on such an HDMI port of 300 MHz it goes out at 2160p24, 25 and 30, and a video at 50 or 60 Hz says that the output has no mode at that rate.
- NVIDIA: the vendor InfoFrame and the HDMI properties on a dual-mode DisplayPort connector while it drives the display as HDMI (`nvidia-open-kernel-modules-9010`, `9011`), HDR at 12 bpc over HDMI on Turing where the mode fits, off by default and turned on with `nvidia-modeset.hades_turing_12bpc=1` on the kernel line (`9012`), and the depth the driver drives the output at, which the player information now shows (`9013`).
- A native Dolby Vision file opened over another at another frame rate goes on into its mode: one change of the display where there were up to seven, and none for the next file of a list at the same rate. While a change of mode waits for the video, the menus go out in the mode on the screen instead of freezing.
- NVIDIA has no wait for a vertical blank by default: a repeated picture is drawn again every refresh there, as in 1.0 RC1 (`<video><dvkeepbycommit>true</dvkeepbycommit></video>`, off until it is measured on a GPU, keeps it by another way). An output whose page flips stop coming is turned off and on again after 1, 2, 4 and up to 64 s for as long as they do not come, with its state read from the kernel.
- The audio engine is told of every modeset of native Dolby Vision; a change of mode that the driver refuses is not taken as made; the HDR state follows what the kernel took; and after a wake from suspend the mode is set again where nvidia-drm left the output off.
- The notice for a display that offers Dolby Vision Low-Latency only is one sentence, shown for the usual time of a notification, and says what to do; why is in `kodi.log` and on the Dolby Vision page of the player information.
- Add-ons are told that the display takes Dolby Vision only where native output can be had with the settings as they are, and HDR10+ only where it can go out.
- *System > Display > Monitor* shows each display by the name in its EDID, with its connector, and with its graphics where there are two ("LG TV SSCR2 (HDMI-A-2, NVIDIA)"); a choice saved before keeps working. A change to another output of the graphics in use is made at once: the driver is asked first with a commit that only tests it, the output is given the mode its display prefers and the CRTC in use where it can drive it, else a free one, with any other display taken off it, and Kodi's own question to keep the change follows (10 s; without a yes, Kodi goes back). A display of another graphics device asks whether to start Kodi again now; the start that follows asks "Keep this monitor?" with 15 s to answer, and what is not answered yes (no, back, the time running out, or a question that cannot be shown within 90 s) puts the display of before back with one more start, which asks too, and then the setting goes back to Default. The setting is greyed with one display; LibreELEC hides it, and this build shows it again. While a video or music plays, the setting keeps what it had and says to stop playback; while the output still finishes a change of its own, it says to wait a moment. Tested on the build machine (the choice of output and of CRTC); not yet run on graphics hardware.
- 3D: where the display has no 3D mode at the rate of a 30p or 25p video, the 3D mode at exactly twice that rate is used where the display has it, each picture shown twice.

**Files and streams**

- Profile 7 in two video tracks of MP4 files (the dual-track carriage of Dolby's specification) and Matroska files, also those that libavformat does not group, confirmed by their packets; in a transport stream also when its enhancement layer is a private stream found to be HEVC while it plays. The log says why two tracks are not joined.
- A HEVC stream whose packets carry an RPU without a Dolby Vision record in the container becomes Dolby Vision (profile 7 with both layers, profile 5, or profile 8 over PQ, HLG or SDR).
- At a play item of a Blu-ray that is not connected seamlessly, about 9 seconds of video (223 pictures of a test stream) were lost; now 1 picture (`ffmpeg-9928`).
- 3D Blu-ray video: three fixes to the decoder of both views, found when the findings of a review of FFmpeg pull request 24303 were checked against it, each measured against the reference decoder with streams made to show the fault.

**Audio**

- PCM goes out in no more channels than the display declares in its EDID (*System > Audio > Limit PCM channels to what the display declares*, Expert, on), also through a change of display mode, which takes the audio data of the display away for a moment.
- Where the display takes only stereo PCM but Dolby in more channels, a multichannel track that Kodi decodes goes out as Dolby Digital Plus 5.1 where the display declares it and the output takes it (192 kHz, as Kodi's own transcoding asks), else as Dolby Digital 5.1, passed through, instead of mixed down to stereo (*Surround as Dolby when the display takes only stereo PCM*, on). Where no encoder opens, stereo PCM as before. Checked on the build machine with FFmpeg: each channel in its place, the LFE kept; not yet heard through a TV.
- Kodi's own *Enable Dolby Digital transcoding* counts only where *Number of channels* is 2.0, as in Kodi: left on from a 2.0 setting and hidden at 5.1 or 7.1, it no longer encodes to Dolby there.
- An HDMI or DisplayPort audio output that is set but not there is replaced in memory by the one Kodi would take for it, before its formats are asked, so passthrough works; the setting is not changed, and is used again when that output comes back. What the sink opened is in the log.

**Player information and skins**

- A "GPU" row in the overview page, with the use and temperature that the driver gives; a row on the Dolby Vision page for the video scaling method, and one for the content mapping sent.
- Every skin gets the overview and Dolby Vision pages of Hades: a second press of the key that opens the player process information opens them, where the skin has no such pages.
- cinemaONE's two changes of Kodi pull request 29463: opaque menus stay opaque over HDR video (a white highlight at 50 % over an opaque panel came out between 356 and 588 of 1023 by the video below; now 475, the SDR reference being 476), and a small video window shows the video. One case measured further from the SDR reference than before: the edge of a white subtitle without an outline straight over the video.

**Interface**

- The mouse pointer keeps the fraction of a pixel of each motion that libinput gives, which Kodi dropped: 1000 reports of 0.4 px moved it 0 px, and now 400. Of motions that follow one another in the queue only the last is handled, with buttons, the wheel and keys kept in their place and order, and a drag moves as far as before. Host tests; not yet tried on hardware.
- Before a stream without a type is played, its server is asked for it on a thread of its own, with the menus drawing and the busy dialog after 100 ms, as Kodi already waits there for a plugin; before, the interface froze until the server answered (3.2 to 6.7 s each time a film started from a Debrid add-on in tests). The wait cannot be cancelled, as before. Compiled and read, not run: this part does not run on the build machine.
- Once a minute, when the interface was slow, a line `GUI: in the last N s, ...` in `kodi.log` says how many passes took 50 ms or more with no video playing, how long add-ons held the interface, and how many motions of the mouse were left for the one after them.

**Diagnostics and logs**

- Every file of the diagnostics goes through the filter before it is written; IP and MAC addresses, Wi-Fi names, serial numbers (also inside EDIDs), UUIDs, SSH fingerprints and the name of the PC become markers made with a secret that never leaves the PC, and the names of media go. In five diagnostics that users sent, none of these values is left, and every Dolby Vision line is.
- "Submit Log" filters a log also when it is uploaded.
- *System > Submit Log > Copy the diagnostics to a USB drive*, and a USB drive with a folder `hades-diag` at its top gets the copy when it is plugged in, read back and checked file by file; an update with the installer keeps the folder `hades-diag`.
- The diagnostics service cannot use the network.

**System**

- *System > OS Configuration > Hardware clock*: local time (the default, as before) or UTC.
- *Services > Phone remote control*: turns on what Kore or Yatse need, through Kodi's own confirmations, and shows what to enter in the app.
- *Updates*: "Available Versions" says where the versions are published. A signed update channel is built in and stays off: no image has a channel yet.
- The unused blocks of the storage partition are given back to the drive once a week (`fstrim`, idle priority, only that partition).
- Hardening: kernel options against the exploitation of memory bugs, sysctl settings (no ICMP redirects, the kernel log for root only), BusyBox no longer setuid root, the folders that hold secrets for root only, and compiler hardening on FFmpeg, zlib, sqlite, libjpeg-turbo and taglib, each measured within the noise of playback; left out where it cost (libass and its fonts, libpng, curl).
- The USB4 and Thunderbolt driver, which LibreELEC lost in 2020; PCIe tunnels stay unauthorized.
- MediaTek MT7925 Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, MT7927 Wi-Fi; button maps for Xbox and DualShock 4 controllers.
- The GBM library of NVIDIA's EGL (egl-gbm, MIT) built from its source instead of copied from NVIDIA's installer.

**Updates and security**

- Linux 7.2.12, libplacebo 285d1cc8af2a, Kodi Piers up to de8192b4200c, LibreELEC master up to fb1edf29eb07 (systemd 262.1, sqlite 3.54.1, cifs-utils 7.9, Pillow 13.0.0), Python 3.14.9, LLVM 23.1.4, kernel firmware 20261019, elfutils 0.197, ethtool 7.2, NSS 3.131.1, pycryptodome 4.1.0, setuptools 84.2.1, nano 9.3.
- The CA bundle as Mozilla's root store of 2026-10-20: three eMudhra roots are out, one of which gave a trust path to intermediates whose keys are public (CVE-2026-113812).
- Fixes taken from upstream, each read whole: FFmpeg (70), OpenSSH (4), Python (3), curl (5), BlueZ (2), libarchive (3), libtiff (3). Fixed by Hades where upstream has no fix yet: libbluray (CVE-2026-112282, CVE-2026-112283), libdisplay-info (CVE-2026-112271), libdvdnav (CVE-2026-112058).

Each change was tested on the build machine where it can be: against exact references or the code before it, with tests that fail on purpose when the code is broken; where a number above was measured another way, it says how. What has been seen on a TV is in the README, "What has been tested". Some ideas came from other builds and were written again for Hades; the README credits them.

### En español

#### Novedades

- **Los menús sobre Dolby Vision cuestan mucho menos.** Un fotograma de Dolby Vision nativo con un menú, el OSD, la barra de búsqueda o subtítulos encima ahora se hace solo donde se dibujan, con los mismos bytes enviados. Medido en la gráfica Radeon integrada de un Ryzen 9 7950X3D: 17,8 ms de GPU en vez de 30,5 con la barra de búsqueda sobre una película de perfil 7, que tiene 41,7 ms para cada fotograma. Una medición que costaba unos 40 ms por fotograma en una Radeon RX 7600, y que hacía que una película con un menú encima fuera a unos 16 fotogramas por segundo (issue #3), ahora se hace por bloques. El perfil 5, cuyos píxeles bajo un menú son los que más cuestan, tiene ahora una pasada propia para ellos: bajo la información del reproductor, unos 60 ms en vez de unos 83 en esa gráfica integrada, medido con Direct3D 12 en Windows en una máquina ocupada con otro trabajo y todavía no con el controlador de Linux, con los mismos bytes enviados.
- **Dolby Vision Low-Latency ("player-led"), como opción.** Para una entrada de la tele que ofrece Dolby Vision solo en su forma de baja latencia (el modo juego de algunas teles, o una tele que no tiene otra), Hades puede enviarlo así, adaptando él mismo la imagen a la pantalla. Apagado por defecto. La adaptación es de LibreELEC Hades, no la de Dolby.
- **HDR10+ a las pantallas que lo admiten.** Los metadatos dinámicos de cada fotograma de un vídeo HDR10+ salen ahora con ese fotograma hacia una pantalla que declara HDR10+, en gráficas NVIDIA, AMD e Intel, dentro de los límites de cada controlador. Por DisplayPort en las gráficas NVIDIA Ada (RTX 40) ahora se escribe sin la suma de comprobación que el hardware añade a los InfoFrames HDMI: antes de este cambio, un monitor que declara HDR10+ mostraba HDR10 por DisplayPort; si ahora muestra HDR10+, sin verificar, y la FAQ oficial de HDR10+ Gaming dice que en un PC va solo por HDMI. Por HDMI sale ahora también desde las RTX 40 a una pantalla que declara refresco variable por HDMI, y desde las gráficas Intel a 3840x2160 a 24, 25 y 30 Hz a una pantalla HDMI 2.0 que lista ese modo; ninguna de las dos cosas se ha visto aún en una pantalla.
- **Otros formatos enviados como Dolby Vision, como opciones (apagadas por defecto):** un vídeo HDR10+ a una tele que admite Dolby Vision pero no HDR10+, con metadatos hechos de sus propios metadatos HDR10+; y un vídeo HDR10, con metadatos que Hades mide de la imagen mientras se reproduce y que lo dicen: no son los del estudio.
- **Para proyectores:** Dolby Vision se puede enviar sin sus trims, para una pantalla que con ellos muestra negros lavados o colores equivocados (apagado por defecto).
- **HDR y Dolby Vision en una pantalla sin HDR.** HDR10, HLG y los perfiles 5, 7 y 8.1 de Dolby Vision se adaptan a SDR con la adaptación de una pantalla de referencia (ITU-R BT.2390), que ahora es el método de mapeo de tonos por defecto: medido, mucho más cerca del máster que los métodos propios de Kodi. El perfil 5 en una pantalla así ya no sale verde y morado.
- **Más archivos Dolby Vision se ven como Dolby Vision:** el perfil 7 en dos pistas de vídeo de archivos MKV y MP4 (issues #1 y #5), y los flujos HEVC que llevan Dolby Vision sin un registro de ello en su contenedor.
- **Más teles y gráficas:** teles Dolby Vision que leen la gestión de pantalla 2 o 3 (muchas de 2017 y 2018), las gráficas Radeon RX 9000 (RDNA 4), las gráficas Intel UHD 6xx, y el puerto HDMI de los portátiles cuya gráfica NVIDIA lo maneja a través de un conector DisplayPort de doble modo.
- **Mejor HDR10 en AMD y NVIDIA:** en AMD, HDR10 en YCbCr 4:2:2 a 12 bits donde RGB iría a 8 bits; en las NVIDIA GeForce GTX 16 y RTX 20, HDR por HDMI a 12 bits en vez de 8 donde el modo cabe, apagado por defecto y que se enciende con `nvidia-modeset.hades_turing_12bpc=1` en la línea del kernel.
- **Menos fotogramas negros y menos cambios de pantalla:** Dolby Vision mostrado como HDR10 conserva la última imagen entera donde una falla; el vídeo siguiente de una lista a otra frecuencia cambia la pantalla una vez; los menús siguen moviéndose mientras un cambio de pantalla espera; una salida NVIDIA cuya pantalla deja de contestar se apaga y se enciende hasta que vuelve; y al despertar de la suspensión el modo se vuelve a poner donde el controlador de NVIDIA dejó la salida apagada (un portátil se quedaba sin imagen al despertar).
- **Sonido:** PCM en no más canales de los que la tele dice que admite (a una tele que declaraba 2 canales de PCM se le podían enviar más, la causa probable de una pista DTS-HD que no sonó), también durante un cambio de modo de pantalla, con un ajuste experto para apagarlo; donde la tele solo admite PCM estéreo pero Dolby de más canales, una pista multicanal que decodifica Kodi sale como Dolby Digital Plus o Dolby Digital 5.1 en vez de estéreo (un ajuste, activado); y una salida de audio HDMI configurada que no está ya no deja apagado el paso directo. La codificación a Dolby todavía no se ha oído en una tele.
- **El ratón y los menús:** el puntero conserva las fracciones de píxel de cada movimiento (un movimiento lento lo movía menos de lo debido, o nada), y los movimientos que se acumulan se atienden como uno; cuando una película empieza desde un complemento, los menús siguen dibujándose, con el indicador de espera, mientras se pregunta al servidor qué es el flujo (se quedaban quietos de 3,2 a 6,7 s en las pruebas); y `kodi.log` dice una vez por minuto cuándo la interfaz fue lenta. Todavía sin probar en hardware.
- **Cambiar de monitor:** el ajuste Monitor de *Sistema > Pantalla*, que LibreELEC oculta, vuelve a verse, con el nombre de cada pantalla, y en gris con una sola pantalla. Un cambio a otra pantalla de la misma gráfica lleva Kodi allí al momento, con la pregunta de Kodi para mantenerlo; una pantalla de otra gráfica reinicia Kodi, y el arranque pregunta si mantenerla, y vuelve a la pantalla de antes donde no se mantiene. No mientras algo se reproduce. Todavía sin probar en hardware.
- **Un diagnóstico que se puede compartir:** las direcciones, los números de serie, los nombres de red y los nombres de las películas pasan a ser marcas o desaparecen, en todos los archivos; el diagnóstico se puede copiar a una unidad USB, también sin nada en pantalla, y una actualización con el instalador lo conserva.
- **Más cómodo:** un mando a distancia en el móvil en un paso, un ajuste para un PC cuyo reloj va en UTC, un recorte semanal de la partición de almacenamiento, la información del reproductor de Hades en cualquier interfaz con el uso y la temperatura de la GPU, y un endurecimiento que se midió sin coste en la reproducción.
- **Al día y más seguro:** LibreELEC y Kodi en sus últimos commits, libplacebo, LLVM, systemd y el firmware del kernel, y arreglos de fallos de seguridad conocidos en FFmpeg, OpenSSH, Python, curl, BlueZ, libarchive y libtiff, con cuatro arreglos de Hades para libbluray, libdisplay-info y libdvdnav.

**Imagen y metadatos Dolby Vision**

- La curva PQ de la decodificación Dolby Vision se calcula sin cancelación (`libplacebo-9942`): una imagen de perfil 5 reconstruida como HDR10 tenía bandas de hasta 315 códigos de 4095 en el borde de BT.2020; ahora 39 como mucho, medido en una carta.
- La adaptación a una pantalla (SDR, proyectores, Low-Latency) va al pico de la pantalla, no a un pico virtual por encima que oscurecía toda la imagen entre un 2 y un 11 % (`libplacebo-9943`): medido en 17 imágenes, el percentil 99 del error más bajo en los 45 mapeos, de hasta 7,60 a 1,35 Delta E ITP, con las altas luces a menos de 1. Su tabla de gama es de 96x64x512 en vez de 48x32x256: el error más grande en SDR, 1,10 en vez de 2,22.
- Los menús o subtítulos sobre las bandas ya no marcan una escena nueva en los metadatos (ETSI GS CCM 001, 6.2.2): un cambio de la zona activa lleva en su lugar un identificador de metadatos nuevo, y una banda sigue dentro de la zona 2 s de vídeo después de que se vaya lo dibujado encima (un minuto de subtítulos en una banda: 2 cambios en vez de 32).
- A una pantalla de gestión de pantalla 2 o 3 se le envía la parte de mapeo de contenido v2.9 de los metadatos, la que lee; los bloques de mapeo de contenido v4.0, que no lee, se quedan fuera.
- Una imagen que sale sin su imagen de mejora (tras un salto, o tarde) lleva el RPU de su propia unidad de acceso, no el de una vecina.
- La capa de mejora arranca por orden de imagen en un punto de acceso aleatorio propio donde las dos capas no comparten ninguno; con la capa base reordenada, antes no arrancaba nunca (medido con un flujo hecho para ello). Un título de perfil 7 cuya capa no arrancó nunca en una gráfica AMD integrada (253 unidades de mejora saltadas en 10 s) pudo ser un flujo así: sin verificar. Un contexto VAAPI nuevo de la misma pantalla ya no la reinicia, y un flujo MEL pierde un residuo una vez como mucho.
- Dolby Vision mostrado como HDR10 dibuja en una segunda textura y muestra una imagen solo cuando está entera (132 MB más a 3840x2160); donde no se puede mostrar nada reconstruido, se muestra la capa base cuando es una imagen por sí misma. Su copia de 8 bits en rango limitado tiene el blanco en 235, no en 234.
- El método de escalado de vídeo de Kodi no lo usa el motor Dolby Vision, que remuestrea como dice la especificación; la página Dolby Vision de la información del reproductor ahora lo dice.

**Rendimiento**

- Un fotograma con elementos de la interfaz sobre Dolby Vision nativo se hace solo en las teselas de 16x16 píxeles a las que llega la interfaz, con los mismos bytes. Medido con el OpenGL de Mesa sobre Direct3D 12 en Windows, todavía no con los controladores de Linux: en la gráfica Radeon integrada de un Ryzen 9 7950X3D, el fotograma que espera a su refresco (uno por adelantado) con dos líneas de subtítulos 17,7 ms (16,5) en vez de 29,8 (24,2), con un OSD 28,5 (21,6) en vez de 42,6 (25,9), y 13,3 ms sin interfaz; en una RTX 4080 SUPER, de 1,4 a 2,7 ms en vez de 1,5 a 9,3. `<video><dvdrawntiles>false</dvdrawntiles></video>` lo apaga, para medirlo.
- Los bordes dibujados sobre las bandas se miden por bloques, leyendo cada píxel una vez: en esa gráfica integrada, 5,5 ms en vez de 19,6 bajo una pantalla atenuada.
- Fotogramas de perfil 5 con la interfaz encima: la imagen bajo un píxel dibujado encima se decodifica una vez en vez de dos, y una pasada propia convierte cada par de esos píxeles una vez antes de que los tome el transporte, tres conversiones donde había cuatro. En la gráfica Radeon integrada de arriba, con Direct3D 12 en Windows y en una máquina ocupada con otro trabajo, así que las cifras son aproximadas, un fotograma de perfil 5 bajo la información del reproductor tarda unos 60 ms en vez de unos 83, una ventana sobre una pantalla atenuada unos 74 en vez de unos 101, el OSD unos 30 en vez de unos 38; con radeonsi, el controlador de Linux, todavía sin medir. Los perfiles 7 y 8.1 no usan la pasada. Los mismos bytes en 162 fotogramas con la interfaz encima, en un renderizador por software y en las dos GPU de ese PC. Donde una GPU no puede hacer la pasada, el transporte convierte él mismo esos píxeles como antes (los mismos bytes, más tiempo de GPU) y `kodi.log` lo dice.
- La presentación un fotograma por adelantado no la encienden ni la apagan un salto ni unos menús que acaban de aparecer.
- La reducción de fidelidad guarda un nivel para cada juego de niveles que puede tener un formato, así que lo encontrado se recuerda también con el transporte por defecto, cosa que antes no pasaba: en un modelo de una GPU lenta, 26 refrescos perdidos al empezar un vídeo en vez de 102.

**Pantalla y HDMI**

- Dolby Vision Low-Latency (`videoscreen.hadesdvlowlatency`, apagado): solo en una pantalla que ofrece Low-Latency y no Standard. La imagen se adapta con la curva BT.2390 guiada por los metadatos de nivel 1 de cada escena, al pico y a los primarios del bloque Dolby Vision de la pantalla, y se envía como YCbCr 4:2:2 a 12 bits, BT.2020, rango limitado, por el túnel, con el InfoFrame del fabricante de baja latencia (`linux-9975` a `9977` para amdgpu, i915 y xe; `nvidia-open-kernel-modules-9030`). `<video><dvllpattern>true</dvllpattern></video>` muestra un patrón de prueba en lugar de la imagen, para un primer vistazo a una pantalla.
- HDR10+: una propiedad de DRM para el InfoFrame del fabricante de cada fotograma (`linux-9980`) y sus implementaciones (`linux-9981` amdgpu, `linux-9982` i915 y xe, `nvidia-open-kernel-modules-9020`), enviado con el commit del fotograma y nunca con un cambio de modo; menos de 12 imágenes sin metadatos conservan el último InfoFrame, y 12 vuelven a HDR10. El indicador de gráficos sobre el vídeo se pone mientras la interfaz de Kodi está encima.
- HDR10+ por DisplayPort en NVIDIA Ada: el SDP del InfoFrame se escribe en su ranura sin la suma de comprobación del hardware (`nvidia-open-kernel-modules-9020`). Esa suma es la de un InfoFrame HDMI, y en un SDP ocuparía el lugar del byte de versión; los bytes del SDP no cambian. Antes de este cambio, un monitor que declara HDR10+ mostraba HDR10 por DisplayPort desde una RTX 4080 SUPER, con el SDP escrito en cada una de cinco películas; si la suma era la causa, y si ahora muestra HDR10+, sin verificar. Una prueba de anfitrión pasa los paquetes por los propios escritores de paquetes de NVIDIA (24 comprobaciones). La vía HDMI no cambia. La FAQ oficial de HDR10+ Gaming dice que en un PC HDR10+ va solo por HDMI.
- HDR10+ por HDMI en NVIDIA a una pantalla que declara refresco variable por HDMI: el controlador envía sus datos de refresco variable en la ranura genérica de InfoFrame en cada fotograma a una pantalla así (a 50 Hz y 720 líneas o más), usen o no el refresco variable los cambios de página, y ahí HDR10+ se rechazaba (visto desde una RTX 4080 SUPER a un monitor que declara HDR10+ y VRR por HDMI). En Ada va ahora en la ranura genérica compartida 2, en cada fotograma, con su propia suma de comprobación; las GTX 16, RTX 20 y RTX 30, que no tienen otra ranura, siguen sin enviarlo. Que esa ranura llegue a una pantalla HDMI no lo documenta NVIDIA y no está verificado. Una prueba de anfitrión pasa los paquetes por los propios escritores de paquetes de NVIDIA (49 comprobaciones). Un rechazo dice por qué en el registro del kernel (`Hades: HDR10+ refused on head N: ...`), y `kodi.log` dice que el controlador rechazó los metadatos en la prueba de sus commits.
- HDR10+ por HDMI en Intel a 3840x2160 a 24, 25 y 30 Hz y 4096x2160 a 24 Hz (y sus frecuencias de 1000/1001), que DRM señala con el VIC de HDMI en el InfoFrame del fabricante que HDR10+ también necesita: a una pantalla HDMI 2.0 que lista el VIC CTA del modo y declara HDR10+, el InfoFrame AVI lleva ese VIC, como señala esos modos el controlador de NVIDIA, y el InfoFrame del fabricante lleva HDR10+ (`linux-9982`); el cambio de modo del vídeo lleva los datos HDR10+ de su primer fotograma. Una película a 23,976 Hz desde un Ice Lake a una tele HDR10+ (issue #2) salía como HDR10. Todo lo demás sale como antes, y un rechazo dice por qué en el registro del kernel. Prueba de anfitrión con el empaquetador de InfoFrames del propio kernel y los EDID de tres pantallas (33 comprobaciones); todavía sin ver en una pantalla.
- HDR10+ como Dolby Vision (`videoscreen.hadesdvfromhdr10plus`, apagado): metadatos de perfil 8.1 escena a escena a partir de los metadatos HDR10+, con las reglas y los valores por defecto de dovi_tool y hdr10plus_tool; medido en 166 imágenes contra el nivel 1 calculado de las imágenes, la media se desvía 11 códigos de media (la regla de dovi_tool: 112 de más).
- HDR10 como Dolby Vision, medido (`videoscreen.hadesdvfromhdr10`, apagado): el nivel 1 y la zona activa medidos en la GPU sobre las imágenes que Kodi ya ha decodificado por delante, los cortes de escena encontrados en la imagen y marcados como tales, sin trims ni nivel 3. Medido en dos películas abiertas contra los cortes de escena de sus metadatos Dolby Vision: 96 de 99 encontrados en su imagen en aquella con la que se ajustó la regla, 42 de 51 en la otra, y también cortes encontrados donde no los hay.
- Sin trims (`videoscreen.hadesdvnotrims`, apagado): no se envían bloques de nivel 2, 8 ni 10; la imagen no cambia.
- Teles Dolby Vision cuyo bloque de versión 1 de 12 bytes dice gestión de pantalla 2 o 3: 119 de 584 EDID públicos con bloque Dolby (LG de 2017 y 2018, TCL, Toshiba, Vestel, Vizio, Hisense, Roku, receptores AV) recibían antes HDR10 (`linux-9978`, y la regla de Kodi).
- AMD: el túnel en DCN 4.0.1 (Radeon RX 9000), rechazado bajo cualquier cosa que el núcleo de pantalla aplicaría por su cuenta al flujo (`linux-9973`, `linux-9974`); HDR en YCbCr 4:2:2 a 12 bits donde RGB iría a 8 bits, en DCN salvo DCN 3.1 y 3.1.5, que siguen en 4:2:0 (`linux-9972`); el rango RGB por HDMI como dice CTA-861, el cambio de AMD de Adrian Betschart que aún no está en ninguna versión de Linux (`linux-9970`), con rango completo en una tele que elige el rango (`linux-9971`), y los modos 4K cuyo VIC va en el InfoFrame del fabricante de HDMI (3840x2160 a 24, 25 y 30 Hz, 4096x2160 a 24 Hz) contados también como modos CTA, que el cambio de AMD deja fuera (`linux-9983`).

- Un archivo de Dolby Vision nativo abierto sobre otro a otra frecuencia sigue en su modo: un cambio de pantalla donde había hasta siete, y ninguno para el archivo siguiente de una lista a la misma frecuencia. Mientras un cambio de modo espera al vídeo, los menús salen en el modo que hay en pantalla en vez de quedarse quietos.

Una salida cuyos cambios de página dejan de llegar se apaga y se enciende tras 1, 2, 4 y hasta 64 s mientras no lleguen, con su estado leído del kernel.
- Al motor de audio se le avisa de cada cambio de modo del Dolby Vision nativo; un cambio de modo que el controlador rechaza no se da por hecho; el estado HDR sigue a lo que tomó el kernel; y al despertar de la suspensión el modo se vuelve a poner donde nvidia-drm dejó la salida apagada.
- El aviso para una pantalla que solo ofrece Dolby Vision Low-Latency es de una frase, se muestra el tiempo habitual de un aviso, y dice qué hacer; el porqué está en `kodi.log` y en la página Dolby Vision de la información del reproductor.

Un cambio a otra salida de la gráfica en uso se hace al momento: antes se le pregunta al controlador con un commit que solo comprueba, la salida recibe el modo que prefiere su pantalla y el CRTC en uso donde puede manejarla, si no uno libre, sin ninguna otra pantalla en él, y sigue la pregunta del propio Kodi para mantener el cambio (10 s; sin un sí, Kodi vuelve). Probado en la máquina de compilación (la elección de salida y de CRTC); todavía sin ejecutar en una gráfica.
- 3D: donde la pantalla no tiene modo 3D a la frecuencia de un vídeo de 30p o 25p, se usa el modo 3D a exactamente el doble donde la pantalla lo tiene, y cada imagen se muestra dos veces.

**Diagnóstico y registros**

- Cada archivo del diagnóstico pasa por el filtro antes de escribirse; las direcciones IP y MAC, los nombres de las redes Wi-Fi, los números de serie (también dentro de los EDID), los UUID, las huellas SSH y el nombre del PC pasan a ser marcas hechas con un secreto que nunca sale del PC, y los nombres de los medios desaparecen. - *Sistema > Enviar registro > Copiar el diagnóstico a una unidad USB*, y una unidad USB con una carpeta `hades-diag` en su raíz recibe la copia al enchufarla, releída y comprobada archivo por archivo; una actualización con el instalador conserva la carpeta `hades-diag`.

## 1.0 RC2

### What's new for you

- **Native Dolby Vision, rebuilt exactly as the specification defines it.** Real Dolby Vision, handled by the TV itself as always; the picture sent to it is now rebuilt exactly as the published Dolby Vision specification describes it, and sent the way certified players send it. Colours, edges and bright highlights are more faithful, for every profile (5, 7 FEL, 7 MEL and 8.1).
- **Smoother on modest graphics.** Less work for the GPU on every frame, a repeated picture is not drawn again, and only on graphics that cannot keep up does the picture lose a tiny, measured amount of precision, and only as much as needed. Strong graphics stay bit-exact.
- **Menus at 120 Hz just work.** Starting and stopping Dolby Vision, HDR10 and HLG is a single display change, also with the menus at 119.88 or 120 Hz, and on NVIDIA, AMD and Intel.
- **Turn the TV on after the PC.** The TV is looked at again when each video starts, so Dolby Vision works even if the PC booted with the TV off.
- **New "Overview" page** in the player information: what is really sent to the TV (signal, mode, colour depth, the metadata actually transmitted), the video, the audio and the system, at a glance.
- **Projectors and TVs without Dolby Vision:** Dolby Vision can be mapped to the brightness of your display (off by default), and the Dolby Vision output can be chosen for each video.
- **Older AMD graphics:** native Dolby Vision now also on Raven, Picasso, Navi 1x, Renoir and Cezanne.
- **HDR10 at 10 bits** on Intel and AMD where the cable could only carry 8 bits before.
- **Live TV:** audio passthrough (Atmos included) on live channels, 10-bit channels that showed no picture, faster channel changes.
- **Profile 7 MEL** no longer decodes its empty enhancement layer: less work, the same picture.
- **Up to date and safer:** Linux 7.2.11, Kodi and LibreELEC at their latest commits, and fixes for known security issues in glibc, FFmpeg, curl, libnfs, libass and more.

### Details

**Dolby Vision picture**

- The reconstruction follows the normative composer of ETSI GS CCM 001: identical bit for bit to an integer reference of the specification in every case tested. libplacebo's reshaping, which every free Dolby Vision player uses and which departs from the specification by 4 to 15 codes on most pixels (and by hundreds at strong colour edges), stays available as an expert setting.
- Sent the way a certified source device sends it: the composer's output in the colour representation of its RPU (IPT for profile 5), with the RPU's own matrices in the DM metadata; menus and subtitles are blended into it, and pixels they do not touch are sent exactly as composed. An expert setting sends it converted to BT.2020, as 1.0 RC1 did.
- Chroma of both layers at the position the stream signals; 4:2:2 taken from the even pixel; exact Lanczos weights for whole-ratio scaling; very bright pixels that came out black are fixed; exact BT.2020, P3 and BT.709 matrices.
- Level 5 describes what is sent, also when menus or subtitles are drawn over the bars; metadata up to 512 bytes; level 11 checked as dovi_tool checks it; scene refresh after a gap.
- Profile 7 MEL: the enhancement layer, which carries no residual, is not decoded; the picture is the same, byte for byte. FEL always is.

**Performance**

- Automatic fidelity step-down only where frames are actually missed (measured per format, remembered per GPU and driver); it can be turned off.
- Repeated pictures are not drawn again; shader cache on disk; GPU programs prepared before the first frame; fewer imports per frame.
- A switch to check on the GPU that frames send the bytes they claim (`<dvverify>` in advancedsettings.xml).

**Display and HDMI**

- One display change at the start and end of Dolby Vision, HDR10 and HLG on all drivers.
- The TV's EDID is read again at each video start (a TV switched on after boot, Deep Colour switched on later, another TV).
- A frame whose page flip never comes no longer stalls playback.
- HDR10 in YCbCr 4:2:0 at 10 bits where RGB would be 8 bits (Intel, AMD); Intel's BT.2020 YCbCr matrix.
- The reason when native Dolby Vision is not used, as the graphics driver reports it; native Dolby Vision on AMD DCN 1.0 and 2.x.

**Settings and audio**

- Dolby Vision engine settings, Automatic by default; per-video Dolby Vision output; Dolby Vision as HDR10 mapped to the display.
- Passthrough on live TV and streams (a setting, on by default); DRM PRIME hands back what it cannot decode.

**Updates and security**

- Linux 7.2.11, libplacebo 804dc71cd38d, Kodi Piers up to 01d4146d5d25, LibreELEC master up to 3ca0d49bee40.
- Fixes taken from upstream, each read whole: glibc 2.44 (nine CVEs), FFmpeg (about ninety), libnfs (CVE-2026-108985), libass (CVE-2026-95127), curl, libbluray, libdvdread, libdvdnav, libarchive, Samba, BusyBox; BlueZ CVE-2026-105460 fixed by Hades.

Every change was measured on a software renderer (Mesa) against exact references, with tests that fail on purpose when the code is broken. What has been seen on a TV is in the README, "What has been tested". Some ideas came from other Dolby Vision builds and were written again for Hades; the README credits them.
