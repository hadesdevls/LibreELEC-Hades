# Changelog

The versions of LibreELEC Hades. Before 1.0 RC1 there were only private test builds.

Patch numbers are those of the version in whose section they appear. In 1.0 the patches of Hades were numbered again in the order in which they apply, so a number in an older section may name another patch today.

## 1.0

The first version that is not a release candidate. It makes Hades easier to set up and to understand: a check of your setup that plays test pictures and sounds and says in plain words what reached the TV, a diagnostic report made with one button, and a player information page that says in one sentence whether a video plays as intended. It fixes what was reported after 1.0 RC8, keeps picture and sound in step, sends Dolby Vision as the composer makes it by default, maps HDR to displays more faithfully, plays DVD films as films, keeps a stream going through a network drop, and brings every part up to date. Every change was tested on the host, with the code that the image runs, or in a virtual machine. What is marked "not tried on hardware" has not been seen on a real screen, and the README lists it under [Not tried on hardware yet](README.md#not-tried-on-hardware-yet).

The documentation is rewritten for readers: the README, the [extras](docs/features.md), the [settings guide](docs/settings.md) and [how it works](docs/how-it-works.md). It is in English only. The Spanish README and NOTICE are retired; the Windows installer and the menus of Hades keep their Spanish.

### What's new for you

**Easier to use**

- **Check this setup** (*Settings > LibreELEC > System > Check this setup*). It plays short test clips that the build of Hades makes itself: a test picture in SDR, HDR10, HLG and Dolby Vision (profiles 8.1 and 5), a soft noise from each speaker in turn, and a chime in each sound format that your receiver or TV may take as it is (Dolby Digital, Dolby Digital Plus and Dolby TrueHD). After each one it says what went out and how (the display mode, the HDR signal, whether Dolby Vision went out natively and why not, the sound format), asks what you saw or heard, and says what to do if something was wrong. It switches on only the sound formats that you heard; nothing else is changed. DTS cannot be checked this way and is left as it is. No material of anyone else is in the clips. Tried in a virtual machine; not tried on hardware.
- **A diagnostic report in one file** (*Settings > LibreELEC > System > Submit Log > Create a diagnostic report*). One button makes one zip file with what is needed to find out why something does not work: the logs of Kodi and of the system, the graphics and the displays, the sound, the network, the USB devices, the journal of this start and of the one before it, the newest crash log of Kodi, how long the start took, and what the diagnostics saved at the last starts. Every file goes through the filter of the diagnostics first, and nothing is sent anywhere. The file stays in `/storage/logfiles` and can be copied at once to the top of a USB drive. Tried in a virtual machine; not tried on hardware.
- **The diagnostic switch is a setting** (*Settings > LibreELEC > System > Submit Log > Save diagnostics at every start*), on a USB drive and on an internal disk alike, so the Windows installer is no longer the only way to turn it on. When it is turned on it says what it saves, where, how much and through which filter. It works from the next start. Tried in a virtual machine; not tried on hardware.
- **A player information page that a person can read.** Press O, or the red key of a TV remote that sends keys over HDMI-CEC (the red key also closes it). The first page is one panel: a sentence that says whether the video plays as intended and, if not, what is wrong and what to do; what the file carries; what Hades does with it; and what reaches your TV. The details are behind it, with every level of the Dolby Vision metadata (L1 to L6, L8 to L11, L254 and L255) and which of them are used, why HDR is not told to the display, the colour of the film itself (profile 5 is named IPT-PQ-c2), the pictures dropped and skipped, why the sound is decoded where a setting is the reason, and the decoder as "Graphics card (VAAPI)" or "Processor (software)". A stream that does not say it is HLG or HDR10 is named by its picture (an HLG stream of YouTube through an add-on, found by vertox; after an idea of CroqueMr's build). Where the display has no mode at the rate of the video although *Adjust display refresh rate* is on, the advice says that, instead of asking you to turn on a setting that is on already. Its values move twice a second, so opening it over a Dolby Vision picture no longer redraws the whole interface for the values that change with every packet. The same pages are made for other skins, with their words in every language. Not tried on hardware.
- **The settings are in groups, with help in plain words.** *System > Display* has four groups with a heading each (HDR; Dolby Vision; HDR on displays without Dolby Vision, and projectors; HDR colour depth and format). Most of the settings that Hades adds are now at the Expert level, so the Advanced level shows only what is meant for everyone. Their help says what changes on the screen or in the sound, when to want it and what to do if the picture goes wrong; labels and messages no longer use internal names. The Dolby Vision settings that are about a display that takes Dolby Vision are shown only where the display declares it. Not tried on hardware.
- **Samba tells you how to get in.** Turning Samba on, in the first-run wizard or in *Settings > LibreELEC > Services*, shows a window with the address, the user (`libreelec`) and the generated password, says where to read them again and how to open the share from File Explorer, and says that the same login copies an update file to the Update share. The README explains updating with a `.tar` step by step, with Samba and SSH. Not tried on hardware.
- **The boot menu waits 5 seconds, and shows only once after an update.** The menu now waits 5 seconds before it starts the system by itself (10 before), as LibreELEC's BIOS menu does, and *Settings > LibreELEC > System > Boot menu* offers 10, 5, 3 and 1 second. Updating to 1.0 brings a menu that waited the old 10 seconds to 5, once; a wait that you had set to something else is kept, and from 1.0 on every update keeps the wait you choose, also an update with the Windows installer. An update restarts the PC by itself, and the PC showed the boot menu, with its wait, before the update was applied and again after the restart that ends it: the restart that applies an update now goes straight on (on a BIOS PC, holding Shift still shows the menu, which then waits as usual), and the menu is back from the next start. Tried in virtual machines, with UEFI and with BIOS; not tried on hardware.
- **A display connected while Hades runs is taken without a restart** on the same graphics card: the menu stays where you were and music keeps playing (Hades asks first, and goes there by itself after a few seconds without an answer). When another display takes the place of the one in use, its HDR, Dolby Vision, 3D and sound are known at once, not from the next video. A display on another graphics card still needs a restart. Tried in a virtual machine; not tried on hardware.
- **A notice of the playback mode, off by default.** *System > Display > Show the playback mode when a video starts* says, a moment after each video starts (the next one of a playlist too), what it plays as: for example "Dolby Vision, FEL, 3840x2160 at 23.976 Hz". Not tried on hardware.
- **"Restart LibreELEC Hades" in the power menu.** Quit is hidden on LibreELEC, so the only way to restart the application was to restart the PC.
- **Clearer wording where Dolby Vision is not sent.** The notice that a display takes Dolby Vision only as Low-Latency no longer blames the game mode: some displays offer it only that way whatever the input, and for them turning the game mode off does nothing. It now says the fact and what to do (turn on *Dolby Vision for TVs in game mode (Low-Latency)*). A TV behind a DisplayPort or USB-C converter is told to use an HDMI output of the graphics instead. *Video scaling method* in the video settings now says that Dolby Vision output does not use it: the bridge scales with libplacebo's filters.
- **Nameservers of your own, also with DHCP** (*Settings > LibreELEC > Connections*, a connection, IPv4 or IPv6). Empty means the ones the network gives, and the fields show only what you set; saving no longer turns the network's nameservers into a fixed setting, and a connection no longer shows the lists of the one opened before. After an idea of damnms for CoreELEC. Not tried on hardware.

**Dolby Vision**

- **Sent as composed by default.** Native Dolby Vision of profiles 7 and 8 now goes to the TV as the composer of the specification makes it, in the video's own YCbCr, bit for bit. Measured over the whole colour of every pixel, on pictures of two profile 7 films and on test patterns, it was as close to the film as IPT-PQ-c2 or closer however a TV fills in the colour between the pixels of a pair; IPT gave the right pixel of a pair a colour worked out for the left one, up to 13 to 19 delta E ITP on the edges of neon lights. Sending IPT-PQ-c2, as licensed players are reported to, stays as an expert choice (*Transport*, "As licensed players send (IPT)"). A setting left at the old default takes the new one. Not tried on a TV.
- **Films whose metadata repeat a pivot play in Dolby Vision.** The Dolby Vision specification makes each pivot of a reshaping curve the one before it plus a value that may be 0. The picture check refused such metadata, so every picture of the film was refused and, after two seconds, native output gave way to HDR10. It now accepts them, as the specification does; pivots that go down are still refused. This is the cause of the Transformers films dropping to HDR10 in the reports. Tested against the specification and the metadata of the sample. Not tried on hardware.
- **The TV's Dolby Vision block is no longer lost on its way from the TV.** On NVIDIA, an EDID whose extra blocks are counted by an HDMI Forum EEODB now reaches Kodi whole: the driver kept only the blocks of the base count, and a Dolby Vision block among the others was lost. On AMD and Intel, one damaged block in such an EDID no longer takes the other extra blocks out with it. The player information now tells "this TV input offers no Dolby Vision" (some TVs leave it out in their 4K 120 Hz or VRR input mode) from "the TV's EDID was read only in part", and the log names the blocks that are missing (issue #7). Checked with NVIDIA's EDID code and the kernel's tests; not tried on hardware.
- **Native Dolby Vision on the newest Ryzen processors** (display hardware DCN 4.2 and 4.2B). Each time Dolby Vision starts, the kernel reads every stage of the display pipe back from the hardware; if anything is not as Dolby Vision needs, that same video goes on as HDR10 within about two seconds (the picture holds still meanwhile), and Dolby Vision stays off until the next start. The same now holds for the Radeon RX 400, RX 500 and Vega cards. Not tried on hardware.
- **Dolby Vision through a passive DisplayPort adaptor** (DP++) that identifies itself as HDMI, on Intel and AMD graphics: such an adaptor makes the port send HDMI itself, so the TV gets what an HDMI port sends. The adaptor's own limit is kept: a type 1 adaptor carries 1080p, a type 2 one up to 3840x2160 at 30 Hz. An adaptor that says DVI, or nothing, gets no Dolby Vision. NVIDIA already sent Dolby Vision through such a port. Not tried with an adaptor.
- **The output is given back as it was found, and checked.** Native Dolby Vision changes properties of the output (its depth, range, colour format, HDR data and more). When it ends, Hades now puts back each property that is not as it found it, reads the output back and sets again what did not take, and the range and colour format are those that the settings ask for at that moment, also when you changed them during the video. A setting that the driver will not take back in that mode no longer keeps the menus from coming back. If Kodi stops during Dolby Vision, the next start puts back what the transport left. Not tried on hardware.
- **Check the Dolby Vision output** (*Settings > LibreELEC > System > Submit Log*). A button that measures whether the graphics card sends the pictures of Dolby Vision exactly as Hades draws them, with the card's own check of what leaves it (a CRC). Kodi closes for about twenty seconds, the TV may show a black or noisy picture in Dolby Vision meanwhile, and Kodi comes back with the result in plain words. No setting is changed. It is meant for AMD and Intel graphics; NVIDIA's driver does not offer the measure. With the Dolby Vision path of the Radeon RX 400, RX 500 and Vega cards turned on, the diagnostics also keep that measure of 12 frames during each Dolby Vision video. Tried with the virtual display of the kernel; not tried on hardware.
- **The Dolby Vision path of the Radeon RX 400 and 500 (Polaris) and Vega cards is off unless you turn it on** (issue #20: an RX 550 with an LG G3 showed a black picture with the Dolby Vision logo). It is an expert setting now, *System > Display > Dolby Vision on this Radeon (experimental)*, shown only on those cards; Hades writes it into the boot menus and asks whether to restart. Without it, Dolby Vision plays as HDR10 there, and the log says why. The cause on that card is not found. Not tried on hardware.
- **A failure of native output no longer takes Dolby Vision out of the supported HDR types.** After a video that fell back to HDR10, add-ons were told that the display had lost Dolby Vision (forum post 209039).
- **The log says why a frame is not drawn.** "Render rejected" used to name the stage whatever the check; it now names the rule. The line that gives up the direct way of drawing, the case where the interface cannot be put over the picture, and the layers that could not be imported say why, and the 10-second lines count them. An interlaced or 3D Dolby Vision video, which plays from its base layer, now says so in the log, and so does a mode with no Dolby Vision mode at its refresh rate, by its rates.
- **Videos with Dolby Vision and HDR10+ in two video streams** of one picture play the stream that *Videos with both Dolby Vision and HDR10+* chooses, and on a display that takes only one of them, that one. Before, the format that played depended on the order of the tracks. Not tried on hardware.
- **HLG sent as Dolby Vision** (*System > Display > HLG as Dolby Vision*). Automatic by default: only where the output cannot send HLG at all but the display takes Dolby Vision (as the HDMI of some Intel graphics), so that the video keeps its HDR instead of being mapped to SDR. On: wherever the display takes Dolby Vision. Off: HLG always plays as HLG. The picture is converted to PQ as ITU-R BT.2100 shows an HLG picture on its reference display, and the metadata are measured from it. It works in Low-Latency too, and reads the SEI message that says a stream is HLG. Not tried on hardware.
- **The trims of Dolby Vision where Hades maps the picture, as an expert choice, off by default** (*Dolby Vision: apply the trims when adjusting to your display*). Where Hades maps Dolby Vision to the display itself (Dolby Vision as HDR10 adjusted to the display, as SDR, or in Low-Latency), it can apply the level 2 trims made for exactly the peak of your display. The only published way to apply them is an example in SMPTE ST 2094-10; measured against a studio's own version of a film for a 1000-nit display, it made the shadows much darker than the studio did, so it stays off. Native Dolby Vision is not affected: the TV applies the trims itself. Not tried on hardware.
- **HDR to SDR for the display you have** (*HDR to SDR: the display it is made for*, which was named for Dolby Vision only, though it always applied to HDR10, HDR10+ and HLG mapped to SDR as well): Reference (the same output as before), Bright room (white at 300 cd/m² with gamma 2.2), Projector (white at 100 cd/m²) and Custom, with the white (20 to 500 cd/m²) and the gamma (1.8 to 2.8) that you set. HLG mapped to an SDR display brighter than the reference no longer comes out about half as bright in the middle tones. Not tried on hardware.
- **The black of the display** (*Black of the display*, Expert) for *Adapt Dolby Vision to my display (projectors)* and, while that is on, for *HDR10 and HDR10+ adapted to the display*: the darkest parts of the film are lifted as ITU-R BT.2390 describes, so that they do not sink into the black of a projector. 0, the default, keeps the black of the film. After an idea of CroqueMr's build. Not tried on hardware.
- **The peak of the TV by hand for the automatic tone mapping (v4.0)** (*Tone mapping v4.0: set the TV's peak by hand*, Expert), for a TV that declares a wrong peak in its EDID. After an idea of YBLOD. Not tried on hardware.
- **Dolby Vision pictures up to 4096x2304** are taken in and scaled down to the 3840x2160 output. Before, a 4096-wide video entered Dolby Vision, every picture was refused, and a profile 5 picture came out as its base layer in the wrong colours. A larger one plays as Kodi plays it, with a line in the log. Tried on a made-up sample. Not tried on hardware.
- **The menus in Dolby Vision cannot lock you out** (issue #20). Turning on *Dolby Vision for the menus* asks you to confirm, with a 15-second countdown whose default is No, and goes back to Off unless you answer Yes. Not tried on hardware.
- **Dolby Vision through the DisplayPort-to-HDMI converter of a board** (*Dolby Vision through a display converter*, Expert). The HDMI socket of many mini PCs and NUCs with Intel graphics of the 11th generation and later is behind such a converter, which Linux calls a DisplayPort output, and native output was refused there. Automatic offers it only on the boards where it was seen working (one today); On tries it on any converter; Off never. If Kodi stops during Dolby Vision, the console takes such an output back without the Dolby Vision signal. The idea and the first board are YBLOD's. Not tried on hardware.
- **With NVIDIA's driver 620, Dolby Vision always goes out at 8 bits in RGB.** Driver 620 has its own settings of the depth and the colour format, which another program, or *Colour format*, may leave at another value. Native Dolby Vision now sets them to automatic while it plays and puts them back after, and the driver refuses a Dolby Vision signal at any other depth or format. Not tried on hardware.
- **For add-ons: `Player.Process(video.sidedata)`,** the Dolby Vision and HDR payloads of the picture on screen, in the format and with the name that the CoreELEC builds use, so that add-ons that parse the RPU work. Nothing is collected until something reads it. `Player.Process(videooutputhdrtype)` and the new output transfer function (`videooutputeotf`, which vertox asked for) now say what the graphics driver holds, not what Kodi asked for, and are empty where the driver has no property. Tried on the host only.
- **What the conversion of a video made is given back when the video ends.** After a Dolby Vision video sent as HDR10 or HDR10+, or an HDR10 video adapted to the display, the menus went on with the conversion engaged and the graphics chip in the profile of the video. They are now released as soon as the output of the video has gone and no other video has begun.
- **The file of GPU programs no longer depends on the version of the system.** An update that changed neither the driver nor libplacebo sent the first video after it to compile every program again. The log now says how many were compiled.

**HDR and the signal to the TV**

- **HDR on a display without HDR keeps its middle tones as mastered.** The reference tone mapping, "Reference (BT.2390)", now starts compressing where ITU-R BT.2390 says: everything up to a little below the display's white goes out as mastered, and only what is brighter is squeezed in. Until now the squeezing started much lower, so faces, skin and interiors came out darker than mastered: on the reference SDR display, 100 nits of a film mastered at 1000 nits went out at 87 instead of 99, and of one mastered at 4000 nits at 61 instead of 86. The same curve, with the same fix, is used by *Dolby Vision as HDR10 mapped to the display*, *HDR10 and HDR10+ adapted to the display* and Dolby Vision Low-Latency (on a TV that shows more than the film's brightest, nothing changes). If you preferred the old look, which keeps a little more detail in the brightest highlights at the cost of darker middle tones, choose "BT.2390, libplacebo's knee" as the tone mapping method of the video, or as the *Tone mapping curve*: it is exactly the old picture. Measured with the software bridge; not tried on hardware.
- **HDR10+ adapted by its own metadata.** Where Hades adapts an HDR10+ video to the display itself (*HDR10 and HDR10+ adapted to the display*, or a display without HDR), it now follows the HDR10+ data of each scene, and the studio's tone curve where the video carries one, instead of the one peak of the whole film. In Dolby Vision Low-Latency, an HDR10+ video sent as Dolby Vision is mapped by the studio's tone curve too, and the player information says so. Measured with the software bridge; not tried on hardware.
- **Play HDR videos as SDR** (*System > Display*, Expert, Never by default): HLG videos, or HLG, HDR10 and HDR10+ videos, sent as ordinary SDR to a display that takes HDR, for a TV that shows HLG badly or a projector whose SDR picture is better. They are converted as for a display without HDR, and *Check this setup* and the player information say why. Dolby Vision videos are not affected. The display is now also told the colours that the picture is drawn in: an SDR video in BT.2020, or HDR shown as SDR with one of Kodi's own tone mapping methods, went out as BT.709 colours that the display was told were BT.2020, which a display that honours it shows too saturated. Not tried on hardware.
- **HDR in 4:2:2 at 12 bits over HDMI 2.0 on Intel and AMD graphics** (*HDR in 4:2:2 at 12 bits over HDMI 2.0*, Advanced, on). At 3840x2160 and 50 or 60 Hz an HDMI 2.0 link cannot carry RGB at 10 bits, so these drivers sent HDR10, HLG and HDR10+ as YCbCr 4:2:0 at 10 bits, or as RGB at 8. Where the TV takes 4:2:2, Hades now sends them as YCbCr 4:2:2 at 12 bits inside the same 8-bit RGB signal that carries Dolby Vision, with the HDR data of the video as always: the video goes out exactly, and the menus and subtitles over it keep twice the colour detail. It is used on Intel graphics and on the AMD graphics that sent 4:2:0 there, such as the integrated graphics of the Ryzen 7000 series. Such a video starts with one more change of mode of the TV. Where the graphics cannot keep up with the frames, that mode goes back to the ordinary output for the rest of the session, and the log says why. The player information names it as the HDR10 or HLG that it carries. After an idea of YBLOD. Not tried on hardware.
- **HDR above 8 bits on NVIDIA where the card would send RGB at 8, on by default** (*HDR at more than 8 bits where the card sends RGB at 8 (NVIDIA)*, issues #4 and #17). Where the HDMI output would send HDR in RGB at only 8 bits (GeForce GTX 10, GTX 16 and RTX 20 cards, the TITAN V, and newer cards at 3840x2160 and 50 or 60 Hz without an HDMI 2.1 link), HDR now goes out as YCbCr 4:2:2 at 12 bits (10 on the GeForce GTX 10 cards and the TITAN V) at the same link speed. If the TV does not lock onto it, that mode goes back to 8 bits by itself for the session. *RGB at 12 bits first (GeForce GTX 16, RTX 20)*, off by default, sends RGB at 12 bits instead where the mode fits, as this setting did on those cards before; some TVs lock onto it and show nothing. At 10 bits it was seen working on a GeForce GTX 1060 with an LG OLED65C8 (issue #17); at 12 bits, not tried on hardware.
- **HDMI variable refresh rate is left off during native Dolby Vision** on NVIDIA GeForce GTX 16, RTX 20 and RTX 30 graphics, whose driver turns it on in modes of 50 Hz or more whose rate reaches 110 % of the display's VRR minimum (with a minimum of 48 Hz: at 59.94 and 60 Hz, not at 50; issue #4). The kernel log says each change of the VRR packet. Not tried on hardware.
- **What another program left on the output is put back** (LibreELEC forum thread 30675). After a Flatpak app that ran its own Wayland session (VacuumTube, for one), HDR10 came out as RGB at 8 bits, because the depth of the link that the other program had lowered stayed. On the first commit of Kodi on an output, `max bpc` goes back to the top of its range on amdgpu, i915 and xe, and a colour transform or lookup table left on the output is cleared. A clean output goes out as before. Not tried on hardware.
- **Colour format and maximum bit depth** (*System > Display*, Expert, Automatic by default). They set the `color format` property that Linux 7.3 gives amdgpu, `NV_CONNECTOR_COLOR_FORMAT` of NVIDIA's driver 620, and `max bpc`. A format or depth that the driver cannot carry in a mode goes out as Automatic in that mode, and the log says so. After an idea of CroqueMr's fork (Kodi pull requests 30099 and 30192, by smp79). Not tried on hardware.
- **HDR over HDMI on Intel HD and UHD 6xx graphics, as an expert choice** (*System > Display > HDR over HDMI on this Intel graphics*, shown only on that graphics). Its HDMI port has no packet of its own for HDR, so HDR10 and HLG went to a TV without Dolby Vision as SDR. On, the HDR data go out in another packet of the graphics chip. Off by default: nobody has tried it on a TV yet. The idea of that packet is CroqueMr's.
- **HDR10 whose mastering display is written in two ways no longer stutters.** The same mastering display comes as fractions in the HEVC data of a picture and as floats from Matroska. A stream whose pictures carried it one way and then the other made Kodi set up the output again at each change: a stutter, the display was told the data again, and native Dolby Vision ended and began again in the middle of the video. The values are now compared as HDMI codes them. A stream that carries the data only with its first picture keeps it after every key picture and after a seek. After an idea of CroqueMr's build. Not tried on hardware.
- **A video whose container calls it SDR but whose stream says HLG or PQ** is taken as HDR from the start, so that its mode, its HDR signal and HLG sent as Dolby Vision are decided from the start, not from its first picture.
- **The check of which video the graphics can show follows the layout of each picture.** At start Kodi tries which video formats it can take from the hardware decoder. That test left out the memory layout that the driver gives each picture, which showing a picture uses, so on Intel and AMD graphics whose driver uses another layout Kodi could be left without its hardware renderer of video, or without 10-bit video in it. The test and the showing of a picture now describe each layer the same way. After an idea of CroqueMr's build. Not tried on hardware.
- **Monitors on the same graphics card as the TV are no longer left on with a black picture** where Hades is not sure which output carries the sound. Only the output with the picture is kept; a display with no audio descriptors in its EDID cannot carry sound at all. If your monitors declare sound, this may not be enough: the `kodi.log` lines "stays on for" say why. Not tried on hardware.
- **Only the display in use is lit, and the sound stays with it.** With several displays on one graphics card, the display whose number was chosen as the audio output was kept on, lit again after each video with the notification "The sound of ... comes back", and took the sound for a moment each time. The NVIDIA driver numbers its HDMI audio outputs by the order in which the displays came, so the number chosen in *System > Audio* can be another display from one start to the next. Now only the display that shows the picture is lit, and the sound goes to its own audio output (and from there to a sound bar or receiver on its eARC). The audio output is remembered by the display it was chosen with, not by its number; the setting itself is not changed, and `kodi.log` says where the sound went. For a receiver or sound bar on its own output of the graphics card, *System > Audio > Keep the display of the audio output on* (Expert, off by default) keeps that output on for its sound, as before. Not tried on hardware.
- **Each output is matched with its own display.** Linux gives the EDID of a display a new number each time it reads it, and reuses old numbers, so Kodi could read another display's EDID for an output: with three displays on an NVIDIA card, the TV was taken for a monitor and a monitor for the TV, which decided the output kept on for the sound and the name a display is shown by. Kodi now reads each output's EDID as it is at that moment. Not tried on hardware.
- **HDMI-CEC over a DisplayPort connector** (the AUX channel and the Linux CEC framework, with libcec), and **HDMI-CEC of SECO boards** (UDOO x86 and the like) as a module, which loads only on those boards. Not tried on hardware.
- **Hades waits for the NVIDIA driver before it starts.** Linux loads the driver of an NVIDIA card while the rest of the system starts. Where Hades looked for the graphics before the driver was ready (the first start after an installation, or with the wait for the network turned off in the LibreELEC settings), it showed itself on no output, or on other graphics, until it was started again. It now waits for the driver, 20 seconds at most, and not at all on a PC without an NVIDIA card; the kernel log says how long it waited. Idea from CroqueMr's build. Not tried on hardware.
- **A frame that the graphics driver refuses no longer leaves a half-drawn picture.** After three refused frames in a row, the picture that the screen still showed was drawn over. The buffer of a refused frame is now given back at once. After an idea of CroqueMr's build. Tested on the host.
- **Intel HDMI: the check of a TV after it drops its signal for a moment is no longer lost.** For 4 seconds after an HDMI hotplug, Hades looks at whether the TV still has the settings of the link, and sets the link up again where it lost them (an LG G5 does, after a Dolby Vision or refresh rate change). A look that came while a commit was under way was skipped without a word, which an HDR10+ video makes likely; it now counts as no look, for 10 more seconds at most, and the kernel log says when it gave up. After an idea of CroqueMr's build. Not tried on hardware.
- **Intel Panther Lake and Wildcat Lake: the fallback for reading the EDID works.** Where the hardware transfer of the display's data channel fails, Linux reads it bit by bit, and its workaround for these chips did not work. Five fixes by Ville Syrjälä from the development tree of Intel's graphics, not in Linux mainline yet. Not tried on hardware.
- **The kernel log says each InfoFrame of i915 and each packet of amdgpu** that goes out, when it changes, at most once a minute for those that change with each scene. Not tried on hardware.
- **NVIDIA driver 620.70.06.** Its patches were remade for 620; 580.196.07 stays for the GeForce GTX 900 and GTX 10 cards and the TITAN V. With driver 580, HDR of more than 8 bits that Kodi draws into its own buffers is flipped on the graphics chip where the plane cannot reflect it (issue #17). The kernel log says what the EDID that the driver read holds. The installer entry no longer loads an NVIDIA module, so the console stays on the framebuffer of the boot. Not tried on hardware.

**Picture and sound in step**

- **Picture in step with its sound** (*Settings > Player > Videos > Picture in step with its sound*, Advanced, on). On every driver the picture left the PC one refresh before its sound: 42 ms at 24 Hz and 17 ms at 60 Hz, as the logs of seven computers show. Hades now measures when each frame really reaches the screen and shows it with its sound. Which frames are seen, and for how long each, does not change: each one only starts at the right refresh. If you had set *Audio offset* to make up for this, set it back to 0. The offset of the frames from the refreshes is now also averaged on one branch: where a frame comes every second or fifth refresh (25 fps at 50 Hz, 23.976 fps at 119.88 Hz), its samples were a whole period apart. The averaging follows a finding of SamuriHL. Tested on the host; not tried on hardware.
- **A pause keeps passthrough sound** (Dolby TrueHD, Dolby Digital Plus, Dolby Digital, DTS). A pause threw away the sound that Kodi had sent to the output and not yet played (up to 480 ms of TrueHD, about 200 ms of the others) and started the sync over at the resume: the sound came back further on than where it stopped, after a run of corrections, and while paused the receiver got silence, which it takes for the end of the stream. The sound now goes on where it stopped, in sync, with nothing lost or repeated, and while paused the receiver gets pause bursts that keep it on the stream. What the output already held when you paused still plays out with the picture stopped, as before. Measured on the host; not tried with a receiver.
- **TrueHD comes back sooner after a start or a seek, and its corrections end where they should.** The measure of the sync at each start and seek turned all the TrueHD that the player had cached into silence, 680 ms, against about 300 ms of the other formats; TrueHD now comes back at about 320 ms. A correction of TrueHD stopped at less than half of the error; packets left out to catch up were counted as sound for up to 400 ms; and a correction longer than its pool of pauses let the sound through in the middle of it, with cuts. Each is fixed. After findings of SamuriHL and pannal. Measured on the host; not tried with a receiver.
- **DTS-HD and TrueHD passthrough that could fail or crash.** A DTS-HD Master Audio stream at its peak bit rate could be taken for DTS-HD High Resolution after a few frames and crash Kodi; a burst of the largest size could not be sent; a DTS frame of an odd size sent one byte too many; a DTS-HD stream whose core changed size kept the timing of the first frames; and a DTS-HD frame that claims to be larger than the parser made passthrough stop for the rest of the stream. After a seek, a TrueHD stream could be taken for Dolby Digital Plus for a moment and the output opened twice. The first and the last are fixes still under review for Kodi, by tanio99 (for OSMC) and by heit88, taken with their authors. Tested on the host; not tried with a receiver.
- **The sound output is closed without playing out its own silence** when a video starts and the format changes, and a resampler that cannot be set up no longer crashes Kodi: the audio engine opens its output again.

**Performance**

- **A lighter interface during native Dolby Vision, HDR10 and HLG.** Where no control changed, the pass that draws the interface is left out and the interface of the frame before is put over the picture, and only over the rectangles where the interface is, instead of the whole 3840x2160 frame. The same bytes go to the display, and a subtitle still goes out with its picture. `System.FPS` still counts such frames. A step of the conversion under the interface that saves nothing is left. Not tried on hardware.
- **Dolby Vision made from HDR10, HDR10+ and profile 8.1 takes about a third less GPU time.** Their metadata map the picture to itself, so the decoder's picture now goes straight into the signal, the same bytes: an HDR10+ picture sent as Dolby Vision took 35.0 ms instead of 53.5 on a software renderer. Where small graphics cannot keep up, frames with the player information or menus over such a picture go out converted while they are on screen, only where measured to help; frames without them are unchanged. Not tried on hardware.
- **Dolby Vision packing takes less GPU time while the interface is over the picture** (menus, the player information, subtitles), with the same bytes. The value that the two pixels of a pair share is worked out once, through the quad operations of the GPU, instead of twice, and black or opaque pixels of the interface take a shorter path. Measured on the host with two of Mesa's software renderers, with the interface over part of the picture: 14 to 28 % less with zink and 6 to 11 % less with llvmpipe (3 to 4 % where the interface is composited first). Without the interface it is unchanged. After ideas of CroqueMr's build. Not tried on hardware.
- **No pause where a video mapped to the display starts.** The colour table that a mapping to the display needs (Low-Latency, Dolby Vision or HDR10 mapped to an HDR10 display, anything mapped to SDR) was made when its first picture was drawn, on the thread of the interface: 0.64 s of one processor core of a Ryzen 9 7950X3D. It is now made ahead, on a thread of its own, as soon as the video is set up. Not tried on hardware.
- **Frames ahead within seconds where a graphics chip cannot keep up at all.** A GT 1030 with a profile 7 film missed up to half of its frames, and the old test took 48 seconds to see it. Not tried on hardware.
- **Less memory for the HEVC decoder.** The pool of video surfaces is sized from the level of the stream (a 4K level 5 stream: 14 surfaces instead of 22, 20 instead of 28 with two layers), and with nvidia-vaapi-driver the surface given back last goes out first, so that only as many surfaces get an image as are held at once. Meant for graphics cards with 2 GB. Not tried on hardware.
- **With nvidia-vaapi-driver, a surface of the enhancement layer that was let go without being drawn is synced when it is taken again.** That driver copies a decoded picture on a thread of its own, and a copy that ended late could mark the next picture as copied. Not tried on hardware.
- **Smaller things:** a video paused in its last seconds no longer keeps a processor core busy (71 % of a core before, 2.3 % now); the debug information on screen takes its figures twice a second instead of in every frame, so it no longer redraws the interface over HDR; thumbnails of HDR videos take 98 MB at most instead of 163 MB, and thumbnails of DVDs and HDR videos no longer set their colour conversion up again for each one; a picture shown at its own size is never sent through a high quality scaler (it was with the threshold at 0 %).

The picture-in-picture video and the secondary audio of a disc are never opened in place of the film. The audio and subtitles that a disc chooses before its playlist is ready are given again once the play item is known (issue #1: the subtitles chosen in a menu were not on when the film started), and the log says each choice. Not tried with a disc.

### Details

**Dolby Vision**

- The default transport (`videoscreen.hadesdvtransport`) is "As composed" (2): the composer's 12-bit YCbCr of profiles 7 and 8, as it is; "As licensed players send (IPT)" (0) is the IPT-PQ-c2 of before, and a value that cannot be read gives the default. The bridge's object code is the same. Measured with `tools/regress/chroma` against a full 4:4:4 picture in delta E ITP (ITU-R BT.2124), for a display that rebuilds the right pixel of a pair with a four-tap filter, on pictures of two profile 7 films: a mean of 0.127 and 0.131 as composed against 0.163 and 0.178 in IPT, and a largest error of 6.5 and 6.7 against 6.5 and 17.8. The bytes of the IPT model are the bridge's to within one code in 99.96 % of samples.
- The check of the reshaping curve (`dvbridge_map_color()`) accepts repeated pivots, with the piece of a sample picked by the first pivot above it (ETSI GS CCM 001, 5.4.2.2 and 5.4.2.3). libplacebo does the same (`libplacebo-9979`): its reshaping picks the piece by the sample as it is, so that a pivot that equals the one before it behaves as the specification says. A frame that is not drawn keeps a fixed text for each check (`dvbridge_render_not_drawn()`) beside why the metadata were refused (`dvbridge_render_refused()`).
- The trim pass follows ETSI TS 103 572 (4.3, equations 4 to 8: slope, offset, power, chroma weight and saturation gain, 2048 when absent) and SMPTE ST 2094-10:2021 (Annex B.3, equations 8 and 9, an informative example in linear light), for the level 2 block whose target peak equals the peak of the display to within one code, on Hades' own curve. It is drawn only with `videoscreen.hadesdvtrimpass` on, and never with `videoscreen.hadesdvnotrims` on, which still decides alone what native output sends. Against the colourist's own 1000-nit grade of "Sparks" (Netflix Open Content, CC BY 4.0), six frames: the mean delta E ITP went from 25.3 without the trims to 47.2 with them, and under 100 cd/m² from 18.1 to 53.0. There is no interpolation between blocks, because the standard does not define one, and no level 8, whose algorithm is not public.
- HLG sent as Dolby Vision is profile 8.1 over a PQ picture that libplacebo makes of the HLG picture, not a profile 8.4 with a made-up reshaping, which would only approximate the OOTF of BT.2100. With the setting off, the bytes sent are the same as before.
- The update of libplacebo leaves what native Dolby Vision sends byte for byte as it was. It changes two conversions of HLG to a 4000 cd/m² target, in values of 0.06 or less, because libplacebo dropped a floor that BT.2100 does not have.
- The output that native output takes is held by `COutputHandover` (`xbmc/windowing/gbm/drm/OutputHandover.cpp`), with its decisions in `OutputState.h` and its calls to libdrm in `LibdrmKms.h`. It captures every property of the connector, the CRTC and the plane in use that is not read-only (a blob by its bytes), except those that something else owns, that cannot be put back or that are not a state, each named with its reason. What the transport sets is of two kinds: its signals (the Dolby Vision, Low-Latency, HDMI 1.4b and HDR tunnel flags, and the InfoFrame of nvidia-drm), which every attempt carries, and the settings that it makes neutral (depth, range, colorimetry, colour format, HDR metadata, dithering, HDMI VRR, colour processing), which a refusal can leave at the transport's value, with a warning in the log. The end puts in its request only what is not as it was found, reads the output back once the commit is taken, and sets again in a commit of its own what is not as found. Objects are told by their ids. What the transport made of the output is kept in `/run/hades-dv-state`, written whole with a checksum. `DetectedProperty.h` keeps what the kernel said of the converter behind a port, and of a dual-mode port of nvidia-drm, until the display is looked at again, instead of reading the kernel with each picture. These files are written from the KMS interface and are GPL-2.0-or-later.
- With driver 620, the transport sets `NV_CONNECTOR_OUTPUT_BPC` and `NV_CONNECTOR_COLOR_FORMAT` to `auto` where the connector has them, for Standard, Low-Latency and the HDMI 1.4b signalling (`TransportSpecs.h`), and `nvidia-open-kernel-modules-9062` refuses a connector state with Dolby Vision unless both are `auto`, 8 or RGB.

## 1.0 RC8

An update that fixes faults found in 1.0 RC7 and adds a few smaller things. Each change was tested on the host, with the code that the image runs; each item says how.

### What's new for you

- **HDR10 upright with NVIDIA's driver 580** (GeForce GTX 900 and GTX 10 cards, TITAN V; issue #17). In 1.0 RC7, HDR video drawn into the 10-bit buffers that Kodi allocates itself on these cards went out upside down, menus and subtitles too. The plane that shows them now flips them back, in the same commit as their first frame. A plane that cannot flip them never gets them: the video then goes out upright at 8 bits.
- **No GRUB error after an update** (issue #17). An update with the `.tar` now also replaces the two GRUB programs of the boot partition with those of the new system. A drive installed before 1.0 RC6 kept its older GRUB, which lacks the command `[` that the boot menu of 1.0 RC7 uses: each start said "can't find command `['" and waited for a key.
- **Seeking and the arrows during a Blu-ray played with its menus** (issue #1). A Blu-ray with Java menus keeps its menu graphics open, and fully transparent, through the whole film, and Kodi took every key for the disc: nothing could seek, the arrows did not move through the film and the player information did not open. Kodi now takes the disc to be in its menu only while its menu plane shows something; subtitles alone do not count.
- **The thin text of Blu-ray menus is no longer faint** (issue #1). The graphics of a Blu-ray are drawn at 1920x1080, and at 3840x2160 each of their pixels was mixed with its neighbours, which takes a quarter of the contrast off a line one pixel wide and about half off a dot. At a whole scale they now keep their pixels, as the disc drew them.
- **How long the boot menu waits** (issue #17). *LibreELEC Settings > System > Boot menu* sets how long the menu shown at start waits before it starts the system by itself: 10 seconds (the default), 3 or 1, on UEFI and BIOS computers alike.
- **HDR at 10 bits over HDMI on GeForce GTX 10 cards and the TITAN V, as an option** (issue #17). Over HDMI, the display code of driver 580 sends RGB at 8 bits at most on these cards, whatever Kodi asks and the display takes, so 1.0 RC7 was wrong to say that HDR10 goes out at 10 bits there. They do drive YCbCr 4:2:2 at 10 bits, at the speed of RGB at 8 bits: with *System > Display > HDR at more than 8 bits where the card sends RGB at 8 (NVIDIA)* on, HDR goes out that way where the display takes it, from the next video. The same setting turns on the 12 bits of the GeForce GTX 16 and RTX 20 graphics, which needed a kernel parameter until now. It is off until it is confirmed on those cards. The GeForce GTX 900 cards stay at 8 bits over HDMI, and DisplayPort is not under this limit.
- **Up to date:** LibreELEC (NSS 3.133, LLVM 23.1.5, HarfBuzz 15.1.0, lz4 1.11.0, rsync 3.5.2) and the `Piers` branch of Kodi, whose one new commit keeps the clock of the display running through a change of mode: a video that switches the output to HDR as it starts no longer falls back to the system clock until it ends.

**Display**

## 1.0 RC7

### What's new for you

- **A film no longer ends when its drive stops answering for a while** (issue #16). When a USB drive stopped answering and the kernel reset it after 30 s, the wait used up the time that the player gives each packet, and the film ended just as the drive came back. The time that a read of a local file waits for its drive no longer counts against that time, and a read that fails with an I/O error after the reset is tried again at the same byte for up to 15 s: the picture stands still while the drive is away, and the film goes on when it answers. This covers Blu-ray and DVD images and folders on a local drive too, and Stop during such a wait ends it within a fraction of a second. A drive that is unplugged ends the film at once, as before, and files over the network are not changed. Tested on the host.
- **Limited colour range on NVIDIA is right from the start** (issue #17). With *Use limited colour range (16-235)* on, a GTX 1060 with driver 580 sent full range after each start of Kodi while the AVI InfoFrame said limited, which crushed the blacks until a video changed the mode. Where the range changes at the same mode, NVIDIA's driver now turns the output off and on, so that the new range reaches the cable: the TV loses its signal for a moment at Kodi's start with the setting on, when the setting changes, and when Dolby Vision starts or ends at the same refresh rate. A flip that changes the HDR state of the output no longer puts the range back to full. With the setting off nothing changes. Both driver series, 615 and 580.
- **Outputs that carry the sound stay on.** Ten seconds after it starts, Hades turns off the outputs that Kodi does not use, and a receiver or sound bar on another output lost its sound. Hades now never turns off the output whose sound Kodi uses (the audio and passthrough outputs of *System > Audio*, or the ones used in their place): it finds it by the graphics that its sound card belongs to and by the display that its audio data (ELD) name, and where that is not certain, every output that can carry sound and has a display stays on. When Kodi moves its picture to another output, the one it leaves stays on, black, if its sound is in use. An output kept on for its sound is turned off again once that sound is no longer in use, as in 1.0 RC6. An output turned off before its sound came into use comes back by itself on other graphics, and on Kodi's own graphics with the next video that changes the display mode, which Kodi says on screen, or when Kodi starts again. The file `/storage/.kodi/userdata/hades-keep-outputs` names more outputs to keep on, one per line (`DP-1`, `card0-HDMI-A-2` or `*`): it is harment's patch, with his authorship (issue #11). `kodi.log` says which output is kept for which sound, and why.
- **"Dolby Vision as HDR10+" for one video** (issue #16). *Dolby Vision output (this video)* now offers "Dolby Vision as HDR10+" where the display takes HDR10+, and "Dolby Vision as HDR10" sends plain HDR10, whatever *Send it as HDR10+ where the display takes it* says, so that the two can be compared from the player. "Dolby Vision as HDR10" kept for a file by 1.0 RC6 still does what it did, and the list shows it under the name of what it gives. On Intel at 3840x2160 up to 30 Hz and 4096x2160 at 24 Hz, HDR10+ chosen while a video plays now goes out, with one change of mode. The help line of the button in the video OSD no longer says "RadioText Plus info". Tested on the host.
- **The output depth during native Dolby Vision** (issue #16). The overview of the player information said "8 bits (fixed)" on NVIDIA and "up to N bits" elsewhere; on every driver it now says "8 bits: Dolby Vision carries its 12 bits in them", which is how Dolby Vision goes over HDMI.
- **HDR10 at 10 bits with NVIDIA's driver 580** (GeForce GTX 900 and GTX 10 cards, TITAN V). That driver offers Kodi no window of more than 8 bits, so HDR10, HLG and Dolby Vision shown as HDR10 went out at 8 bits. Kodi now draws such video into 10-bit buffers that it allocates itself, after checking that the driver takes them, also after a change of resolution during the video; where a step fails, the video goes out at 8 bits as before, and `kodi.log` says which step. `<video><hdrownbuffers>false</hdrownbuffers></video>` turns it off. Driver 615 is not changed. Tested on the host.
- **A Blu-ray played with its menus keeps its seamless joins.** From the disc's menus, the player closed and opened its streams at every new play item, so a film made of shared and specific clips (several cuts on one disc) paused at each join. Where the playlist joins two play items seamlessly and their streams are the same, playback now goes on, on one timeline, as it has without menus since 1.0 RC6; anything else is as before, and `kodi.log` says which, and why, for each join. A key pressed while the disc changes its title, playlist or play item no longer makes that change get lost. Tested on the host with discs made for the test.
- **Dolby Vision decoded by the processor goes out natively.** Where the graphics cannot decode a stream (AV1 on GeForce RTX 20 and GTX 16, AMD Renoir and Cezanne, Intel before Tiger Lake) or VAAPI is off, single-layer Dolby Vision (profiles 5, 8 and 10) played as its base layer, and profile 10.0 with wrong colours. Its pictures now go to native Dolby Vision output, to Dolby Vision shown as HDR10 and to the mapping to SDR, from the planes that Kodi already uploads, with the same bytes as those of a hardware decoder (measured with the software bridge). Profile 7 is as before. Idea from CroqueMr's build. No AV1 file with Dolby Vision has been tried.

Offsets that fit no reading of the picture made every picture be refused, and the video left native output; offsets that say bars where the picture has content (an open-matte film with the metadata of its letterboxed master) turned those rows black. The first are now left aside for the rest of the video. For the second, Hades measures a picture now and then and leaves the offsets aside too once at least ten pictures have been measured under that level 5 value, more than half of them with picture across the width of the bars, three of them 2 s or more apart; the whole picture shows after about ten seconds. ### Details

**Dolby Vision**

- "Dolby Vision as HDR10+" is offered where the display declares HDR10+ and its connector can send it, the picture can be rebuilt as HDR10, and no setting maps it to the display; where it is not offered, no choice sends HDR10+. What a rebuilt picture goes out as is decided once a video, and again when the choice changes during the video (it stayed the decision of the start). A file kept with "Dolby Vision as HDR10" by 1.0 RC6 follows *Send it as HDR10+ where the display takes it*, as it did, and is listed as "Dolby Vision as HDR10+" where that gives HDR10+, otherwise as "Dolby Vision as HDR10"; back on 1.0 RC6, the new choices kept for files are dropped and the others stay. `kodi.log` says what a rebuilt picture goes out as, and why, also with "As the settings say".
- HDR10+ started while a video plays: amdgpu starts and ends it with a flip; on i915 and xe, at the modes that DRM names by their HDMI VIC, the next commit sets the mode again, as at the start of a video, and the first picture waits for its own measurement in it; nvidia-drm puts the CTA VIC in the AVI InfoFrame with the flip that starts it. Once HDR10+ has gone out at a mode, the AVI InfoFrame keeps the CTA VIC until the next change of mode, so HDR10+ chosen again goes out with a flip. A refused commit is tried again without HDR10+, and the video goes on as HDR10. Tested on the host.
- Dolby Vision decoded by the processor: the bridge takes the three planes of 10-bit 4:2:0 that Kodi's renderer uploads, without a copy, and the composer of ETSI GS CCM 001 reads each sample as its code, so that they give the codes of the P010 surface of a hardware decoder. Not for profile 7, whose enhancement layer needs VAAPI, nor for a picture made by a filter or of another format, of an odd size, larger than 3840x2160 or rotated. Single-layer HEVC decoded by the processor (VAAPI off, or graphics without HEVC Main10) goes the same way. When the processor cannot keep up, the AV1 decoder drops pictures that are not references. The player information no longer gives "software" as the reason for such a stream.
- Level 5 offsets fit when they describe the picture read at its own size or as a 3840x2160 master. The measurement runs only for native output, on VAAPI pictures (P010): a picture a second in the first 10 s, then one at a scene cut at most every 10 s and one every 30 s without a cut. A picture contradicts the offsets where more than half of the rows of a bar (columns, for a side bar) have content across its width, in at least 12 of the 16 strips of the measurement (6 of 8 for a side bar): a dirty edge, a dark scene, a logo, a line of subtitles or bright rows at the edge do not. It is the measurement of HDR10 measured as Dolby Vision, collected a frame later without waiting. Pictures decoded by the processor are not measured, and in profile 5 very dark content counts as black, so that it can only decide less often. A decision holds while the level 5 offsets keep their value, also when the renderer is made anew; other offsets start their own count. The measurement goes on at a low rate, and the offsets come back if half or fewer of the measured pictures contradict them.

**Display and HDMI**

- NVIDIA and limited range (`nvidia-legacy-open-9069` and `9070`, `nvidia-open-kernel-modules-9058` and `9059`): where the RGB range of a head changes and its clock, raster and protocol do not, NVKMS now shuts the head down in an update of its own before it sets it again, as it already does for DisplayPort and FRL; set again in place, the head kept its old range. A flip that changes the HDR state of a head (a plane turned off, new HDR metadata of a plane and, with driver 615 on RTX 30 cards whose connectors are all HDMI, the start and end of HDR10) keeps the range that the mode set asked for. The kernel log says when a head is shut down first for its range, and when its range compression goes on or off. No report shows the fault with driver 615; it is done the same way there, so that both series behave alike.
- Outputs kept on for their sound: a sound card belongs to graphics when it is on the same PCI slot (NVIDIA, AMD) or when the Intel graphics are its supplier, and the output kept is the one whose EDID names the display that the ELD names (maker, product and name). Two displays of the same model are both kept. Where that is not certain (the audio engine has said nothing yet, the card belongs to no graphics that can be told, the display is not known or is on no output), every HDMI, DisplayPort or DVI output with a display stays on, of the graphics of the card if they are known, otherwise of all. An analogue, S/PDIF or USB output keeps nothing on. The turning off waits up to 30 s for the audio engine.

A refused commit that turned off the outputs that Kodi does not use is asked for again 10 s later, three times at most for a mode; before, a refusal left them on, also without the file. An output of Kodi's graphics comes back only in a commit that changes a mode anyway, never with native Dolby Vision or a move, black or, on amdgpu, with a black picture of its own. Each entry of `hades-keep-outputs` that names no output is said once, with the names there are.
- HDR10 at 10 bits with driver 580: three buffers of AB30 (or XB30, then half floats where the half-float output is allowed), with the modifiers that the plane takes. Before use, four pixels are written with values that 8 bits cannot hold and read back from each, and the driver takes one in a test commit. A failure keeps 8 bits, and that display, mode and format are not tried again; a commit refused for the buffer puts the window back for the rest of the video (after three videos in a row, for the next seven). A frame-packed 3D mode stays at 8 bits, and native Dolby Vision at its 8. While the buffers are in use, the interface is drawn whole at each frame, and the 8-bit window stays allocated (about 100 MB at 3840x2160). `<video><hdrownbuffersalways>true</hdrownbuffersalways></video>` uses them with any driver. Tested on the host with a software renderer; the import of a real buffer and what the driver sends are not tried.

**Files and streams**

- A read of a local file that takes 1 s or more gives its time back to the deadline of the demuxer, 20 s for each packet; what the demuxer itself takes still counts, and a stop is never undone. A read of a played file that fails with EIO is tried again at the same byte after 0.1, 0.25, 0.5 and 1 s, then every 2 s, up to 15 s from the first error; not for a file that is not on a drive of this machine (network, FUSE, memory: said once), for another error, or for a drive that is gone (it left `/sys` or is offline), which is checked before each wait. Only reads for playback retry, also those of a Blu-ray or DVD image or folder on a local drive, which also count as local for the deadline; thumbnails, file details and other reads that are not playback fail at once, as before. A damaged sector now holds the picture for up to about 14 s before the video ends. Stop pressed during a retry wait ends it within a fraction of a second; Stop pressed while the kernel still waits for the drive keeps the interface still until the read returns, as before.

**Blu-ray**

- A join is kept when it is the next play item, reached by reading to the end of the one before; its bytes start where the playlist puts it; its connection condition is 5 or 6 (read with Kodi's own playlist parser); the play item before does not end in a still; it is not the second view of a 3D disc; and the streams that the playlist and the two clips present are the same (PIDs, codings, formats, rates, languages, dynamic range and the others). The kept play item goes on the timeline of the one that the demuxer opened with, and the times of the disc's menus are not moved.
- The events of the disc are taken by one thread at a time. A key could empty the event queue of libbluray while the player held an event (a title, a playlist, a play item, an angle, a seek or a still) and lose it; such an event is now left to the thread that reads. An event that comes with a key press, most often with BD-J, now opens the streams again as when it comes with a read; before, it was processed without.

**Boot and system**

- GRUB: `search --fs-uuid` for the identifier in the entry's `boot=`, with the partition that the menu was read from as hint. The installer of the image and the setting of the internal disks know the new lines, and the Windows installer writes the new menus with an update, as it always did. The update in place (`bootloader/update.sh`, run by the system that is updated, and only then) changes a menu only when it is, line by line, one of Hades as written, with the identifier of this start in its four entries; anything else is left as it is, with a line on the console of the update. The new menu is written to `grub.cfg.new`, flushed, read back and renamed, so that a power cut leaves one menu or the other. `syslinux.cfg` is not touched: in BIOS the kernel always came from the menu's own partition.
- `/opt` is an empty folder of the system, made with the other folders of its base layout.

**Diagnostics**

- `results.txt` of each snapshot: the power control and runtime state of each USB device; every USB controller (UHCI, OHCI, EHCI, xHCI and USB4) and every device of the `thunderbolt` driver, with their root hubs; the autosuspend of usbcore; and the USB4 lines of the kernel, who runs its connection manager, the security level of each domain and, of each router, its name, generation, authorisation, lanes and speed. No identifier of a router is read.
- The storage timeline of the shutdown (`io-timeline.txt.gz`): after the columns of `/storage`, each other disk with its reads, writes, times and requests in flight, on each line where one of them changed or a request is waiting, and a line whenever the disks, or those mounted under `/var/media`, change.

## 1.0 RC5

### What's new for you

- **Dolby Vision goes out in IPT, as licensed players send it.** Profiles 7 and 8 now reach the TV in IPT-PQ-c2, as licensed players are reported to send every profile (Amlogic's licensed driver; captures of licensed Amlogic and MediaTek players), with the matrices that profile 5 videos carry. The TV no longer converts the composer's YCbCr itself, and the intensity between the pairs of 4:2:2 chroma no longer depends on how it interpolates the chroma. Where a graphics chip cannot make the conversion in time, the automatic fidelity step-down sends the composer's YCbCr at its last level; `kodi.log` says every 10 s how many frames went out each way. The expert setting "As composed" sends the YCbCr as before. Profile 5 is unchanged, byte for byte. The documents no longer call the old default what certified players send ([how it works](docs/how-it-works.md#what-licensed-players-send)).
- **Dolby Vision starts sooner and without losing its first pictures.** The change of mode for a Dolby Vision video now goes out at once, with the Dolby Vision signal already set up (a black frame with valid metadata), before the video's clock starts, instead of with its first picture. The TV sets itself up while the video opens, the first pictures are no longer dropped, and the HDMI audio opens once. Only where nothing is in doubt (Dolby Vision Standard, profiles 5, 7, 8.1 and 8.6, a change of mode that can carry the tunnel); anything else starts as before. `<video><dvstartsignal>false</dvstartsignal></video>` turns it off. Idea from YBLOD. *Not tried on hardware yet.*
- **A seek no longer freezes the menus and the picture for half a second.** A seek from the progress bar of the on-screen display, a big step, a bookmark or `PlayerControl(SeekPercentage)` made Kodi stop drawing until the player had sought the stream, for up to 500 ms each time; over a network every such seek in the hardware test of 1.0 RC4 took the full 500 ms, with or without Dolby Vision, on the AMD integrated graphics and on the RTX 4080 alike, and every step froze the interface and the picture. Kodi now goes on drawing, and the time it shows is the target of the seek while the seek is on its way, as it is once it is done. A run of steps ends in one seek of the stream, and a step asked for while a seek is on its way goes from where that seek goes. The step seeks of the arrow keys already worked this way. Seeks asked for by add-ons and JSON-RPC wait as before. *Not tried on hardware yet.*
- **A stream that runs dry for a moment no longer loses the rest of its group of pictures** (a slow network, a seek over a network), and Dolby Vision FEL keeps its enhancement layer: the decoder starts again at the last random-access point instead of waiting for the next one. `kodi.log` says each time, and how many pictures went out without the FEL layer and why. *Not tried on hardware yet.*
- **Native Dolby Vision in sync with the sound as the ordinary output.** A frame of native output presented the way that waits reaches the screen one refresh sooner after it is chosen than one of the ordinary output, and went out that much before its sound: 50 to 77 ms at 23.976 Hz on the AMD integrated graphics in the test of 1.0 RC4. It is now chosen one refresh later, and a frame presented a frame ahead as the ordinary output; which frames are shown at which refresh rate does not change. Every 10 s `kodi.log` gives the measured A/V offset of each way (`DVBridge: A/V in the last 10 s`), and every minute that of the ordinary output. *Not tried on hardware yet.*
- **Dolby Vision for the menus**, as an option (off by default): while the menus are shown, the TV stays in Dolby Vision, so a Dolby Vision video starts without the TV changing its picture mode, and the menus come back the same way. Any other video (SDR, HDR10, HDR10+, HLG) makes the TV change its mode once more at its start and once more at its end. The menus go out with fixed metadata, at the white of "GUI and subtitle brightness in HDR"; nothing of a video's picture changes. *Dolby Vision for the menus*, in *System > Display*, shown for a TV that takes Dolby Vision, with the menus at 3840x2160 ([how it works](docs/how-it-works.md#dolby-vision-for-the-menus)). The idea is YBLOD's, written again for Hades. Not yet seen on a TV.
- **The menus, the OSD and subtitles over HDR keep their dark tones.** Over HDR10, HLG and Dolby Vision, the GUI was made PQ or HLG through two small tables that darkened its dark greys (at the default white, a grey of sRGB code 13, 5 % of the scale, came out at 0.12 cd/m² where it is 0.82, and the darkest codes black), the more the brighter "GUI and subtitle brightness in HDR" is set. It is now converted exactly, as Kodi's OpenGL output converts it (the sRGB curve, BT.2020, the GUI white): measured within 0.03 codes of 4095 of the exact conversion, where it was up to 452 off at the default white. The video under the GUI does not change; HDR image subtitles of UHD Blu-ray keep their own levels. Measured on a software renderer; not yet seen on a TV.
- **Native Dolby Vision on older AMD graphics,** over HDMI: the Radeon RX 400 and 500 series (Polaris), the Radeon RX Vega M of Intel's Kaby Lake G processors, and the Radeon RX Vega 56 and 64 and the Radeon VII. It started from the Vega M work in the fork by CroqueMr (`linux-9925`), and goes on from it to Polaris and Vega 10, 12 and 20. Nobody has tried it on them yet: if you have one with a Dolby Vision TV, please report what you see, with the diagnostic switch on ([diagnostics](docs/diagnostics.md)), since the kernel log then says what the graphics hold for Dolby Vision.
- **Native Dolby Vision through an HDMI port that NVIDIA drives through a DisplayPort output** (issue #4, HP ZBook 17 G6): since 1.0 RC4 it never started there. Hades waits for the video's mode to be on the output before it starts Dolby Vision on such a port, and a change that is no change of mode (the video's colours) kept it waiting; it now asks the kernel which mode is on the output. On such a port every change of the refresh rate now also turns the output off and on, so that the TV takes the new rate, which it did not always do when the mode changed on a running output. `<video><dualmoderestart>false</dualmoderestart></video>` changes the mode in place there, as before. *Not tried on that laptop yet.*
- **Dolby Vision sent as HDR10+ on Intel at 2160p24, 25 and 30** (issue #2): the first picture now goes with its metadata in the change of mode of the video, which Intel's driver needs at those modes. **Dolby Vision picture and metadata**

A picture drawn smaller than its own size is still rebuilt in RGB and converted to BT.2020. Measured with the bridge running in software against the 4K master, a picture scaled up this way is closer to it than converted to BT.2020 as before (average ΔE ITP 0.349 to 0.266 and 0.382 to 0.312 on two profile 5 film pictures, 1.312 to 1.170 on a profile 5 chart).

They are measured on the GPU from a grid of up to 960x540 pixels of the film's area (the bars left out) and read back a frame or more later; the metadata of a frame are those of its scene so far, and the first picture of a scene waits for its own measurement where that comes before the next vblank. Within a scene they change only when a value moves by more than 32 codes of 4095 in PQ, and then at most every 24 pictures unless it moves by more than 64. Profile 8.1 is rebuilt too while it is on. `kodi.log` and the Dolby Vision page say when it goes out ([how it works](docs/how-it-works.md#dolby-vision-sent-as-hdr10)).

With BT.2390, the default, the ramp comes out as it is.
- Profile 5, and AV1 profile 10 with a base layer compatible with nothing, are rebuilt for output as HDR10 wherever native output is not possible, whatever "Dolby Vision as HDR10 when needed" says; HDR10+ and "map to the display" follow that setting as their help says.

- Profile 8.2 (an SDR base layer): composed at 14 bits and converted to PQ as clause 5.5 of ETSI GS CCM 001 says (`libplacebo-9955`); never by libplacebo's reshaping, which would take its base layer for PQ. Where the composer does not take it, it plays as Kodi plays it, and `kodi.log` says why.
- An enhancement layer coded larger than its picture (1088 rows for 1080, for example) is imported at the size of the picture: the rows beyond it were read by the resampling.
- A content mapping v4.0 part that cannot be carried (a block of a length or value that dovi_tool does not know, or one that would take the metadata of a frame past 512 bytes) is left out for the rest of the stream, which keeps native output with the v2.9 part; `kodi.log` says which block and why. Before, every frame was refused, and native output stopped.

- Where an RPU has no level 5 block but has a content mapping v4.0 part, the level 5 block that Hades makes goes at the end of the v2.9 part, where an RPU carries its own.
- The enhancement layer: a paired picture whose enhancement picture brought no RPU takes the base decoder's reading of the RPU; the layer starts by picture order only where that can be known; the start codes of a packet are found once.
- Dolby Vision shown as HDR10 in a 10-bit window is kept as the codes of that window (`libplacebo-9946`): the same bytes, with less GPU memory and bandwidth.
- libplacebo's own reshaping (the expert setting "Composition", and the pictures that the composer of the specification does not take) dequantizes the enhancement layer on its value as the scaler gives it, without the rounding to an integer that libplacebo 7.387.0 brought (`libplacebo-9978`). Measured against the reference of the specification, the rounding took the decoded picture further from it at every step-down level (61.0 % of its pixels a code or more off instead of 56.7 %), and at levels 2 and 3 it made the two ways of drawing a frame differ in 5.5 % of their chroma codes, by up to 2, instead of 0.3 %, by 1. The composer of the specification, the default, does not use it.
- The expert setting "Finer gamut mapping" (*System > Display > Dolby Vision engine*) maps through a table of 256x192x512 entries instead of 96x64x512; where 16-bit unorm textures cannot be filtered, the table is kept in floats.

Off by default. The mapping is Hades' own, not Dolby's.

- **HDR and Dolby Vision on a display without HDR.** HDR10, HLG and Dolby Vision profiles 5, 7 and 8.1 are mapped to SDR as a reference display maps them (ITU-R BT.2390), now the default tone mapping method: measured, much closer to the master than Kodi's own methods. Profile 5 on such a display no longer comes out green and purple.
- **More Dolby Vision files play as Dolby Vision:** profile 7 in two video tracks of MKV and MP4 files (issues #1 and #5), and HEVC streams that carry Dolby Vision without a record of it in their container.
- **More TVs and graphics:** Dolby Vision TVs that read display management 2 or 3 (many of 2017 and 2018), Radeon RX 9000 graphics (RDNA 4), Intel UHD 6xx graphics, and the HDMI port of laptops whose NVIDIA graphics drive it through a dual-mode DisplayPort connector.

### Details

**Dolby Vision picture and metadata**

- The PQ curve of the Dolby Vision decoding is computed without cancellation (`libplacebo-9942`): a profile 5 picture rebuilt as HDR10 had bands of up to 315 codes of 4095 at the edge of BT.2020, now 39 at most.
- The mapping to a display (SDR, projectors, Low-Latency) maps to the display's peak, not to a virtual peak above it that darkened the whole picture by 2 to 11 % (`libplacebo-9943`), and its gamut table is finer (96x64x512 instead of 48x32x256), which halves the largest error on SDR.
- Menus or subtitles over the black bars no longer mark a new scene in the metadata (ETSI GS CCM 001, 6.2.2): a change of the active area takes a new metadata id instead, and a bar stays in the area for 2 s of video after what was drawn over it is gone.
- A display of display management 2 or 3 is sent the content mapping v2.9 part of the metadata, which it reads, without the content mapping v4.0 blocks, which it does not.
- A picture that goes out without its enhancement picture (after a seek, or late) carries the RPU of its own access unit, not that of a neighbour.
- The enhancement layer starts by picture order at a random-access picture of its own where the two layers do not share one; with the base layer reordered, it never started before. A new VAAPI device context of the same display no longer starts the layer again, and a MEL stream whose layer turns out to carry a residual loses it once at most, then has its layer decoded.
- Dolby Vision shown as HDR10 draws into a second texture and shows a picture only when it is whole (132 MB more at 3840x2160); where nothing rebuilt can be shown, it shows the base layer when that is a picture by itself. Its 8-bit limited-range copy has white at 235, not 234.
- Kodi's video scaling method is not used by the Dolby Vision engine, which resamples as the specification says; the Dolby Vision page of the player information now says so.

**Performance**

The times in this section were measured through Direct3D 12 under Windows, not with the Linux drivers.

- A frame with GUI elements over native Dolby Vision is remade only in the 16x16-pixel tiles that the GUI reaches, with the same bytes: on an RTX 4080 SUPER, 1.4 to 2.7 ms instead of 1.5 to 9.3. `<video><dvdrawntiles>false</dvdrawntiles></video>` in `advancedsettings.xml` turns it off, to compare.
- What is drawn over the bars is measured in blocks, each pixel read once: on the integrated graphics of the Ryzen 9 7950X3D, 5.5 ms instead of 19.6 under a dimmed screen.
- Profile 5 frames with the GUI over them: the pixels drawn over are converted in a pass of their own, each pair once and with the picture under them decoded once, three conversions where there were four, with the same bytes: about 60 ms instead of about 83 under the player information on that integrated graphics. Where a GPU cannot run the pass, the transport converts those pixels itself as before (the same bytes, more GPU time) and `kodi.log` says so.
- Where those profile 5 frames still miss refreshes (with *Fidelity step-down* at Automatic), their conversion goes up one level at a time, only as far as needed: first a faster way with the same bytes, then a simplified rounding that can leave a pixel drawn over one code off, which the player information then shows. Frames without GUI elements, profiles 7 and 8.1, and graphics that keep up are not affected.
- The presentation a frame ahead is not switched on or off by a seek, or by menus in the first moment after they appear.
- What the fidelity step-down finds is now remembered also with the default transport (it keeps a level for each set of levels a format can have): a slow GPU misses far fewer refreshes at the start of a video.

**Display and HDMI**

**Audio**

- PCM goes out in no more channels than the display declares in its EDID (*System > Audio > Limit PCM channels to what the display declares*, Expert, on), also through a change of display mode, which takes the audio data of the display away for a moment.
- Where the display takes only stereo PCM but Dolby in more channels, a multichannel track that Kodi decodes goes out as Dolby Digital Plus 5.1 where the display declares it and the output takes it (192 kHz, as Kodi's own transcoding asks), else as Dolby Digital 5.1, passed through, instead of mixed down to stereo (*Surround as Dolby when the display takes only stereo PCM*, on). Where no encoder opens, stereo PCM as before.
- Kodi's own *Enable Dolby Digital transcoding* counts only where *Number of channels* is 2.0, as in Kodi: left on from a 2.0 setting and hidden at 5.1 or 7.1, it no longer encodes to Dolby there.
- An HDMI or DisplayPort audio output that is set but not there is replaced in memory by the one Kodi would take for it, before its formats are asked, so passthrough works; the setting is not changed, and is used again when that output comes back. What the sink opened is in the log.

**Player information and skins**

- A "GPU" row on the overview page, with the use and temperature that the driver gives; rows on the Dolby Vision page for the video scaling method and for the content mapping sent.
- Every skin gets Hades' overview and Dolby Vision pages: where the skin has no such pages, a second press of the key that opens the player process information opens them.
- cinemaONE's two changes of Kodi pull request 29463: opaque menus stay opaque over HDR video, and a small video window shows the video. One case is further from the SDR reference than before: the edge of a white subtitle without an outline straight over the video.

**Interface**

- The mouse pointer keeps the fraction of a pixel of each motion that libinput gives, which Kodi dropped (1000 reports of 0.4 px moved it 0 px; now 400). Of motions that follow one another in the queue only the last is handled, with buttons, the wheel and keys kept in their place and order, and a drag moves as far as before.
- The server of a stream without a type is asked for it on a thread of its own, as Kodi already waits for a plugin there: the menus keep drawing, with the busy dialog after 100 ms, where the interface froze for several seconds each time a film started from a Debrid add-on. The wait cannot be cancelled, as before.
- Once a minute, when the interface was slow, a line `GUI: in the last N s, ...` in `kodi.log` says how many passes took 50 ms or more with no video playing, how long add-ons held the interface, and how many mouse motions were left for the one after them.

**Diagnostics and logs**

- Every file of the diagnostics goes through the filter before it is written: IP and MAC addresses, Wi-Fi names, serial numbers (also inside EDIDs), UUIDs, SSH fingerprints and the name of the PC become markers made with a secret that never leaves the PC, and the names of media are removed.
- "Submit Log" also filters a log that is uploaded.
- The diagnostics are copied to a USB drive with *System > Submit Log > Copy the diagnostics to a USB drive*, or when a USB drive with a folder `hades-diag` at its top is plugged in; the copy is checked file by file. An update with the installer keeps the folder `hades-diag`.
- The diagnostics service cannot use the network.

**System**

- *System > OS Configuration > Hardware clock*: local time (the default, as before) or UTC.
- *Services > Phone remote control*: turns on what Kore or Yatse need, through Kodi's own confirmations, and shows what to enter in the app.
- *Updates*: "Available Versions" says where the versions are published. A signed update channel is built in and stays off: no image has a channel yet.
- The unused blocks of the storage partition are given back to the drive once a week (`fstrim`, idle priority, only that partition).
- Hardening: kernel options against the exploitation of memory bugs, sysctl settings (no ICMP redirects, the kernel log for root only), BusyBox no longer setuid root, the folders that hold secrets for root only, and compiler hardening on FFmpeg, zlib, sqlite, libjpeg-turbo and taglib, each measured within the noise of playback; not applied where it cost (libass and its fonts, libpng, curl).
- The USB4 and Thunderbolt driver, which LibreELEC lost in 2020; PCIe tunnels stay unauthorised.
- MediaTek MT7925 Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, MT7927 Wi-Fi; button maps for Xbox and DualShock 4 controllers.
- The GBM library of NVIDIA's EGL (egl-gbm, MIT) is built from its source instead of copied from NVIDIA's installer.

## 1.0 RC2

### What's new for you

- **Native Dolby Vision rebuilt as the specification defines it.** The picture sent to the TV is now rebuilt exactly as the published Dolby Vision specification describes it, and sent the way certified players send it. Colours, edges and bright highlights are more faithful in every profile (5, 7 FEL, 7 MEL and 8.1).
- **Smoother on modest graphics.** Less GPU work on every frame, and a repeated picture is not drawn again. Only on graphics that cannot keep up does the picture lose a small, measured amount of precision, and only as much as needed; graphics that keep up stay bit-exact.
- **Menus at 120 Hz.** Starting and stopping Dolby Vision, HDR10 and HLG is a single display change on NVIDIA, AMD and Intel, also with the menus at 119.88 or 120 Hz.
- **Turn the TV on after the PC.** The TV's EDID is read again when each video starts, so Dolby Vision works even if the PC started with the TV off, and a later change (Deep Colour turned on, another TV) is seen.
- **New "Overview" page** in the player information: what is sent to the TV (signal, mode, colour depth, the metadata sent), the video, the audio and the system.
- **Projectors and TVs without Dolby Vision:** Dolby Vision shown as HDR10 can be mapped to the brightness of the display (off by default), and the Dolby Vision output can be chosen for each video.
- **Older AMD graphics:** native Dolby Vision also on Raven, Picasso, Navi 1x, Renoir and Cezanne (DCN 1.0 and 2.x).
- **HDR10 at 10 bits** on Intel and AMD where RGB would be 8 bits, in YCbCr 4:2:0, with the BT.2020 YCbCr matrix on Intel.
- **Live TV:** audio passthrough (Atmos included) on live channels and streams (a setting, on by default), a picture on 10-bit channels that showed none, and faster channel changes.
- **Profile 7 MEL** no longer decodes its empty enhancement layer: less work, the same picture, byte for byte. A FEL is always decoded.
- **Up to date and safer:** Linux 7.2.11, Kodi and LibreELEC at their latest commits, and fixes for known security issues in glibc, FFmpeg, curl, libnfs, libass and more.

### Details

**Dolby Vision picture**

- The reconstruction follows the normative composer of ETSI GS CCM 001: identical bit for bit to an integer reference of the specification in every case tested. libplacebo's reshaping, which departs from the specification by 4 to 15 codes on most pixels (and by hundreds at strong colour edges), stays available as an expert setting.
- Sent the way a certified source device sends it: the composer's output in the colour representation of its RPU (IPT for profile 5), with the RPU's own matrices in the display management metadata; menus and subtitles are blended into it, and pixels they do not touch are sent exactly as composed. An expert setting sends it converted to BT.2020, as 1.0 RC1 did.
- Chroma of both layers at the position the stream signals; 4:2:2 taken from the even pixel; exact Lanczos weights for whole-ratio scaling; very bright pixels that came out black are fixed; exact BT.2020, P3 and BT.709 matrices.
- Level 5 describes what is sent, also when menus or subtitles are drawn over the bars; metadata up to 512 bytes; level 11 checked as dovi_tool checks it; scene refresh after a gap.

**Performance**

- Automatic fidelity step-down only where frames are missed (measured per format, remembered per GPU and driver); it can be turned off.
- Repeated pictures are not drawn again; shader cache on disk; GPU programs prepared before the first frame; fewer imports per frame.
- A switch to check on the GPU that frames send the bytes they claim (`<dvverify>` in `advancedsettings.xml`).

**Display and settings**

- A frame whose page flip never comes no longer stalls playback.
- Where the graphics driver refuses native Dolby Vision (Intel and AMD), `kodi.log` and the Dolby Vision page of the player information give its reason.
- Dolby Vision engine settings, Automatic by default.
- DRM PRIME hands back what it cannot decode.

**Updates and security**

- Linux 7.2.11, libplacebo 804dc71cd38d, Kodi Piers up to 01d4146d5d25, LibreELEC master up to 3ca0d49bee40.
- Fixes taken from upstream: glibc 2.44 (nine CVEs), FFmpeg (about ninety), libnfs (CVE-2026-108985), libass (CVE-2026-95127), curl, libbluray, libdvdread, libdvdnav, libarchive, Samba, BusyBox; BlueZ CVE-2026-105460 fixed by Hades.
