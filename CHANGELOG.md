# Changelog

The versions of LibreELEC Hades. Before 1.0 RC1 there were only private test builds.

*Las versiones de LibreELEC Hades, cada una también en español. Antes de 1.0 RC1 solo hubo compilaciones de prueba privadas.*

## 1.0 RC5

### What's new for you

- **Dolby Vision goes out in IPT, as licensed players send it.** Profiles 7 and 8 now reach the TV in IPT-PQ-c2, as licensed players are reported to send every profile (Amlogic's licensed driver; captures of licensed Amlogic and MediaTek players), with the matrices that profile 5 videos carry. The TV no longer converts the composer's YCbCr itself, and the intensity between the pairs of 4:2:2 chroma no longer depends on how it interpolates the chroma. Where a graphics chip cannot make the conversion in time, the automatic fidelity step-down sends the composer's YCbCr at its last level; `kodi.log` says every 10 s how many frames went out each way. The expert setting "As composed" sends the YCbCr as before. Profile 5 is unchanged, byte for byte. The documents no longer call the old default what certified players send ([how it works](docs/how-it-works.md#what-licensed-players-send)).
- **Dolby Vision starts sooner and without losing its first pictures.** The change of mode for a Dolby Vision video now goes out at once, with the Dolby Vision signal already set up (a black frame with valid metadata), before the video's clock starts, instead of with its first picture. The TV sets itself up while the video opens, the first pictures are no longer dropped, and the HDMI audio opens once. Only where nothing is in doubt (Dolby Vision Standard, profiles 5, 7, 8.1 and 8.6, a change of mode that can carry the tunnel); anything else starts as before. `<video><dvstartsignal>false</dvstartsignal></video>` turns it off. Idea from YBLOD. *Not tried on hardware yet.*
- **A seek no longer freezes the menus and the picture for half a second.** A seek from the progress bar of the on-screen display, a big step, a bookmark or `PlayerControl(SeekPercentage)` made Kodi stop drawing until the player had sought the stream, for up to 500 ms each time; over a network every such seek in the hardware test of 1.0 RC4 took the full 500 ms, with or without Dolby Vision, on the AMD integrated graphics and on the RTX 4080 alike, and every step froze the interface and the picture. Kodi now goes on drawing, and the time it shows is the target of the seek while the seek is on its way, as it is once it is done. A run of steps ends in one seek of the stream, and a step asked for while a seek is on its way goes from where that seek goes. The step seeks of the arrow keys already worked this way. Seeks asked for by add-ons and JSON-RPC wait as before. *Not tried on hardware yet.*
- **A stream that runs dry for a moment no longer loses the rest of its group of pictures** (a slow network, a seek over a network), and Dolby Vision FEL keeps its enhancement layer: the decoder starts again at the last random-access point instead of waiting for the next one. `kodi.log` says each time, and how many pictures went out without the FEL layer and why. *Not tried on hardware yet.*
- **Native Dolby Vision in sync with the sound as the ordinary output.** A frame of native output presented the way that waits reaches the screen one refresh sooner after it is chosen than one of the ordinary output, and went out that much before its sound: 50 to 77 ms at 23.976 Hz on the AMD integrated graphics in the test of 1.0 RC4. It is now chosen one refresh later, and a frame presented a frame ahead as the ordinary output; which frames are shown at which refresh rate does not change. Every 10 s `kodi.log` gives the measured A/V offset of each way (`DVBridge: A/V in the last 10 s`), and every minute that of the ordinary output. *Not tried on hardware yet.*
- **Dolby Vision for the menus**, as an option (off by default): while the menus are shown, the TV stays in Dolby Vision, so a Dolby Vision video starts without the TV changing its picture mode, and the menus come back the same way. Any other video (SDR, HDR10, HDR10+, HLG) makes the TV change its mode once more at its start and once more at its end. The menus go out with fixed metadata, at the white of "GUI and subtitle brightness in HDR"; nothing of a video's picture changes. *Dolby Vision for the menus*, in *System > Display*, shown for a TV that takes Dolby Vision, with the menus at 3840x2160 ([how it works](docs/how-it-works.md#dolby-vision-for-the-menus)). The idea is YBLOD's, written again for Hades. Not yet seen on a TV.
- **The menus, the OSD and subtitles over HDR keep their dark tones.** Over HDR10, HLG and Dolby Vision, the GUI was made PQ or HLG through two small tables that darkened its dark greys (at the default white, a grey of sRGB code 13, 5 % of the scale, came out at 0.12 cd/m² where it is 0.82, and the darkest codes black), the more the brighter "GUI and subtitle brightness in HDR" is set. It is now converted exactly, as Kodi's OpenGL output converts it (the sRGB curve, BT.2020, the GUI white): measured within 0.03 codes of 4095 of the exact conversion, where it was up to 452 off at the default white. The video under the GUI does not change; HDR image subtitles of UHD Blu-ray keep their own levels. Measured on a software renderer; not yet seen on a TV.
- **Native Dolby Vision on older AMD graphics,** over HDMI: the Radeon RX 400 and 500 series (Polaris), the Radeon RX Vega M of Intel's Kaby Lake G processors, and the Radeon RX Vega 56 and 64 and the Radeon VII. Nobody has tried it on them yet: if you have one with a Dolby Vision TV, please report what you see, with the diagnostic switch on ([diagnostics](docs/diagnostics.md)), since the kernel log then says what the graphics hold for Dolby Vision.
- **Native Dolby Vision through an HDMI port that NVIDIA drives through a DisplayPort output** (issue #4, HP ZBook 17 G6): since 1.0 RC4 it never started there. Hades waits for the video's mode to be on the output before it starts Dolby Vision on such a port, and a change that is no change of mode (the video's colours) kept it waiting; it now asks the kernel which mode is on the output. On such a port every change of the refresh rate now also turns the output off and on, so that the TV takes the new rate, which it did not always do when the mode changed on a running output. `<video><dualmoderestart>false</dualmoderestart></video>` changes the mode in place there, as before. *Not tried on that laptop yet.*
- **Dolby Vision sent as HDR10+ on Intel at 2160p24, 25 and 30** (issue #2): the first picture now goes with its metadata in the change of mode of the video, which Intel's driver needs at those modes. In 1.0 RC4 every Dolby Vision video went out as HDR10 there. The same on GTX 16 to RTX 40 graphics and with driver 580 where a 4K mode at 24, 25 or 30 Hz goes by its HDMI VIC, so that the mode in the AVI InfoFrame changes with the mode and not a frame after it (untested on a display).
- **HDR10+ on more NVIDIA graphics** (untested on a display): on RTX 50 graphics in a slot of its own, so that the TV is still told the 4K mode at 24, 25 and 30 Hz; on GTX 16 to RTX 40 graphics and with driver 580 at those modes, the mode goes in the AVI InfoFrame instead; and on GTX 16, RTX 20 and RTX 30 graphics at 50 and 60 Hz to a TV with HDMI variable refresh, which is left off in the change of mode of the video so that HDR10+ can go out.
- **HDR10+ to displays of application version 0** (untested on a display): such a display is now seen, and sent metadata of version 0, also from Dolby Vision sent as HDR10+. Films of version 1 go to it as HDR10, as licensed players do.
- **Dolby Vision sent as HDR10+ measures every percentile**: the places of 5 and 10 % carried fixed values ([how it works](docs/how-it-works.md#dolby-vision-sent-as-hdr10)).
- **Dolby Vision sent as HDR10+: the next refresh asked another way first** (issue #10): the output's CRTC by its id, then the old way. On an RX 7600, 1.0 RC4 found no time to wait by at the start of a video, for a reason not known yet; where neither way answers, `kodi.log` now says the error of each (untested on that GPU).
- **Menus over a Dolby Vision video cost less GPU time where they are opaque and still** (Home, a dialog, the player information): the packed bytes of the parts of the screen where the menu is opaque and did not change are copied from the frame before instead of being converted again, with the same bytes. Idea from YBLOD. *Not measured on hardware yet.*
- **Dual-track Dolby Vision MP4 files whose enhancement track is an ordinary HEVC track** (`hvc1` or `hev1` with its Dolby Vision record) play with their enhancement layer instead of as HDR10.
- **3D subtitles at the depth the disc asks for**, frame by frame, on 3D Blu-ray discs and MVC remuxes that carry it, instead of at the depth of the screen (not seen on a 3D display yet).
- **The Dolby Vision page of the player information fits its panel** as the other pages do, instead of running down over the seek bar: it is now two pages, *Dolby Vision* (what the video is and how it goes out) and *Dolby Vision: pictures* (their metadata, and how they are drawn and shown), with every row it had.
- **The output of a Dolby Vision video can be chosen from the player information**: *Dolby Vision output (this video)* on the Dolby Vision page, down from its tab, opens the same list as the player's video settings. Skins and keymaps can use it too: `PlayerControl(HadesDVOutput)`.
- **`kodi.log` says what each seek took**, without debug logging: from where to where, when the player began, where the stream got to and when, and when the first frame after it went to the screen. The lines of every 10 s of native Dolby Vision output leave the seeks out of their gaps and late presentations and count them apart: the gaps of half a second that the integrated AMD graphics showed in the test of 1.0 RC4 were seeks.
- **`kodi.log` says what can cut out the sound of a passthrough stream** (issue #8): an underrun that the device played past, the corrections of the sync that put pauses in or leave packets out, a change of what the display says of its audio (its ELD), and the closing and opening of the output that follows, with how long it took. The diagnostics record whether a laptop is on its charger, and the PCIe link, power and clock limits of an NVIDIA card, every 10 s.
- **The "Installer" boot entry gives the installed system the boot loader and the boot entries of Hades,** as the Windows installer does: Hades' GRUB built from source instead of syslinux's prebuilt EFI loader, Run and Live entries that keep the other internal disks away and the kernel locked down, the entry with the internal disks read-only, the diagnostics switch, and the notice and licences. Before, a system installed that way saw every other internal disk. A disk that the built-in installer gives an MBR partition table (a start in legacy BIOS, a disk under 2.2 TB) starts in UEFI too, and a step of the installer that fails ends in "Install Failed" with what failed, never in "Install Complete". Its window title, its menu and its dialogs say "LibreELEC Hades".
- **Up to date and safer:** LibreELEC at its latest commit; nine commits of Kodi's master, among them fixes for a stack buffer overflow in the WS-Discovery listener (on by default), a heap buffer overflow in the closed-caption decoder and `resource://` paths that went up out of their folder; Mesa 26.2.6, which fixes code that its ACO compiler compiled wrongly on RDNA2 to RDNA4 graphics (the integrated graphics of the Ryzen 7000 among them); tz 2026f and libjpeg-turbo 3.2.1; fixes for two known security issues in expat; and fixes from the branches of OpenSSL, curl, OpenSSH, BlueZ, avahi and libbluray after their releases.

### Details

**Dolby Vision picture and metadata**

- The native HDR10+ of a film goes out as it is in every shape of metadata, also the four that another build refuses (issue 62 of CroqueMr's fork), tested on the host.
- Dolby Vision as HDR10 mapped to the display, Dolby Vision to SDR and Low-Latency take the level 3 offsets of content mapping v4.0 into account (SMPTE ST 2094-10 6.1.6 to 6.1.9), and leave out those that break 6.1.9; `kodi.log` says the offsets used. With the streams tested the picture is the same, bit for bit.
- Expert, off by default: `<video><dvcm40default>true</dvcm40default></video>` in `advancedsettings.xml` sends an RPU without a content mapping v4.0 part with dovi_tool's default one (levels 3, 9, 11 and 254; no level 8 trims), to see what a display of display management 4 does with it.
- Expert, off by default: `<video><dvmeasurednoavgfloor>true</dvmeasurednoavgfloor></video>` sends the level 1 average of HDR10 measured as Dolby Vision without dovi_tool's floor of 819 (Dolby's own analysis writes lower averages for dark scenes), to be judged on a display.

**Performance**

- The GUI cache ([how it works](docs/how-it-works.md#two-ways-of-drawing-a-frame)): a tile of 16x16 pixels whose GUI texels are opaque and the same as at the frame before keeps the bytes that it was packed with; translucent pixels are composed and converted at every frame. Measured with the software bridge: the same bytes as 1.0 RC4 on every picture and configuration of the test bench. `<video><dvguicache>false</dvguicache></video>` turns it off, `<video><dvguicachecheck>true</dvguicachecheck></video>` checks it on a graphics chip.
- Studied and not taken: merging identical contiguous MMR pivot spans (an idea of CroqueMr's). None of 18,688 RPUs measured has a chroma MMR curve of more than one piece, the only place where the CCM 001 composer picks a piece per sample.

**Display and HDMI**

- AMD display hardware DCE 11.2 (Polaris), 11.22 (Vega M) and 12.0 and 12.1 (Vega 10, 12 and 20): native Dolby Vision, with Low-Latency and the HDMI 1.4b signalling as on DCN (`linux-9997`, `linux-9998`). There the display core chooses by itself a line buffer of 30 bits per pixel, an input prescale that scales 8-bit surfaces by 1 + 1/256, an input CSC that it never programs (it keeps what the VBIOS left) and an output CSC as an identity matrix. For a tunnelled stream it now programs a line buffer of 24 bits and the other three in bypass, whatever the update bits of the plane, and gives a pipe that leaves the tunnel what it had before. Once the display core has validated a commit, amdgpu checks what it will program for the tunnel and refuses it otherwise, with the reason; each time the tunnel goes on, the kernel log has what the pipe holds, read back from its registers. CroqueMr's fork offers it on Vega M alone; Polaris runs the same display code. 2160p at 50 and 60 Hz needs HDMI at 6 Gbps, which the VBIOS of each card enables or not. A host test builds the display core's own files on a fake register file: without the tunnel they write the same registers in the same order as before; with it, eight registers differ from the same stream without it, and the pipe is as before once the tunnel ends; 30 mutations of the two patches make it fail.
- Intel (`linux-9999`): a commit without a change of mode that would bring the first HDR10+ at 2160p24, 25 or 30 is refused as before, now with the reason in the kernel log; `kodi.log` names it too when the driver refused 8 commits.
- NVIDIA: `nvidia-open-kernel-modules-9049` (HDR10+ in generic InfoFrame slot 9 on RTX 50), `9050` and `nvidia-legacy-open-9062` (the CTA VIC in the AVI InfoFrame next to HDR10+), `9051` (`NV_HDMI_VRR_OFF`), `9052` and `nvidia-legacy-open-9063` (`NV_HDR10PLUS_AVI_CTA_VIC`: where the first HDR10+ goes in the change of mode).

**Files and streams**

- MP4: libavformat groups a Dolby Vision enhancement track in an `hvc1` or `hev1` sample entry when its record is that of a profile 7 enhancement layer and its `vdep` reference names one track and no other (`ffmpeg-9930`); the `dvhe` and `dvh1` entries are grouped as before.
- MPEG-TS and Blu-ray: an HEVC picture whose parameter sets came in a PES of their own without a time takes the time of the PES that its first slice begins in, where no other picture begins in that PES (`ffmpeg-9931`); a time stays with the picture it belongs to, and none is made up.
- 3D: the plane offsets of the MVC dependent view are read and applied to the subtitles, as Dom Cobley's and cinemaONE's commits do in popcornmix's 3D branch of Kodi; where nothing describes a frame, the depth setting of the subtitles applies, and in 2D nothing moves.
- The drop control of the video decoder no longer compares a picture with an unknown time (the first one after every seek): that computed a signed overflow. H.264 MVC was checked to be counted by whole pictures, not by eyes.
- After Kodi drains the video decoder (no packet for ten periods), a packet that comes waits until every picture is out (3 s at most); then an HEVC decoder starts again at the last random-access point, whose packets it keeps (300, or 64 MiB, at most: 5 s at 60 frames a second, 12.5 s at 24), is given them again one by one, and only the pictures made from those packets are left out: they went out before the drain. A picture of a packet that came after the drain goes out, whatever its time. Not for MVC or still pictures; without a kept point it goes on as before, and `kodi.log` says why. The FEL layer is drained only once the base layer has received its end, and its drain passes over an access unit whose base packet has not come yet. Measured on the host with the module itself: the 9 cases lost 4 to 21 pictures before, and none now; with the times going back 1 s after a drain without a flush (live TV, a discontinuity of HLS), leaving out by time lost 23 of 60, and the mark none; the FEL suites give the same bytes as before.

**Player information and skins**

- Labels for any skin: `Player.Process(videooutputhdrtype)` (what the display is sent: `dolbyvision`, `hdr10plus`, `hdr10`, `hlg` or `sdr`), `videodvoutput`, `videodvreason`, `videodvlayers` and `videodvoutputchoice`.

**Interface**

- Text no longer takes a level of the modelview stack that it never pushed (`GUIFontTTFGLES`); nothing drew wrong for it yet in Hades.

**Diagnostics and logs**

- Five lines of `kodi.log` that said "error" for what is not a fault are told once, at the level they deserve, and a real error is still an error: an add-on naming its own window by its file (`Can't find window sources_results.xml`), a list asked to take the focus before it has items, NVIDIA's VAAPI driver without video post-processing (`vaQueryVideoProcFilters`), the audio engine taking no data while the stream waits to start (`AddPacketsRenderer - timeout`; still an error while the stream plays), and an add-on asking for one that is not installed (`Unknown addon id`; the script still gets its error).
- The enhancement layer of profile 7 FEL, started again after the decoders were drained while the stream had run dry, says why instead of "unknown".
- The report of a slow pass of the interface says how much of it a change of the display mode took (the passes of half a second to one and a half at the start and the end of a video, which are the kernel and the TV setting up the output).

**Updates and security**

- LibreELEC `master` up to `0314a6cb55d0`: tz 2026f, libjpeg-turbo 3.2.1 (CVE-2026-75466, an overflow in a partial decompression of 4:2:0 without SIMD) and, for the build host, flit_core 4.2.0 and hatchling 1.33.0. Mesa 26.2.6.
- Kodi's master, nine commits with their authors: the stack buffer overflow of WS-Discovery's UDP receive path, the heap buffer overflow of `cc_to_text`, no `resource://` path through a parent segment (ported to `CResourceFile::TranslatePath()`, which every `resource://` file and folder goes through), JSON-RPC registered before anything can call it, a DTS core frame confirmed before it is taken as the stream, RGB with no range given taken as full range, a picture's display size without a mask, the result of a slow renderer configure used, and no crash adding the first extra of a movie.
- Fixes after the releases in use: OpenSSL 4.1 `openssl-9908` to `9910` (the data pointer of an SSL BIO reset in `ssl_free()`, a read out of bounds of verify names without NUL in x509_vpm, a double free in `openssl pkcs12 -jdktrust`) and the first of them for OpenSSL 3.5, `openssl3-9913`; curl `curl-9906` to `9908` (the reader stack cleared when a line conversion cannot be set up, and two checks of connections before they are used again); OpenSSH `openssh-9904` (glob.c of OpenBSD's revision 1.51: a read past the pattern, which sftp and scp reach); BlueZ `bluez-9038` to `9042` (9042, btmon's stack overflow in `packet_hci_scodata()`, ported to 5.87); avahi `avahi-9044` and `9045` (9044: the receive options of its sockets set before they are bound, without which their first packets were lost on Linux); libbluray `libbluray-9037` (BD-J `getMediaTime()` at rate 0); expat `expat-9024` to `9027`, from pull requests not merged yet (CVE-2026-117610, a NULL dereference in a subparser, and CVE-2026-118370, an overflow with more than 2^30 attributes).

### En español

#### Novedades

- **Dolby Vision sale en IPT, como lo envían los reproductores con licencia.** Los perfiles 7 y 8 llegan ahora al televisor en IPT-PQ-c2, como se informa que los reproductores con licencia envían todos los perfiles (el controlador con licencia de Amlogic; capturas de reproductores con licencia de Amlogic y MediaTek), con las matrices que llevan los vídeos de perfil 5. El televisor ya no convierte por su cuenta el YCbCr del compositor, y la intensidad entre los pares de la croma 4:2:2 ya no depende de cómo interpola la croma. Donde una gráfica no llega a hacer la conversión a tiempo, la reducción automática de fidelidad envía el YCbCr del compositor en su último nivel; `kodi.log` dice cada 10 s cuántas imágenes salieron de cada forma. El ajuste experto "Tal como se compone" envía el YCbCr como antes. El perfil 5 no cambia, byte a byte. La documentación ya no llama al valor por defecto anterior "lo que envían los reproductores certificados" ([cómo funciona](docs/how-it-works.md#what-licensed-players-send), en inglés).
- **Dolby Vision empieza antes y sin perder sus primeras imágenes.** El cambio de modo de un vídeo Dolby Vision sale ahora enseguida, con la señal Dolby Vision ya puesta (un fotograma negro con metadatos válidos), antes de que arranque el reloj del vídeo, y no con su primera imagen. La tele se prepara mientras el vídeo se abre, las primeras imágenes ya no se descartan y el audio HDMI se abre una sola vez. Solo donde no hay ninguna duda (Dolby Vision Standard, perfiles 5, 7, 8.1 y 8.6, un cambio de modo que puede llevar el túnel); lo demás empieza como antes. `<video><dvstartsignal>false</dvstartsignal></video>` lo apaga. Idea de YBLOD. *Sin probar todavía en hardware.*
- **Un salto ya no congela los menús y la imagen medio segundo.** Un salto desde la barra de progreso del menú en pantalla, un paso grande, un marcador o `PlayerControl(SeekPercentage)` hacía que Kodi dejara de dibujar hasta que el reproductor había saltado en el flujo, hasta 500 ms cada vez; por red, cada salto así de la prueba de 1.0 RC4 tardó los 500 ms enteros, con Dolby Vision o sin él, en la integrada AMD y en la RTX 4080 por igual, y cada paso congelaba la interfaz y la imagen. Ahora Kodi sigue dibujando, y el tiempo que muestra es el destino del salto mientras el salto está en camino, como cuando ya se ha hecho. Una serie de pasos termina en un solo salto del flujo, y un paso pedido mientras otro salto está en camino parte de adonde va ese salto. Los pasos con las flechas ya funcionaban así. Los saltos que piden los complementos y JSON-RPC esperan como antes. *Sin probar todavía en hardware.*
- **Un flujo que se queda sin datos un momento ya no pierde el resto de su grupo de imágenes** (red lenta, salto por red), y Dolby Vision FEL conserva la capa de mejora: el decodificador vuelve a empezar en el último punto de acceso en vez de esperar al siguiente. `kodi.log` lo dice cada vez, y cuántas imágenes salieron sin la capa FEL y por qué. *Sin probar todavía en hardware.*
- **El Dolby Vision nativo, en sincronía con el sonido como la salida normal.** Un fotograma del DV nativo presentado de la forma que espera llega a la pantalla un refresco antes después de elegirse que uno de la salida normal, y salía ese tanto antes que su sonido: de 50 a 77 ms a 23,976 Hz en la integrada AMD en la prueba de 1.0 RC4. Ahora se elige un refresco más tarde, y uno presentado un fotograma por adelantado, como la salida normal; qué fotogramas se muestran a qué frecuencia no cambia. Cada 10 s `kodi.log` da el desfase medido entre imagen y sonido de cada forma (`DVBridge: A/V in the last 10 s`), y cada minuto el de la salida normal. *Sin probar todavía en hardware.*
- **Dolby Vision para los menús**, como opción (apagada por defecto): mientras se muestran los menús, la tele se queda en Dolby Vision, así que un vídeo Dolby Vision empieza sin que la tele cambie de modo de imagen, y los menús vuelven igual. Cualquier otro vídeo (SDR, HDR10, HDR10+, HLG) hace que la tele cambie de modo una vez más al empezar y otra más al terminar. Los menús salen con metadatos fijos, con el blanco de «Brillo de la interfaz y los subtítulos en HDR»; nada de la imagen de un vídeo cambia. *Dolby Vision para los menús*, en *Sistema > Pantalla*, visible para una tele que admite Dolby Vision, con los menús a 3840x2160 ([cómo funciona](docs/how-it-works.md#dolby-vision-for-the-menus), en inglés). La idea es de YBLOD, escrita de nuevo para Hades. Aún no visto en una tele.
- **Los menús, el OSD y los subtítulos sobre HDR conservan sus tonos oscuros.** Sobre HDR10, HLG y Dolby Vision, la interfaz se pasaba a PQ o HLG con dos tablas pequeñas que oscurecían sus grises oscuros (con el blanco por defecto, un gris de código sRGB 13, el 5 % de la escala, salía con 0,12 cd/m² donde son 0,82, y los códigos más oscuros, negros), tanto más cuanto más alto está «Brillo de la interfaz y los subtítulos en HDR». Ahora se convierte exactamente, como la convierte la salida OpenGL de Kodi (la curva sRGB, BT.2020, el blanco de la interfaz): medido a 0,03 códigos de 4095 de la conversión exacta, donde antes llegaba a 452 con el blanco por defecto. El vídeo bajo la interfaz no cambia; los subtítulos de imagen HDR de los UHD Blu-ray conservan sus propios niveles. Medido con un dibujado por software; aún no visto en una tele.
- **Dolby Vision nativo en gráficas AMD más antiguas,** por HDMI: las Radeon series RX 400 y RX 500 (Polaris), la Radeon RX Vega M de los procesadores Kaby Lake G de Intel, y las Radeon RX Vega 56 y 64 y la Radeon VII. Nadie lo ha probado todavía en ellas: si tienes una con una tele Dolby Vision, cuenta lo que veas, con el interruptor de diagnóstico encendido ([diagnóstico](docs/diagnostics.md), en inglés), porque el registro del kernel dice entonces qué tiene la gráfica para el Dolby Vision.
- **Dolby Vision nativo por un HDMI que NVIDIA saca por una salida DisplayPort** (issue #4, HP ZBook 17 G6): desde 1.0 RC4 no llegaba a empezar. Hades espera a que el modo del vídeo esté puesto antes de empezar el Dolby Vision en un puerto así, y un cambio que no es de modo (los colores del vídeo) lo dejaba esperando; ahora pregunta al núcleo qué modo hay puesto. Además, en ese puerto cada cambio de frecuencia apaga y enciende la salida, para que la tele tome la frecuencia nueva, cosa que no siempre hacía cuando el modo cambiaba con la salida encendida. `<video><dualmoderestart>false</dualmoderestart></video>` vuelve a cambiar el modo sin apagar, como antes. *Sin probar todavía en ese portátil.*
- **Dolby Vision enviado como HDR10+ en Intel a 2160p24, 25 y 30** (issue #2): la primera imagen sale ahora con sus metadatos en el cambio de modo del vídeo, que el controlador de Intel necesita en esos modos. ### Details

**Dolby Vision picture and metadata**

- A picture that is zoomed, shifted vertically or stretched, or scaled up to the screen, is scaled in the representation of the composer's picture with Lanczos 3 from its integer codes, the luma and the chroma (from 4:2:0 straight to the 4:2:2 of the tunnel), with the same bytes on any graphics, and level 5 describes the part shown. A picture drawn smaller than its own size is still rebuilt in RGB and converted to BT.2020. Measured with the bridge running in software against the 4K master, a picture scaled up this way is closer to it than converted to BT.2020 as before (average ΔE ITP 0.349 to 0.266 and 0.382 to 0.312 on two profile 5 film pictures, 1.312 to 1.170 on a profile 5 chart).
- Dolby Vision sent as HDR10+ (`videoscreen.hadesdvashdr10plus`, off): SMPTE ST 2094-40 application version 1, one window, the luminance parameters only (the average of maxRGB and its distribution) and no tone curve, so that the display maps each scene from them. They are measured on the GPU from a grid of up to 960x540 pixels of the film's area (the bars left out) and read back a frame or more later; the metadata of a frame are those of its scene so far, and the first picture of a scene waits for its own measurement where that comes before the next vblank. Within a scene they change only when a value moves by more than 32 codes of 4095 in PQ, and then at most every 24 pictures unless it moves by more than 64. Profile 8.1 is rebuilt too while it is on. `kodi.log` and the Dolby Vision page say when it goes out ([how it works](docs/how-it-works.md#dolby-vision-sent-as-hdr10)).
- The help of "Tone mapping curve" (Dolby Vision as HDR10 mapped to the display) says, as measured, that libplacebo's spline darkens the shadows of a shot as well as its middle tones, without taking them to black: below 1 cd/m², mapped to a display of 600 cd/m², a ramp of PQ codes comes out 35 % darker, profile 5 film pictures 15 % and profile 7 FEL pictures 10 %. With BT.2390, the default, the ramp comes out as it is.
- Profile 5, and AV1 profile 10 with a base layer compatible with nothing, are rebuilt for output as HDR10 wherever native output is not possible, whatever "Dolby Vision as HDR10 when needed" says; HDR10+ and "map to the display" follow that setting as their help says.
- Profile 8.4: composed by the composer of the specification, or by libplacebo's reshaping, where the RPU of the picture declares its HDR picture in PQ (signal_eotf 0xFFFF), decided picture by picture; otherwise it plays as HLG (`libplacebo-9975`).
- Profile 8.2 (an SDR base layer): composed at 14 bits and converted to PQ as clause 5.5 of ETSI GS CCM 001 says (`libplacebo-9955`); never by libplacebo's reshaping, which would take its base layer for PQ. Where the composer does not take it, it plays as Kodi plays it, and `kodi.log` says why.
- An enhancement layer coded larger than its picture (1088 rows for 1080, for example) is imported at the size of the picture: the rows beyond it were read by the resampling.
- A content mapping v4.0 part that cannot be carried (a block of a length or value that dovi_tool does not know, or one that would take the metadata of a frame past 512 bytes) is left out for the rest of the stream, which keeps native output with the v2.9 part; `kodi.log` says which block and why. Before, every frame was refused, and native output stopped.
- The metadata of a frame that cannot be sent say why in `kodi.log`.
- Where an RPU has no level 5 block but has a content mapping v4.0 part, the level 5 block that Hades makes goes at the end of the v2.9 part, where an RPU carries its own.
- The enhancement layer: a paired picture whose enhancement picture brought no RPU takes the base decoder's reading of the RPU; the layer starts by picture order only where that can be known; the start codes of a packet are found once.
- Dolby Vision shown as HDR10 in a 10-bit window is kept as the codes of that window (`libplacebo-9946`): the same bytes, with less GPU memory and bandwidth.
- libplacebo's own reshaping (the expert setting "Composition", and the pictures that the composer of the specification does not take) dequantizes the enhancement layer on its value as the scaler gives it, without the rounding to an integer that libplacebo 7.387.0 brought (`libplacebo-9978`). Measured against the reference of the specification, the rounding took the decoded picture further from it at every step-down level (61.0 % of its pixels a code or more off instead of 56.7 %), and at levels 2 and 3 it made the two ways of drawing a frame differ in 5.5 % of their chroma codes, by up to 2, instead of 0.3 %, by 1. The composer of the specification, the default, does not use it.
- The expert setting "Finer gamut mapping" (*System > Display > Dolby Vision engine*) maps through a table of 256x192x512 entries instead of 96x64x512; where 16-bit unorm textures cannot be filtered, the table is kept in floats.

**Performance**

- Frames presented the way that waits that miss their refresh again and again (two of the last three runs of 240 frames without GUI elements with three late or more) are presented a frame ahead, whatever their average GPU time.
- Frames with menus, the OSD or a window over a picture that draws the residual of an enhancement layer step down on their own, by the same rule as the frames without them, only while the GUI is over the picture.
- In the enhancement layer's resampling, the vertical passes read only the rows they use (`libplacebo-9970`) and use half floats where those are exact (`libplacebo-9971`); the planes composed for the transport are 16-bit (`libplacebo-9960`); the two rows of 4:2:2 of a row of 4:2:0 are made in one fragment. The same bytes.
- On nvidia-drm, which has no vertical blank to wait for with its defaults, a repeated picture is kept by committing it again with a commit that waits for its flip, instead of drawn again every refresh.
- The GPU work of a frame of the way that waits is finished after its buffer swap, as in the other ways, and a native frame is committed without a test commit first.
- The windows that open over a video (the OSD, the player information, Settings, the player's video, audio and subtitle settings, and the full-screen information) are loaded one at a time while the menus are idle, so that their first opening over a video does not take a refresh.
- The decoder of the enhancement layer keeps a fixed pool of 32 surfaces with NVIDIA's VAAPI driver, and FFmpeg's pool, which grows as needed, with any other; while the layer holds 32, its next access unit waits instead of finding no surface.
- A picture scaled up to the screen (a 1080p video) is more faithful in the source transport and asks for more GPU time (on Mesa's zink, 35 ms against 19 to 20 for the direct way): where the graphics cannot keep up, from level 1 of the step-down on it goes out converted to BT.2020, as earlier builds sent it.

**System**

- *Internal partitions that are shown*, under *System > Internal disks* of the LibreELEC settings: partitions are told by the WWN and serial number of their disk and their identifier in the partition table; where the list cannot be read, no internal partition is mounted.

- A list of the disks that cannot be read when the init looks again, after the system's storage is mounted, keeps every internal disk away, as one that cannot be read at the first look, and the settings say so.
- Bluetooth: the LibreELEC settings write why each device disconnected to `kodi.log`, and say once on screen when a device refused the key saved for it.

**Imagen y metadatos Dolby Vision**

- Una imagen con zoom, desplazada en vertical o estirada, o ampliada a la pantalla, se escala en la representación de la imagen del compositor con Lanczos 3 desde sus códigos enteros, la luma y la croma (de 4:2:0 directamente al 4:2:2 del túnel), con los mismos bytes en cualquier gráfica, y el nivel 5 describe la parte que se ve. Una imagen dibujada más pequeña que su propio tamaño se sigue reconstruyendo en RGB y convirtiendo a BT.2020. Medido con el puente funcionando por software frente al máster 4K, una imagen ampliada así está más cerca de él que convertida a BT.2020 como antes (ΔE ITP medio de 0,349 a 0,266 y de 0,382 a 0,312 en dos imágenes de película de perfil 5, de 1,312 a 1,170 en una carta de perfil 5).

Se miden en la GPU sobre una rejilla de hasta 960x540 píxeles de la zona de la película (sin las bandas) y se leen un fotograma o más después; los metadatos de un fotograma son los de su escena hasta ese momento, y la primera imagen de una escena espera a su propia medida donde esta llega antes del siguiente borrado vertical. Dentro de una escena solo cambian cuando un valor se mueve más de 32 códigos de 4095 en PQ, y entonces como mucho cada 24 imágenes salvo que se mueva más de 64. El perfil 8.1 también se reconstruye mientras está encendido. `kodi.log` y la página Dolby Vision dicen cuándo sale ([cómo funciona](docs/how-it-works.md#dolby-vision-sent-as-hdr10), en inglés).

**Rendimiento**

- Los fotogramas presentados de la forma que espera que pierden su refresco una y otra vez (dos de las tres últimas tandas de 240 fotogramas sin elementos de la interfaz con tres tardíos o más) se presentan un fotograma por adelantado, sea cual sea su tiempo medio de GPU.
- Los fotogramas con menús, el OSD o una ventana sobre una imagen que dibuja el residuo de una capa de mejora bajan de nivel por su cuenta, con la misma regla que los fotogramas sin ellos, solo mientras la interfaz está sobre la imagen.
- En el remuestreo de la capa de mejora, las pasadas verticales leen solo las filas que usan (`libplacebo-9970`) y usan coma flotante de 16 bits donde es exacta (`libplacebo-9971`); los planos compuestos para el transporte son de 16 bits (`libplacebo-9960`); las dos filas de 4:2:2 de una fila de 4:2:0 se hacen en un solo fragmento. Los mismos bytes.
- En nvidia-drm, que con sus valores por defecto no tiene borrado vertical que esperar, una imagen repetida se conserva volviendo a hacer su commit con un commit que espera su cambio de página, en vez de dibujarse otra vez en cada refresco.
- El trabajo de GPU de un fotograma de la forma que espera se termina después de su intercambio de búferes, como en las otras formas, y un fotograma nativo se confirma sin un commit de prueba antes.
- Las ventanas que se abren sobre un vídeo (el OSD, la información del reproductor, Ajustes, los ajustes de vídeo, audio y subtítulos del reproductor, y la información a pantalla completa) se cargan de una en una mientras los menús están en reposo, para que la primera vez que se abren sobre un vídeo no tarden un refresco.
- El decodificador de la capa de mejora tiene un conjunto fijo de 32 superficies con el controlador VAAPI de NVIDIA, y el de FFmpeg, que crece según hace falta, con cualquier otro; mientras la capa retiene 32, su siguiente unidad de acceso espera en vez de quedarse sin superficie.
- Una imagen ampliada a la pantalla (un vídeo de 1080p) es más fiel en el transporte de la fuente y pide más tiempo de GPU (en zink de Mesa, 35 ms frente a 19 a 20 de la vía directa): donde la gráfica no llega, desde el nivel 1 de la reducción sale convertida a BT.2020, como la enviaban las compilaciones anteriores.

**Sistema**

Los menús de arranque, la entrada que muestra los discos internos y un sistema instalado en un disco interno no cambian.
- Un disco que la lista aparta pero que se queda conectado (su controlador no lo soltó, o no se pudo saber con certeza desde qué disco arranca el sistema) se nombra al arrancar Kodi y en la lista, y en ese arranque no se monta ninguna partición de un disco interno; dos discos con el mismo modelo y el mismo número de serie se apartan los dos mientras haya lista; Run y Live dejan la marca de los discos ocultos antes que nada.
- Una lista de los discos que no se puede leer cuando el init vuelve a mirar, con el almacenamiento del sistema ya montado, aparta todos los discos internos, igual que una que no se puede leer la primera vez, y los ajustes lo dicen.
- Bluetooth: los ajustes de LibreELEC escriben en `kodi.log` por qué se desconectó cada dispositivo, y dicen una vez en pantalla cuándo un dispositivo rechazó la clave guardada para él.
- El firmware del controlador `xe` de Intel (`xe/` de linux-firmware) está en el sistema; los textos de licencia del firmware que va dentro del kernel están en `licenses/`, y el sistema tiene el `WHENCE` y los textos de licencia de linux-firmware en `/usr/share/licenses/kernel-firmware`.
- Interruptores expertos para comparar en una pantalla, apagados por defecto: `<dvmmrtable>` (el mapeo MMR del compositor con una tabla, los mismos bytes) y `<dveos>` (un fotograma de transporte negro que marca el final de la señal), en `advancedsettings.xml`; ver [los ajustes](docs/settings.md#advancedsettingsxml) (en inglés).

## 1.0 RC3

### What's new for you

- **Menus over Dolby Vision cost far less.** With a menu, the OSD, the seek bar or subtitles over native Dolby Vision, each frame is now remade only where they are drawn, with the same bytes sent to the TV. On the integrated graphics of the Ryzen 9 7950X3D, the seek bar over a profile 7 film takes 17.8 ms of GPU time instead of 30.5, of the 41.7 ms that each frame has (measured through Direct3D 12 under Windows). The measurement of what is drawn over the black bars, which cost about 40 ms a frame on a Radeon RX 7600 and made films with a menu over them play at about 16 frames a second (issue #3), is now made in blocks. Profile 5, whose pixels under a menu cost the most, has a pass of its own for them.
- **Dolby Vision Low-Latency (player-led), as an option.** For a TV input that offers Dolby Vision only in its low-latency form (the game mode of some TVs, or a TV that has no other), Hades can now send it that way, mapping the picture to the display itself. Off by default. The mapping is Hades' own, not Dolby's.
- **HDR10+ to displays that take it.** The dynamic metadata of each frame of an HDR10+ video now go out with that frame to a display that declares HDR10+, on NVIDIA, AMD and Intel graphics, within the limits of each driver (see the details below).
- **Other formats as Dolby Vision, as options (off by default):** an HDR10+ video to a TV that takes Dolby Vision but not HDR10+, with metadata made from its own HDR10+ metadata; and an HDR10 video, with metadata that Hades measures from the picture as it plays and that say so: they are not the studio's.
- **For projectors:** Dolby Vision can be sent without its trims, for a display that shows washed-out blacks or wrong colours with them (off by default).
- **HDR and Dolby Vision on a display without HDR.** HDR10, HLG and Dolby Vision profiles 5, 7 and 8.1 are mapped to SDR as a reference display maps them (ITU-R BT.2390), now the default tone mapping method: measured, much closer to the master than Kodi's own methods. Profile 5 on such a display no longer comes out green and purple.
- **More Dolby Vision files play as Dolby Vision:** profile 7 in two video tracks of MKV and MP4 files (issues #1 and #5), and HEVC streams that carry Dolby Vision without a record of it in their container.
- **More TVs and graphics:** Dolby Vision TVs that read display management 2 or 3 (many of 2017 and 2018), Radeon RX 9000 graphics (RDNA 4), Intel UHD 6xx graphics, and the HDMI port of laptops whose NVIDIA graphics drive it through a dual-mode DisplayPort connector.
- **Better HDR10 on AMD and NVIDIA:** on AMD, HDR10 in YCbCr 4:2:2 at 12 bits where RGB would be 8 bits; on NVIDIA GeForce GTX 16 and RTX 20 graphics, HDR over HDMI at 12 bits instead of 8 where the mode fits (off by default: `nvidia-modeset.hades_turing_12bpc=1` on the kernel line turns it on).
- **Fewer black frames and display changes:** Dolby Vision shown as HDR10 keeps the last whole picture where one fails; the next video of a list at another frame rate changes the display once; the menus keep moving while a change of display waits; an NVIDIA output whose display stops answering is turned off and on until it comes back; and after a wake from suspend the mode is set again where NVIDIA's driver had left the output off (a laptop had no picture after a wake).
- **Sound:** PCM goes out in no more channels than the TV says it takes, also through a change of display mode (a TV that declared 2 channels of PCM could be sent more, the likely cause of a DTS-HD track that played no sound; an Expert setting turns it off); where the TV takes only stereo PCM but Dolby in more channels, a multichannel track that Kodi decodes goes out as Dolby Digital Plus or Dolby Digital 5.1 instead of stereo (a setting, on by default); and an HDMI audio output that is set but not there no longer keeps passthrough off.
- **The mouse and the menus:** the pointer keeps the fractions of a pixel of each motion (a slow movement moved it less than it should, or not at all), and motions that pile up are handled as one; when a film starts from an add-on, the menus keep drawing, with the busy dialog, while the server of the stream is asked what the stream is, instead of freezing for seconds; and `kodi.log` says once a minute when the interface was slow.
- **Choosing the monitor:** *System > Display > Monitor*, which LibreELEC hides, is shown again, with each display by its name. A change to a display of the same graphics takes effect at once; one on other graphics starts Kodi again. Either way Kodi asks whether to keep it, and goes back if not. Not while something plays.
- **Diagnostics that can be shared:** addresses, serial numbers, network names and the names of films become markers or are removed, in every file; the diagnostics can be copied to a USB drive, also with nothing on the screen, and an update with the installer keeps them.
- **Easier to live with:** a phone remote control in one step, a setting for a PC whose clock keeps UTC, a weekly trim of the storage partition, Hades' player information in any skin, with the GPU's use and temperature, and hardening measured to cost nothing in playback.
- **Up to date and safer:** LibreELEC and Kodi at their latest commits, libplacebo, LLVM, systemd and kernel firmware, and fixes for known security issues in FFmpeg, OpenSSH, Python, curl, BlueZ, libarchive and libtiff, with four fixes of Hades' own for libbluray, libdisplay-info and libdvdnav.

### Before you update

- **AMD graphics with a TV that cannot select the RGB range.** AMD graphics now send such a TV limited-range RGB, as the TV expects (the change is AMD's). If you turned on Kodi's *System > Display > Use limited colour range (16-235)* because blacks were crushed on such a TV, turn it off, or the range is compressed twice. A TV that selects the range, as the LG C5 does, gets what it got before.
- **Tone mapping.** A tone mapping method saved as Hable, which was Kodi's default, becomes "Reference (BT.2390)" once. Hable chosen again afterwards stays.

### Details

**Dolby Vision picture and metadata**

- The PQ curve of the Dolby Vision decoding is computed without cancellation (`libplacebo-9942`): a profile 5 picture rebuilt as HDR10 had bands of up to 315 codes of 4095 at the edge of BT.2020, now 39 at most.
- The mapping to a display (SDR, projectors, Low-Latency) maps to the display's peak, not to a virtual peak above it that darkened the whole picture by 2 to 11 % (`libplacebo-9943`), and its gamut table is finer (96x64x512 instead of 48x32x256), which halves the largest error on SDR.
- Menus or subtitles over the black bars no longer mark a new scene in the metadata (ETSI GS CCM 001, 6.2.2): a change of the active area takes a new metadata id instead, and a bar stays in the area for 2 s of video after what was drawn over it is gone.
- A display of display management 2 or 3 is sent the content mapping v2.9 part of the metadata, which it reads, without the content mapping v4.0 blocks, which it does not.
- A picture that goes out without its enhancement picture (after a seek, or late) carries the RPU of its own access unit, not that of a neighbour.
- The enhancement layer starts by picture order at a random-access picture of its own where the two layers do not share one; with the base layer reordered, it never started before. A new VAAPI device context of the same display no longer starts the layer again, and a MEL stream whose layer turns out to carry a residual loses it once at most, then has its layer decoded.
- Dolby Vision shown as HDR10 draws into a second texture and shows a picture only when it is whole (132 MB more at 3840x2160); where nothing rebuilt can be shown, it shows the base layer when that is a picture by itself. Its 8-bit limited-range copy has white at 235, not 234.
- Kodi's video scaling method is not used by the Dolby Vision engine, which resamples as the specification says; the Dolby Vision page of the player information now says so.

**Performance**

The times in this section were measured through Direct3D 12 under Windows, not with the Linux drivers.

- A frame with GUI elements over native Dolby Vision is remade only in the 16x16-pixel tiles that the GUI reaches, with the same bytes: on an RTX 4080 SUPER, 1.4 to 2.7 ms instead of 1.5 to 9.3. `<video><dvdrawntiles>false</dvdrawntiles></video>` in `advancedsettings.xml` turns it off, to compare.
- What is drawn over the bars is measured in blocks, each pixel read once: on the integrated graphics of the Ryzen 9 7950X3D, 5.5 ms instead of 19.6 under a dimmed screen.
- Profile 5 frames with the GUI over them: the pixels drawn over are converted in a pass of their own, each pair once and with the picture under them decoded once, three conversions where there were four, with the same bytes: about 60 ms instead of about 83 under the player information on that integrated graphics. Where a GPU cannot run the pass, the transport converts those pixels itself as before (the same bytes, more GPU time) and `kodi.log` says so.
- Where those profile 5 frames still miss refreshes (with *Fidelity step-down* at Automatic), their conversion goes up one level at a time, only as far as needed: first a faster way with the same bytes, then a simplified rounding that can leave a pixel drawn over one code off, which the player information then shows. Frames without GUI elements, profiles 7 and 8.1, and graphics that keep up are not affected.
- The presentation a frame ahead is not switched on or off by a seek, or by menus in the first moment after they appear.
- What the fidelity step-down finds is now remembered also with the default transport (it keeps a level for each set of levels a format can have): a slow GPU misses far fewer refreshes at the start of a video.

**Display and HDMI**

- Dolby Vision Low-Latency (`videoscreen.hadesdvlowlatency`, off): only on a display that offers Low-Latency and not Standard. The picture is mapped with the BT.2390 curve, guided by each scene's level 1 metadata, to the peak and primaries of the display's Dolby Vision block, and sent as YCbCr 4:2:2 at 12 bits, BT.2020, limited range, through the tunnel, with the low-latency vendor InfoFrame (`linux-9975` to `9977` for amdgpu, i915 and xe; `nvidia-open-kernel-modules-9030`). `<video><dvllpattern>true</dvllpattern></video>` shows a test pattern instead of the picture, for a first look at a display.
- HDR10+: the vendor InfoFrame of each frame goes out in the commit of that frame, never with a change of mode, through a new DRM property (`linux-9980`) with backends for amdgpu, i915, xe and NVIDIA. Fewer than 12 pictures without metadata keep the last InfoFrame; at 12 it goes back to HDR10. The graphics overlay flag is set while Kodi's GUI is over the video.
- HDR10+ over DisplayPort on NVIDIA Ada (RTX 40): the InfoFrame SDP is written without the hardware's checksum, which belongs to HDMI InfoFrames and in an SDP would take the place of the version byte (`nvidia-open-kernel-modules-9020`). The official HDR10+ Gaming FAQ says that on a PC HDR10+ goes over HDMI only.
- HDR10+ over HDMI on NVIDIA to a display that declares HDMI variable refresh, where the driver fills the generic InfoFrame slot with its own variable refresh data (from 50 Hz and 720 lines, whether or not variable refresh is in use) and HDR10+ was refused: Ada now sends it in shared generic slot 2, a slot that NVIDIA documents only for DisplayPort; GTX 16, RTX 20 and RTX 30 graphics, which have no other slot, still send none. A refusal says why in the kernel log (`Hades: HDR10+ refused on head N: ...`), and `kodi.log` says that the driver refused it.
- HDR10+ over HDMI on Intel at 3840x2160 at 24, 25 and 30 Hz and 4096x2160 at 24 Hz (and their 1000/1001 rates), where DRM puts the HDMI VIC in the vendor InfoFrame that HDR10+ also needs, so that a film at 23.976 Hz went out as HDR10 (issue #2): to an HDMI 2.0 display that lists the CTA VIC of the mode and declares HDR10+, the AVI InfoFrame now carries that VIC and the vendor InfoFrame carries HDR10+ (`linux-9982`). Everything else goes out as before, and a refusal says why in the kernel log.
- HDR10+ as Dolby Vision (`videoscreen.hadesdvfromhdr10plus`, off): profile 8.1 metadata made scene by scene from the HDR10+ metadata, with the scenes as hdr10plus_tool tells them and the defaults of dovi_tool; the average and the peak of each scene are taken by rules of Hades' own ([how it works](docs/how-it-works.md#other-formats-sent-as-dolby-vision)).
- HDR10 as Dolby Vision, measured (`videoscreen.hadesdvfromhdr10`, off): level 1 and the active area measured on the GPU from the pictures that Kodi has decoded ahead, and scene cuts found from the picture and marked as such (a few are missed, and a few are found where there is none); no trims and no level 3.
- Trims left out (`videoscreen.hadesdvnotrims`, off): no level 2, 8 or 10 blocks are sent; the picture does not change.
- Dolby Vision TVs whose 12-byte version 1 block says display management 2 or 3 (LG of 2017 and 2018, TCL, Toshiba, Vestel, Vizio, Hisense, Roku, AV receivers) got HDR10, and now get native Dolby Vision (`linux-9978`, and Kodi's rule).
- AMD: native Dolby Vision on DCN 4.0.1 (Radeon RX 9000), refused where the display core would apply anything to the stream on its own (`linux-9973`, `linux-9974`).
- AMD: HDR in YCbCr 4:2:2 at 12 bits where RGB would be 8 bits, on DCN except DCN 3.1 and 3.1.5, which keep 4:2:0 (`linux-9972`).
- AMD: the RGB range on HDMI as CTA-861 says, AMD's change by Adrian Betschart, not yet in a Linux release (`linux-9970`), kept full on a TV that selects the range (`linux-9971`); and the 4K modes whose VIC goes in the HDMI vendor InfoFrame (3840x2160 at 24, 25 and 30 Hz, 4096x2160 at 24 Hz) count as CTA modes too, which AMD's change leaves out (`linux-9983`).
- Intel display version 9 (UHD 6xx): native Dolby Vision no longer needs a connector property that i915 gives only from version 10; on such an HDMI port of 300 MHz it goes out at 2160p24, 25 and 30, and a video at 50 or 60 Hz says that the output has no mode at that rate.
- NVIDIA: the vendor InfoFrame and the HDMI properties on a dual-mode DisplayPort connector while it drives the display as HDMI (`nvidia-open-kernel-modules-9010`, `9011`); HDR at 12 bpc over HDMI on Turing where the mode fits, off by default, with `nvidia-modeset.hades_turing_12bpc=1` on the kernel line (`9012`); and the depth the driver drives the output at, now shown in the player information (`9013`).
- NVIDIA: a repeated picture is drawn again on every refresh, as in 1.0 RC1, since nvidia-drm has no vertical blank to wait for by default; `<video><dvkeepbycommit>true</dvkeepbycommit></video>` (off by default) keeps it another way. An output whose page flips stop coming is turned off and on again after 1, 2, 4 and up to 64 s for as long as they do not come, with its state read from the kernel.
- A native Dolby Vision file opened over another at another frame rate goes straight into its mode: one change of the display where there were up to seven, and none for the next file of a list at the same rate. While a change of mode waits for the video, the menus go out in the mode on the screen instead of freezing.
- The audio engine is told of every modeset of native Dolby Vision; a change of mode that the driver refuses is not taken as made; the HDR state follows what the kernel took; and after a wake from suspend the mode is set again where nvidia-drm left the output off.
- The notice for a display that offers only Dolby Vision Low-Latency is now one sentence that says what to do, shown for the usual time of a notification; the reason is in `kodi.log` and on the Dolby Vision page of the player information.
- Add-ons are told that the display takes Dolby Vision only where native output can be had with the settings as they are, and HDR10+ only where it can go out.
- *System > Display > Monitor* shows each display by the name in its EDID, with its connector, and with its graphics where there are two ("LG TV SSCR2 (HDMI-A-2, NVIDIA)"); a choice saved before keeps working. How a change is made, confirmed and undone is in [the settings](docs/settings.md#kodis-own-settings-that-matter-here).
- 3D: where the display has no 3D mode at the rate of a 30p or 25p video, the 3D mode at exactly twice that rate is used where the display has it, each picture shown twice.

**Files and streams**

- Profile 7 in two video tracks of MP4 files (the dual-track carriage of Dolby's specification) and of Matroska files, also those that libavformat does not group, once their packets show the pair; in a transport stream, also when its enhancement layer is a private stream found to be HEVC while it plays. The log says why two tracks are not joined.
- A HEVC stream whose packets carry an RPU without a Dolby Vision record in the container plays as Dolby Vision (profile 7 with both layers, profile 5, or profile 8 over PQ, HLG or SDR).
- At a play item of a Blu-ray that is not connected seamlessly, about 9 seconds of video were lost; now 1 picture (`ffmpeg-9928`).
- Blu-ray 3D: three fixes to the decoder of both views, for faults that a review of FFmpeg pull request 24303 pointed to.

**Audio**

- PCM goes out in no more channels than the display declares in its EDID (*System > Audio > Limit PCM channels to what the display declares*, Expert, on), also through a change of display mode, which takes the audio data of the display away for a moment.
- Where the display takes only stereo PCM but Dolby in more channels, a multichannel track that Kodi decodes goes out as Dolby Digital Plus 5.1 where the display declares it and the output takes it (192 kHz, as Kodi's own transcoding asks), else as Dolby Digital 5.1, passed through, instead of mixed down to stereo (*Surround as Dolby when the display takes only stereo PCM*, on). Where no encoder opens, stereo PCM as before.
- Kodi's own *Enable Dolby Digital transcoding* counts only where *Number of channels* is 2.0, as in Kodi: left on from a 2.0 setting and hidden at 5.1 or 7.1, it no longer encodes to Dolby there.
- An HDMI or DisplayPort audio output that is set but not there is replaced in memory by the one Kodi would take for it, before its formats are asked, so passthrough works; the setting is not changed, and is used again when that output comes back. What the sink opened is in the log.

- A "GPU" row on the overview page, with the use and temperature that the driver gives; rows on the Dolby Vision page for the video scaling method and for the content mapping sent.
- Every skin gets Hades' overview and Dolby Vision pages: where the skin has no such pages, a second press of the key that opens the player process information opens them.

**Interface**

- The mouse pointer keeps the fraction of a pixel of each motion that libinput gives, which Kodi dropped (1000 reports of 0.4 px moved it 0 px; now 400). Of motions that follow one another in the queue only the last is handled, with buttons, the wheel and keys kept in their place and order, and a drag moves as far as before.

The wait cannot be cancelled, as before.
- Once a minute, when the interface was slow, a line `GUI: in the last N s, ...` in `kodi.log` says how many passes took 50 ms or more with no video playing, how long add-ons held the interface, and how many mouse motions were left for the one after them.

#### Antes de actualizar

Si activaste el ajuste de Kodi de rango de color limitado (16-235), en *Sistema > Pantalla*, porque en esa tele los negros salían aplastados, desactívalo, o el rango se comprime dos veces. Una tele que elige el rango, como la LG C5, recibe lo mismo que antes.
- **Mapeo de tonos.** Un método de mapeo de tonos guardado como Hable, que era el de Kodi por defecto, pasa una vez a "Referencia (BT.2390)". Si después vuelves a elegir Hable, se queda.

#### Detalles

**Imagen y metadatos Dolby Vision**

- La curva PQ de la decodificación Dolby Vision se calcula sin cancelación (`libplacebo-9942`): una imagen de perfil 5 reconstruida como HDR10 tenía bandas de hasta 315 códigos de 4095 en el borde de BT.2020, ahora 39 como mucho.
- La adaptación a una pantalla (SDR, proyectores, Low-Latency) va al pico de la pantalla, no a un pico virtual por encima que oscurecía toda la imagen entre un 2 y un 11 % (`libplacebo-9943`), y su tabla de gama es más fina (96x64x512 en vez de 48x32x256), lo que reduce a la mitad el error más grande en SDR.
- Los menús o subtítulos sobre las bandas negras ya no marcan una escena nueva en los metadatos (ETSI GS CCM 001, 6.2.2): un cambio de la zona activa lleva en su lugar un identificador de metadatos nuevo, y una banda sigue dentro de la zona 2 s de vídeo después de que se vaya lo dibujado encima.
- A una pantalla de gestión de pantalla 2 o 3 se le envía la parte de mapeo de contenido v2.9 de los metadatos, la que lee, sin los bloques de mapeo de contenido v4.0, que no lee.
- Una imagen que sale sin su imagen de mejora (tras un salto, o tarde) lleva el RPU de su propia unidad de acceso, no el de una vecina.
- La capa de mejora arranca por orden de imagen en un punto de acceso aleatorio propio donde las dos capas no comparten ninguno; con la capa base reordenada, antes no arrancaba nunca. Un contexto VAAPI nuevo de la misma pantalla ya no la reinicia, y un flujo MEL cuya capa resulta llevar un residuo lo pierde una vez como mucho, y desde ahí su capa se decodifica.
- Dolby Vision mostrado como HDR10 dibuja en una segunda textura y muestra una imagen solo cuando está entera (132 MB más a 3840x2160); donde no se puede mostrar nada reconstruido, muestra la capa base cuando es una imagen por sí misma. Su copia de 8 bits en rango limitado tiene el blanco en 235, no en 234.
- El método de escalado de vídeo de Kodi no lo usa el motor Dolby Vision, que remuestrea como dice la especificación; la página Dolby Vision de la información del reproductor ahora lo dice.

**Rendimiento**

Los tiempos de esta sección se midieron con Direct3D 12 en Windows, no con los controladores de Linux.

- Un fotograma con elementos de la interfaz sobre Dolby Vision nativo se rehace solo en las teselas de 16x16 píxeles a las que llega la interfaz, con los mismos bytes: en una RTX 4080 SUPER, de 1,4 a 2,7 ms en vez de 1,5 a 9,3. `<video><dvdrawntiles>false</dvdrawntiles></video>` en `advancedsettings.xml` lo apaga, para comparar.
- Lo dibujado sobre las bandas se mide por bloques, leyendo cada píxel una vez: en la gráfica integrada del Ryzen 9 7950X3D, 5,5 ms en vez de 19,6 bajo una pantalla atenuada.
- Fotogramas de perfil 5 con la interfaz encima: los píxeles dibujados encima se convierten en una pasada propia, cada par una vez y con la imagen de debajo decodificada una vez, tres conversiones donde había cuatro, con los mismos bytes: unos 60 ms en vez de unos 83 bajo la información del reproductor en esa gráfica integrada. Donde una GPU no puede hacer la pasada, el transporte convierte él mismo esos píxeles como antes (los mismos bytes, más tiempo de GPU) y `kodi.log` lo dice.
- Donde esos fotogramas de perfil 5 siguen perdiendo refrescos (con *Reducción de fidelidad* en Automático), su conversión sube un nivel cada vez, solo lo necesario: primero una forma más rápida con los mismos bytes, y después un redondeo simplificado que puede dejar un píxel dibujado encima a un código del valor exacto, cosa que entonces muestra la información del reproductor. No cambian los fotogramas sin elementos de la interfaz, los perfiles 7 y 8.1, ni las gráficas que llegan a tiempo.
- La presentación un fotograma por adelantado no la encienden ni la apagan un salto ni unos menús que acaban de aparecer.
- Lo que encuentra la reducción de fidelidad se recuerda ahora también con el transporte por defecto (guarda un nivel para cada juego de niveles que puede tener un formato): una GPU lenta pierde muchos menos refrescos al empezar un vídeo.

**Pantalla y HDMI**

- Dolby Vision Low-Latency (`videoscreen.hadesdvlowlatency`, apagado): solo en una pantalla que ofrece Low-Latency y no Standard. La imagen se adapta con la curva BT.2390, guiada por los metadatos de nivel 1 de cada escena, al pico y a los primarios del bloque Dolby Vision de la pantalla, y se envía como YCbCr 4:2:2 a 12 bits, BT.2020, rango limitado, por el túnel, con el InfoFrame del fabricante de baja latencia (`linux-9975` a `9977` para amdgpu, i915 y xe; `nvidia-open-kernel-modules-9030`). `<video><dvllpattern>true</dvllpattern></video>` muestra un patrón de prueba en lugar de la imagen, para un primer vistazo a una pantalla.
- HDR10+: el InfoFrame del fabricante de cada fotograma sale en el commit de ese fotograma, nunca con un cambio de modo, por una propiedad nueva de DRM (`linux-9980`) con implementaciones para amdgpu, i915, xe y NVIDIA. Menos de 12 imágenes sin metadatos conservan el último InfoFrame; a las 12 vuelve a HDR10. El indicador de gráficos sobre el vídeo se pone mientras la interfaz de Kodi está encima.
- HDR10+ por DisplayPort en NVIDIA Ada (RTX 40): el SDP del InfoFrame se escribe sin la suma de comprobación del hardware, que es la de los InfoFrames HDMI y en un SDP ocuparía el lugar del byte de versión (`nvidia-open-kernel-modules-9020`). La FAQ oficial de HDR10+ Gaming dice que en un PC HDR10+ va solo por HDMI.
- HDR10+ por HDMI en NVIDIA a una pantalla que declara refresco variable por HDMI, donde el controlador ocupa la ranura genérica de InfoFrame con sus propios datos de refresco variable (desde 50 Hz y 720 líneas, se use o no el refresco variable) y HDR10+ se rechazaba: Ada lo envía ahora en la ranura genérica compartida 2, una ranura que NVIDIA solo documenta para DisplayPort; las GTX 16, RTX 20 y RTX 30, que no tienen otra ranura, siguen sin enviarlo. Un rechazo dice por qué en el registro del kernel (`Hades: HDR10+ refused on head N: ...`), y `kodi.log` dice que lo rechazó el controlador.
- HDR10+ por HDMI en Intel a 3840x2160 a 24, 25 y 30 Hz y 4096x2160 a 24 Hz (y sus frecuencias de 1000/1001), donde DRM pone el VIC de HDMI en el InfoFrame del fabricante que HDR10+ también necesita, así que una película a 23,976 Hz salía como HDR10 (issue #2): a una pantalla HDMI 2.0 que lista el VIC CTA del modo y declara HDR10+, el InfoFrame AVI lleva ahora ese VIC y el InfoFrame del fabricante lleva HDR10+ (`linux-9982`). Todo lo demás sale como antes, y un rechazo dice por qué en el registro del kernel.
- HDR10+ como Dolby Vision (`videoscreen.hadesdvfromhdr10plus`, apagado): metadatos de perfil 8.1 hechos escena a escena a partir de los metadatos HDR10+, con las escenas como las cuenta hdr10plus_tool y los valores por defecto de dovi_tool; la media y el pico de cada escena se toman con reglas propias de Hades ([cómo funciona](docs/how-it-works.md#other-formats-sent-as-dolby-vision), en inglés).
- HDR10 como Dolby Vision, medido (`videoscreen.hadesdvfromhdr10`, apagado): el nivel 1 y la zona activa medidos en la GPU sobre las imágenes que Kodi ya ha decodificado por delante, y los cortes de escena encontrados en la imagen y marcados como tales (se pierden unos pocos, y otros pocos se encuentran donde no los hay); sin trims ni nivel 3.
- Sin trims (`videoscreen.hadesdvnotrims`, apagado): no se envían bloques de nivel 2, 8 ni 10; la imagen no cambia.
- Teles Dolby Vision cuyo bloque de versión 1 de 12 bytes dice gestión de pantalla 2 o 3 (LG de 2017 y 2018, TCL, Toshiba, Vestel, Vizio, Hisense, Roku, receptores AV) recibían HDR10, y ahora reciben Dolby Vision nativo (`linux-9978`, y la regla de Kodi).
- AMD: Dolby Vision nativo en DCN 4.0.1 (Radeon RX 9000), rechazado donde el núcleo de pantalla aplicaría algo al flujo por su cuenta (`linux-9973`, `linux-9974`).
- AMD: HDR en YCbCr 4:2:2 a 12 bits donde RGB iría a 8 bits, en DCN salvo DCN 3.1 y 3.1.5, que siguen en 4:2:0 (`linux-9972`).
- AMD: el rango RGB por HDMI como dice CTA-861, el cambio de AMD de Adrian Betschart, que aún no está en ninguna versión de Linux (`linux-9970`), con rango completo en una tele que elige el rango (`linux-9971`); y los modos 4K cuyo VIC va en el InfoFrame del fabricante de HDMI (3840x2160 a 24, 25 y 30 Hz, 4096x2160 a 24 Hz) cuentan también como modos CTA, que el cambio de AMD deja fuera (`linux-9983`).
- Intel de versión de pantalla 9 (UHD 6xx): el Dolby Vision nativo ya no necesita una propiedad del conector que i915 solo da desde la versión 10; en un puerto HDMI así de 300 MHz sale a 2160p24, 25 y 30, y un vídeo a 50 o 60 Hz dice que la salida no tiene modo a esa frecuencia.
- NVIDIA: el InfoFrame del fabricante y las propiedades HDMI en un conector DisplayPort de doble modo mientras maneja la pantalla como HDMI (`nvidia-open-kernel-modules-9010`, `9011`); HDR a 12 bpc por HDMI en Turing donde el modo cabe, apagado por defecto, con `nvidia-modeset.hades_turing_12bpc=1` en la línea del kernel (`9012`); y la profundidad a la que el controlador maneja la salida, que ahora muestra la información del reproductor (`9013`).
- NVIDIA: una imagen repetida se vuelve a dibujar en cada refresco, como en 1.0 RC1, porque nvidia-drm no tiene por defecto borrado vertical que esperar; `<video><dvkeepbycommit>true</dvkeepbycommit></video>` (apagado por defecto) la conserva de otra forma. Una salida cuyos cambios de página dejan de llegar se apaga y se enciende tras 1, 2, 4 y hasta 64 s mientras no lleguen, con su estado leído del kernel.
- Un archivo de Dolby Vision nativo abierto sobre otro a otra frecuencia pasa directamente a su modo: un cambio de pantalla donde había hasta siete, y ninguno para el archivo siguiente de una lista a la misma frecuencia. Mientras un cambio de modo espera al vídeo, los menús salen en el modo que hay en pantalla en vez de quedarse quietos.
- Al motor de audio se le avisa de cada cambio de modo del Dolby Vision nativo; un cambio de modo que el controlador rechaza no se da por hecho; el estado HDR sigue a lo que tomó el kernel; y al despertar de la suspensión el modo se vuelve a poner donde nvidia-drm dejó la salida apagada.
- El aviso para una pantalla que solo ofrece Dolby Vision Low-Latency es ahora de una frase que dice qué hacer, y se muestra el tiempo habitual de un aviso; el motivo está en `kodi.log` y en la página Dolby Vision de la información del reproductor.
- A los complementos se les dice que la pantalla admite Dolby Vision solo donde se puede tener la salida nativa con los ajustes tal como están, y HDR10+ solo donde puede salir.
- El ajuste Monitor de *Sistema > Pantalla* muestra cada pantalla por el nombre de su EDID, con su conector, y con su gráfica donde hay dos ("LG TV SSCR2 (HDMI-A-2, NVIDIA)"); una elección guardada antes sigue valiendo. Cómo se hace, se confirma y se deshace un cambio está en [los ajustes](docs/settings.md#kodis-own-settings-that-matter-here), en inglés.
- 3D: donde la pantalla no tiene modo 3D a la frecuencia de un vídeo de 30p o 25p, se usa el modo 3D a exactamente el doble donde la pantalla lo tiene, y cada imagen se muestra dos veces.

**Archivos y flujos**

- El perfil 7 en dos pistas de vídeo de archivos MP4 (el transporte en dos pistas de la especificación de Dolby) y de archivos Matroska, también los que libavformat no agrupa, en cuanto sus paquetes muestran la pareja; en un flujo de transporte, también cuando su capa de mejora es un flujo privado que resulta ser HEVC mientras se reproduce. **Audio**

- El PCM sale en no más canales de los que la pantalla declara en su EDID (*Sistema > Audio > Limitar los canales PCM a lo que declara la pantalla*, Experto, activado), también durante un cambio de modo de pantalla, que se lleva un momento los datos de audio de la pantalla.

Donde no abre ningún codificador, PCM estéreo como antes.

## 1.0 RC2

### What's new for you

- **Native Dolby Vision rebuilt as the specification defines it.** The picture sent to the TV is now rebuilt exactly as the published Dolby Vision specification describes it, and sent the way certified players send it. Colours, edges and bright highlights are more faithful in every profile (5, 7 FEL, 7 MEL and 8.1).
- **Smoother on modest graphics.** Less GPU work on every frame, and a repeated picture is not drawn again. Only on graphics that cannot keep up does the picture lose a small, measured amount of precision, and only as much as needed; graphics that keep up stay bit-exact.
- **Menus at 120 Hz.** Starting and stopping Dolby Vision, HDR10 and HLG is a single display change on NVIDIA, AMD and Intel, also with the menus at 119.88 or 120 Hz.
- **Turn the TV on after the PC.** The TV's EDID is read again when each video starts, so Dolby Vision works even if the PC started with the TV off, and a later change (Deep Colour turned on, another TV) is seen.
- **New "Overview" page** in the player information: what is sent to the TV (signal, mode, colour depth, the metadata sent), the video, the audio and the system.
- **Projectors and TVs without Dolby Vision:** Dolby Vision shown as HDR10 can be mapped to the brightness of the display (off by default), and the Dolby Vision output can be chosen for each video.
- **Older AMD graphics:** native Dolby Vision also on Raven, Picasso, Navi 1x, Renoir and Cezanne (DCN 1.0 and 2.x).
- **HDR10 at 10 bits** on Intel and AMD where RGB would be 8 bits, in YCbCr 4:2:0, with the BT.2020 YCbCr matrix on Intel.
- **Live TV:** audio passthrough (Atmos included) on live channels and streams (a setting, on by default), a picture on 10-bit channels that showed none, and faster channel changes.
- **Profile 7 MEL** no longer decodes its empty enhancement layer: less work, the same picture, byte for byte. A FEL is always decoded.
- **Up to date and safer:** Linux 7.2.11, Kodi and LibreELEC at their latest commits, and fixes for known security issues in glibc, FFmpeg, curl, libnfs, libass and more.

### Details

**Dolby Vision picture**

- The reconstruction follows the normative composer of ETSI GS CCM 001: identical bit for bit to an integer reference of the specification in every case tested. libplacebo's reshaping, which departs from the specification by 4 to 15 codes on most pixels (and by hundreds at strong colour edges), stays available as an expert setting.
- Sent the way a certified source device sends it: the composer's output in the colour representation of its RPU (IPT for profile 5), with the RPU's own matrices in the display management metadata; menus and subtitles are blended into it, and pixels they do not touch are sent exactly as composed. An expert setting sends it converted to BT.2020, as 1.0 RC1 did.
- Chroma of both layers at the position the stream signals; 4:2:2 taken from the even pixel; exact Lanczos weights for whole-ratio scaling; very bright pixels that came out black are fixed; exact BT.2020, P3 and BT.709 matrices.
- Level 5 describes what is sent, also when menus or subtitles are drawn over the bars; metadata up to 512 bytes; level 11 checked as dovi_tool checks it; scene refresh after a gap.

**Performance**

- Automatic fidelity step-down only where frames are missed (measured per format, remembered per GPU and driver); it can be turned off.
- Repeated pictures are not drawn again; shader cache on disk; GPU programs prepared before the first frame; fewer imports per frame.
- A switch to check on the GPU that frames send the bytes they claim (`<dvverify>` in `advancedsettings.xml`).

**Display and settings**

- A frame whose page flip never comes no longer stalls playback.
- Where the graphics driver refuses native Dolby Vision (Intel and AMD), `kodi.log` and the Dolby Vision page of the player information give its reason.
- Dolby Vision engine settings, Automatic by default.
- DRM PRIME hands back what it cannot decode.

**Updates and security**

- Linux 7.2.11, libplacebo 804dc71cd38d, Kodi Piers up to 01d4146d5d25, LibreELEC master up to 3ca0d49bee40.
- Fixes taken from upstream: glibc 2.44 (nine CVEs), FFmpeg (about ninety), libnfs (CVE-2026-108985), libass (CVE-2026-95127), curl, libbluray, libdvdread, libdvdnav, libarchive, Samba, BusyBox; BlueZ CVE-2026-105460 fixed by Hades.

What has been seen on a TV is in the [README](README.md), under "What has been tested". Some ideas came from other Dolby Vision builds and were written again for Hades; the README credits them.
